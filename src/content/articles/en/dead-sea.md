Drop a raw egg into a glass of tap water and it sinks. Stir in plenty of salt, try again, and the egg rises and floats. That simple science-class trick explains the most famous thing about the **Dead Sea**, the lowest point on land anywhere on Earth. Visitors lie back on its surface and bob there as if on an air mattress, and some even read a newspaper while floating far from the shore.

## Where is the Dead Sea?

The Dead Sea sits in the Jordan Rift Valley, with Jordan on its eastern side and the West Bank and Israel to the west. In Arabic it also goes by Bahr Lut, the Sea of Lot. From Amman, the Jordanian capital, it's less than an hour's drive. Its shore lies more than 430 metres below sea level, and that figure grows every year because the water keeps dropping, which we'll come back to. The northern basin is about 50 kilometres long and reaches a depth of about 300 metres.

For all its name, it isn't really a sea. Scientists class it as a **salt lake**: a closed body of water with no connection to any sea or ocean. People have called it a sea since ancient times, and the name stuck.

## Why is it so salty?

Picture a bathtub with the tap running and the plug firmly in. Water comes in from the Jordan River and from a few valleys and springs, but there's no outlet, so the only way out is up, through evaporation. In the heat and dry air of the valley, evaporation is intense. Water leaves; salt stays. Over time that salt has built up until the Dead Sea is about **34% salt**, against roughly 3.5% for the oceans, which makes it around ten times saltier than ocean water.

## Why can't you sink in the Dead Sea?

Back to the egg. It all comes down to **density**. With so much salt dissolved in it, Dead Sea water weighs about 1.24 grams per cubic centimetre, clearly more than the human body, whose density is close to that of fresh water.

Archimedes' principle, which students meet in physics lessons, does the rest. Water pushes up on anything in it with a force equal to the weight of the water that object pushes aside. Because Dead Sea water is so heavy, only a small part of your body has to be underwater to displace your whole weight's worth of it. So you float with almost no effort, and can end up sitting on the water as though it were a chair.

> **Did you know?** Swimming normally on your stomach is very hard in the Dead Sea, because the water keeps lifting your legs. The right way is to lie on your back and relax.

Easy floating doesn't make the water harmless, though. You can still drown: swallowing or inhaling water this salty is very dangerous, so treat it with respect and keep a close eye on children the whole time.

## Why is it called "dead"?

Fish and water plants can't survive salt at this level, so none live there. It isn't completely lifeless, however. Some microorganisms that tolerate extremely salty conditions do make it their home.

## Mud, minerals and Dead Sea treatments

The water and mud are rich in minerals such as magnesium, calcium, potassium and bromine, which is why Dead Sea products are so common in skincare. Many people also come for relief from some skin conditions such as psoriasis. The reason is linked to the altitude, or rather the lack of it: because the area is so far below sea level, there's an extra layer of air overhead, and that filters out part of the harmful ultraviolet radiation. Any treatment should be supervised by specialists.

The minerals matter to industry too. Major plants on its shores extract potash and bromine.

## The Dead Sea is shrinking

The water level is falling by about one metre a year; some studies put it at 0.7 to 1 metre. Since the 1960s the lake has lost a large part of its surface area.

Two causes stand out. Water from the Jordan River and its tributaries is diverted for drinking and farming, so very little now reaches the lake. And the mineral industries use evaporation ponds. Remember that the Dead Sea only loses water by evaporation, and the result makes sense: less water arriving, the same fierce evaporation, and a level that keeps dropping.

One visible consequence is the thousands of sinkholes that have opened up near the shores. Several rescue plans have been proposed, including bringing water from the Red Sea, but no comprehensive solution has been carried out so far.

## Tips for a safe swim in the Dead Sea

Concentrated salt stings eyes and cuts, so a dip here needs a bit more care than a normal swim:

1. Don't put your head under, and don't jump in.
2. Protect your eyes. If water gets in them, rinse straight away with fresh water.
3. Don't shave or wax the day before, as the salt stings tiny cuts.
4. Keep it short: 15 to 20 minutes at a time is plenty.
5. Drink lots of water, since the air is hot and dry.
6. Wear water shoes, because salt crystals on the bottom can be sharp.
7. Shower with fresh water when you get out.

Spring and autumn are the best times to go. Summer is extremely hot, with temperatures that can pass 40 °C.

## Nearby places worth visiting in Jordan

A trip to the Dead Sea can easily take in a few other places. Al-Maghtas, the Baptism Site, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Wadi Mujib is a nature reserve with a spectacular canyon that draws adventure lovers. And Ma'in Hot Springs has thermal springs and hot waterfalls.

## From the lowest point on land to the highest

The shore of the Dead Sea, more than 430 metres below sea level, is the lowest point on land. The highest is the summit of Mount Everest.
