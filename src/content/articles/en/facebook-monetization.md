A lot of creators hit the same wall: thousands of followers, regular posts, and still no sign of a payout option. Most of the confusion around **Facebook monetization requirements in 2026** comes from one change. Meta folded its older creator programs into a single one, but the old thresholds kept circulating online as if nothing had happened. Below is how getting paid on Facebook works now, which tools exist, how to tell if you qualify, how payouts reach you, and what is different from a few years ago.

> Facebook's rules change from time to time and differ by country. The final word is always the **Monetization** section of your Professional Dashboard.

## How Facebook monetization has changed

Meta used to pay creators through separate programs, mainly in-stream ads, ads on Reels and the Performance bonus. Each came with its own targets, such as follower counts and minutes viewed, so creators had a fairly clear number to chase.

In October 2024 Meta announced **Facebook Content Monetization**, one program that combines those three, and said it would replace them. Industry sources report that the old programs were later shut down. That is why figures like "10,000 followers and 600,000 minutes viewed" are misleading today: they belong to the old program and are no longer the main route to earning.

## Facebook monetization requirements in 2026

### The basics that apply to every tool

Before any specific program comes into play, a few conditions apply almost everywhere. You need to be 18 or older and live in a country that is eligible for the tool you want, and that list differs from tool to tool. You have to follow Meta's Partner Monetization Policies and Content Monetization Policies as well as the Community Standards. You post from a Page or from a profile in professional mode. And your content has to be original, made by you rather than copied or reposted from other pages.

That last condition trips up more pages than any other. Adding music or a caption to someone else's clip does not make it yours as far as Meta is concerned.

### The Content Monetization program

According to Meta's announcement and industry sources as of 2026, the program is **invite-only**. Meta picks the creators it considers eligible, and no published follower count guarantees a place. There is an interest form in the Monetization section of the Professional Dashboard, and filling it in is worth doing, but it does not guarantee an invitation.

Once you are in, pay depends on how your public posts perform: Reels, longer videos, photos and text posts all count. Meta does not publish a fixed rate per 1,000 views, and earnings vary widely by audience, country and content type. Two pages of the same size can earn very different amounts simply because their viewers live in different places.

### Facebook Reels monetization

Reels no longer have a program of their own. Facebook Reels monetization now sits inside Content Monetization, so the requirements are identical: an invitation, original content and staying within the policies. Stay away from clips cut out of films or other channels, which are among the most common reasons pages lose monetization. A short video you filmed yourself, in your own kitchen or a local market, does more for a page than a stack of borrowed clips, however many views those clips pull in.

## Other ways to earn on Facebook

Content Monetization is not the only door. Facebook has other tools, each built around a different idea:

| Tool | How it works | What's reported about requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stars | Fans buy Stars and send them during lives or on Reels and posts | Industry sources cite about 500 followers for 30 consecutive days, varying by country |
| Subscriptions | Fans pay monthly for exclusive content or perks | Higher thresholds are reported, such as about 10,000 followers or a set number of return viewers |
| Branded content | You partner with a brand and post clearly labelled paid content | You must use the paid partnership tool and disclose the ad |

For a small page, Stars is the realistic starting point, since its reported bar is far lower than for subscriptions. Some income does not depend on Facebook's programs at all: **affiliate marketing** and selling your own services or products through your page keep working regardless of what Meta decides. ## How to turn on Facebook monetization, step by step

The order matters here, because each step opens the way to the next one.

1. Switch to a Page, or turn on professional mode for your profile.
2. Open the Professional Dashboard or Meta Business Suite.
3. Go to Monetization to see which tools are available to you and your eligibility status.
4. If the Content Monetization interest form appears, sign up, and turn on any tools that are available, such as Stars.
5. Add your payout and tax details when prompted.
6. Post original content people engage with on a regular schedule, and track how it does in Insights.

The sixth step never really ends. Insights shows which posts your audience actually responds to, and that tells you what to make more of.

## How to withdraw Facebook earnings

You add a payout method under payout settings in the monetization tools. What is offered depends on your country: bank transfer is the most common, and PayPal is available in some places. Payouts usually arrive monthly once your balance passes your account's **payout threshold**, which you can see in those same settings.

One small detail causes a lot of trouble. The name on your bank account has to match the name in your payout details, and a mismatch, even in spelling or word order, is one of the most common reasons payments are delayed.

## Why Facebook monetization gets rejected or paused

When a page is turned down or its earnings stop, the cause is usually one of these:

1. Unoriginal content: reposting other people's clips, even with small edits.
2. Community Standards violations or misleading information.
3. Fake engagement: buying followers or likes, or engagement bait such as "type YES".
4. Living in a country that isn't eligible for the tool you want.
5. A very new page, or irregular activity.

Be suspicious of anyone who offers to "activate monetization" for a fee or asks for your password. That is one of the most common scams, and nobody outside Meta can switch monetization on for you. ## Questions creators ask about getting paid on Facebook

### How many followers do you need to get paid on Facebook in 2026?

Content Monetization is invite-only, so there is no official follower number that guarantees entry. For Stars, industry sources cite about 500 followers, varying by country.

### How much does Facebook pay per 1,000 views?

Meta doesn't publish a fixed rate. Earnings vary widely with the audience's country, the content type and watch time, so treat the inflated figures on social media with caution.

### Can I monetize a profile without a Page?

Yes, in countries and for tools that support it, as long as professional mode is turned on.

### Why can't I see the Monetization section?

Usually because the account isn't in professional mode, the country isn't eligible, or the account doesn't meet the general requirements yet. If that is the wall you are facing, work through it in order: turn on professional mode, check that everything you post is your own, register interest in the Professional Dashboard, and earn from Stars and affiliate links in the meantime.
