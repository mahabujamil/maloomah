A student holds the door for a classmate whose arms are full of books. Another says sorry after bumping into someone in the line. A third thanks the cleaner in the corridor. Nobody claps for any of this, yet these small moments decide whether a school day feels warm or heavy. That is the idea behind a **school assembly on good manners**: a reminder, first thing in the morning, that character shows in small things long before it shows in big ones. The script below is written out in full, so a teacher can hand the parts to students as they are or trim them for younger classes.

## Running order and timing

The whole assembly takes about ten minutes. Spreading the parts across several students helps, because each of them then feels the assembly belongs to them.

| Order | Segment | Approximate time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Opening | 1 minute |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 minutes |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 minute |
| 4 | Morning talk | 2 minutes |
| 5 | Did you know? | 1 minute |
| 6 | Short story | 2 minutes |
| 7 | Quick quiz | 1 minute |
| 8 | Closing | 30 seconds |

For the youngest grades, keep only the first paragraph of the morning talk, one verse and one hadith. A six-year-old remembers one short sentence they understand far better than a page they don't.

## Opening words for a school assembly on good manners

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
> Good morning, everyone, and welcome to a morning of courtesy and respect.
> Dear principal, dear teachers, dear classmates: may your day be full of good things.
> People say a kind word opens hearts, and a smile costs nothing but is worth a great deal. Today our assembly is about good manners, the qualities that make a person welcome wherever they go: honesty, trustworthiness, humility, mercy, respect for those older than us and kindness to those younger.
> We begin, as always, with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by …

## Quran recitation

These verses were chosen because together they paint a clear picture of good character: God's praise for the Prophet's character, Luqman's advice to his son about humility and a gentle voice, and the value of gentleness with people. The Arabic text is taken exactly from quran.com, and the English meanings follow the Saheeh International translation. The student should recite from the Mushaf.

> ﴿وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ﴾
> "And indeed, you are of a great moral character." [Surah Al-Qalam, 68:4]

> ﴿وَلَا تُصَعِّرْ خَدَّكَ لِلنَّاسِ وَلَا تَمْشِ فِي الْأَرْضِ مَرَحًا ۖ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ كُلَّ مُخْتَالٍ فَخُورٍ ۝ وَاقْصِدْ فِي مَشْيِكَ وَاغْضُضْ مِن صَوْتِكَ ۚ إِنَّ أَنكَرَ الْأَصْوَاتِ لَصَوْتُ الْحَمِيرِ﴾
> "And do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful. And be moderate in your pace and lower your voice; indeed, the most disagreeable of sounds is the voice of donkeys." [Surah Luqman, 31:18–19]

> ﴿فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ ۖ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ الْقَلْبِ لَانفَضُّوا مِنْ حَوْلِكَ﴾
> "So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you." [Surah Al Imran, part of 3:159]

## Hadith on good character

Both of these hadiths are authentic. The first is in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, the second in Sahih Muslim.

> Abdullah ibn Amr reported that the Prophet ﷺ was never obscene or coarse, and he used to say: "The best of you are those with the best character." [Al-Bukhari 3559, Muslim 2321]

> An-Nawwas ibn Sam'an said: I asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about righteousness and sin, and he said: "Righteousness is good character, and sin is what wavers in your heart and you would hate for people to find out about." [Muslim 2553]

A student can explain them in two sentences. The first makes character, not money or looks, the measure of who is best. The second gives us a test we can carry every day: if we would be embarrassed for people to see what we are doing, it is worth stopping to think again.

## Morning talk

> Dear classmates,
> Intelligence can get you through an exam, but only character makes people glad you are around. Years from now, nobody will remember your maths mark. They will remember how you treated them.
>
> Good manners are not lines we learn for the assembly and forget at break time. They mean waiting your turn at the canteen instead of pushing, giving back the pen you borrowed, and saying "thank you", "please" and "sorry" without feeling smaller for it. They also mean standing up for a classmate who is being laughed at, even when laughing along would be easier.
>
> Why does this matter so much? Because a school, like a home or a town, runs on trust. When we tell the truth, respect one another and look after things that aren't ours, the classroom becomes a safe place where everyone can learn. When lying, mockery and selfishness spread, not even the best teacher or the finest building can fix it.
>
> So let's treat this morning as a starting point. Pick one good habit to practise this week, then add another next week. Manners are learned by repetition, the same way neat handwriting is.

## Did you know? Facts about manners

> **Did you know?** In Arabic, the words *khuluq* (character) and *khalq* (physical form) come from the same three-letter root. One describes the inner person, the other the outer shape we can see.

- Did you know that the Quran praises the Prophet's character in a single short verse of Surah Al-Qalam: "And indeed, you are of a great moral character"?
- Did you know that, in a hadith in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet ﷺ defined righteousness, a word that covers every kind of goodness, as "good character"?
- Did you know that Luqman's advice to his son in Surah Luqman includes everyday manners such as walking humbly and lowering your voice?
- Did you know that the psychologist Albert Bandura showed, through his work on observational learning, that children pick up much of their behaviour by watching and copying adults? That is why a good example at home and at school teaches more than any lecture.

## Short story: the wallet on the bus

> Samer got on the school bus one morning and found a small wallet under his seat, with money inside and a card bearing its owner's name. He looked around; nobody seemed to be searching for it. His friend whispered, "Keep it. No one will know." Samer hesitated, then remembered a question his mother often asked him: "If people could see you right now, would you still do it?"
> When the bus reached school, Samer handed the wallet to the supervisor. After second period he was called to the office, where the bus driver was waiting with a smile. The wallet was his, and in it was that month's rent. He didn't say much, but from that day on he waved to Samer every single morning.

The story was written for this assembly, and its lesson is simple: honesty doesn't need a witness, because an honest person keeps watch over themselves.

## Quiz and closing

The presenter asks three quick questions and rewards the first correct answer:

1. In which surah does God tell the Prophet, "And indeed, you are of a great moral character"? (Surah Al-Qalam)
2. In the hadith of an-Nawwas ibn Sam'an, how did the Prophet ﷺ define righteousness? (Good character)
3. Name three polite words we use every day. (Thank you, please, sorry, or others)

> That brings our morning assembly on good manners to an end. Thank you for listening, and let's each carry at least one good habit into our classrooms today. Peace be upon you all.

## Questions teachers ask about this assembly

### How can I shorten it for primary pupils?

Keep the opening, the verse from Surah Al-Qalam, the first hadith, the first paragraph of the morning talk, the story and the quiz. These parts are short and use simple language, and the assembly stays at around five minutes.

### Can it run for a whole week?

Yes, and it works well that way. Give each day one quality: honesty on the first day, trustworthiness on the second, then humility, respect and mercy, with a different class presenting each morning.

### What makes an assembly stick instead of being just a reading?

Students need to understand what they are reading and to rehearse it aloud at least once. It also helps when the examples come from school life itself, such as the line, the canteen and the bus, rather than from general advice.
