Most of us have heard that we should drink eight glasses a day, and many people treat that number as a fixed target. So how much water should you drink a day, really? For healthy adults, the usual reference figure is **about 3.7 litres of total water for men and 2.7 litres for women**. That sounds like a lot until you learn what "total" means, and why your own number may sit above or below it.

## How much water should you drink a day?

Those two figures come from the US National Academies of Sciences, which set an approximate adequate daily intake for healthy adults:

| Group | Approximate total daily water |
| --- | --- |
| Men | About 3.7 litres |
| Women | About 2.7 litres |

The important part is **total water**. The figures cover everything you take in, not just glasses of plain water. Around 20% typically comes from food, particularly watery fruit and vegetables such as cucumber and watermelon. The rest comes from water itself and from other drinks like milk, tea and juice. In practice, the part you actually drink usually works out at roughly 2 to 3 litres.

## Is 8 glasses of water a day enough?

Eight glasses is about 2 litres. As a reminder it works well for plenty of people, and it's easy to remember. It just isn't a precise scientific rule, because the amount a body needs moves around depending on several things:

- Climate: in hot weather you lose a lot of water through sweat.
- Physical activity: sport and hard physical work push your needs up considerably.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: both increase how much fluid a woman needs.
- Illness: fever, vomiting or diarrhoea all drain extra fluid from the body.

Two people of the same age and weight can need quite different amounts. Someone sitting in an air-conditioned office all afternoon loses far less water than someone playing football outside in the midday sun.

## Working out your water intake by weight

A popular rule of thumb links intake to body size: about 30 to 35 ml per kilogram of body weight per day. A 70 kg person would need roughly 2.1 to 2.5 litres of fluid a day by that measure.

It's handy, but it's still a general estimate, and the real figure climbs with heat and exercise. People with kidney or heart conditions are a different case. Their doctor may set a quite different limit, and that advice should come before any general rule.

> **Did you know?** Water makes up about 50 to 60% of an adult's body weight, and an even higher share in newborn babies.

## Signs of dehydration to watch for

You don't need to measure every glass, because your body tells you a lot on its own. The simplest check is **urine colour**. Pale yellow usually means you're drinking enough, while dark yellow can be a sign you need more. Other signs of dehydration include:

- Strong thirst and a dry mouth
- Urinating less often
- Tiredness and headaches
- Dizziness, especially when standing up

Thirst is a signal worth listening to. The catch is that it gets weaker with age, so older adults may not feel thirsty even when their body is short of water. That's why regular reminders to drink help them, and why offering a grandparent a glass of water now and then is a small habit that matters.

## Can you drink too much water?

You can, though it's rare. Taking in very large amounts in a short time can lower the sodium level in your blood, and that is dangerous. It sometimes happens to endurance athletes who drink huge volumes without replacing the salts they sweat out. A healthy person who simply drinks when thirsty isn't normally at risk.

## Habits that make drinking enough easier

People who fall short usually aren't avoiding water. They forget about it on busy days. Tying drinks to things you already do each day helps:

- Keep a water bottle in your bag wherever you go.
- Have a glass of water soon after you wake up.
- Drink a glass with every meal.
- Eat plenty of water-rich fruit and vegetables.
- Drink more in summer, and before, during and after exercise.

## Other questions people ask about water

### Do tea and coffee count?

Yes, both add to your daily fluids. Water is still the better everyday choice, since it has no calories or sugar.

### How much water is in the human body?

Since water is about 50 to 60% of an adult's weight, a 70 kg person is carrying roughly 40 litres of it.

### Does drinking water help with weight loss?

Choosing water instead of sugary drinks cuts calories, and a glass before a meal may help you feel full sooner. On its own, though, it isn't a magic solution; it helps most alongside a balanced diet and regular activity.
