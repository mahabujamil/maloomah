**What is the fastest animal in the world?** It depends on where you look. The fastest animal on land is not the fastest in water, and both are slow next to the fastest bird when it dives out of the sky. Here are the speed champions of land, sea and air, and how they compare with us.

## Fastest land animal: the cheetah

The **cheetah** is the undisputed fastest land animal. In a short sprint it reaches roughly **100 to 110 km/h** (about 60 to 70 mph), and some sources report slightly higher measurements.

But the cheetah is a sprinter, not a marathon runner. It can hold its top speed only over a short distance, usually a few hundred metres, before it has to rest because its body heats up quickly.

What makes it so fast?

- A **very flexible spine** that coils and stretches like a spring with every stride, lengthening each step.
- A **light, slender body** and long legs.
- **Semi-retractable claws** that grip the ground like a sprinter's spikes.
- A **long tail** that works as a counterbalance during sudden turns in a chase.

## Fastest animal on Earth: the peregrine falcon

Drop the "on land" condition and the title of **fastest animal on Earth** goes to the **peregrine falcon**. When it dives on prey with its wings tucked in, a manoeuvre called a stoop, it exceeds **320 km/h** (about 200 mph), and some measurements have recorded even higher speeds.

That is diving speed with gravity's help, though. In ordinary level flight the falcon is much slower.

## Fastest animal in the ocean

In water, the **sailfish** and the **marlin** are usually named as the fastest fish, with speeds of around 100 km/h often quoted. Measuring speed underwater is hard, however, and recent studies suggest their real top speeds may be **lower than those popular figures**. Scientists still debate this title.

> **Did you know?** The cheetah wins the short sprint, but North America's **pronghorn** can hold a high speed for much longer distances. That's why some scientists call it the fastest long-distance runner among land animals.

## How fast are humans by comparison?

The fastest human on record is Jamaican sprinter **Usain Bolt**, who ran 100 metres in **9.58 seconds** in 2009. His top speed during that race was about **44 km/h**.

That's astonishing for a person, but it's less than half a cheetah's speed. Our real strength is **endurance**. Thanks to sweating, which cools the body, humans are among the best long-distance runners in the animal kingdom.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the fastest land animal?

The cheetah, which reaches about 100–110 km/h in a short sprint.

### What is the fastest animal of all?

The peregrine falcon in a dive, at more than 320 km/h.

### What is the fastest animal in the ocean?

The sailfish and the marlin are usually named, though their exact top speeds are still debated.

### Why can't cheetahs run for long?

Their sprint burns enormous energy and raises their body temperature fast, so they have to stop and recover after a short chase.

## Key takeaway

The **cheetah** is the fastest land animal at about 100–110 km/h. The **peregrine falcon** is the fastest animal of all, diving at over 320 km/h. At sea, the **sailfish and marlin** compete for the crown, though their numbers are still debated.
