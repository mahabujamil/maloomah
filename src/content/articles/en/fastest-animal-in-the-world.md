Ask a class what the fastest animal in the world is and most hands will go up for the cheetah. They're right, but only on land. The full answer depends on where you're looking: **the cheetah is the fastest animal on land, and the peregrine falcon is the fastest animal of all when it dives.** In the ocean, the title is still being argued over.

## The fastest land animal: the cheetah

Nothing on land outruns a cheetah. In a short sprint it reaches roughly 100 to 110 km/h (about 60 to 70 mph), and some sources report slightly higher figures. That's motorway speed for a car, reached by an animal on four legs.

The catch is that a cheetah is a sprinter, not a marathon runner. It can hold its top speed for only a short distance, usually a few hundred metres. Running that hard burns a huge amount of energy and heats its body quickly, so if the first chase fails, it has to stop, rest and cool down before trying again.

Its speed comes from the way its whole body is built. A very flexible spine coils and stretches like a spring with each stride, so every step covers more ground. The body is light and slender, carried on long legs. Its claws are only semi-retractable, unlike a house cat's, so they grip the ground the way spikes grip a running track. And the long tail is a counterbalance, keeping the cheetah steady when a gazelle suddenly swerves.

## The fastest animal in the world overall: the peregrine falcon

Drop the "on land" condition and the title goes to the **peregrine falcon**. It climbs high, tucks its wings against its body and drops onto its prey in a dive called a stoop. In that dive it passes 320 km/h (about 200 mph), and some measurements have recorded even higher speeds.

There's an important detail, though. That record is diving speed, and gravity is doing much of the work, the same way it speeds up a stone dropped from a height. In ordinary level flight the falcon is far slower, which is why careful sources say it's the fastest animal "in a dive".

## The fastest animal in the ocean

The sailfish and the marlin are the names you'll usually see, often credited with speeds of around 100 km/h. The problem is measurement: timing a fish sprinting through open water is genuinely difficult, and recent studies suggest their real top speeds may be lower than those popular figures. So the honest answer is that these two are the usual candidates, and scientists haven't settled the question.

> **Did you know?** The cheetah wins the short sprint, but North America's pronghorn can hold a high speed for much longer distances. That's why some scientists call it the fastest long-distance runner among land animals.

## How fast are humans by comparison?

The fastest human on record is Jamaican sprinter Usain Bolt, who ran 100 metres in 9.58 seconds in 2009. His top speed in that race was about 44 km/h. No one in a school sports day comes close, yet it's still less than half a cheetah's speed.

Where we shine is endurance. We sweat, and as sweat evaporates it cools the skin, so we can keep running for hours without overheating. That makes humans some of the best long-distance runners in the animal kingdom, even though we're far from the fastest.

## Questions students ask about animal speed

### Is a cheetah faster than a peregrine falcon?

On the ground, nothing beats a cheetah. Compare top speeds, though, and a diving falcon at over 320 km/h is moving about three times as fast. In level flight the falcon is much slower than its dive.

### Why can't cheetahs run for long?

A full sprint uses enormous energy and drives up their body temperature, so they have to stop and recover after a short chase. Humans are the opposite: slower, but able to keep going far longer.
