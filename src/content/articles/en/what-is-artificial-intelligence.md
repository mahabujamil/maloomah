Artificial intelligence is everywhere now: in your phone, in search engines, and in the apps that suggest what to watch next. But what is it, exactly? And how does a computer "think"? This guide explains the idea from scratch, with no equations.

## A simple definition

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is a field of computer science that builds systems able to do tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognising images, making decisions and solving problems.

## A quick history

- **1950:** British mathematician Alan Turing asked his famous question: "Can machines think?"
- **1956:** The term "artificial intelligence" was formally introduced at a workshop at Dartmouth College in the US, led by computer scientist John McCarthy.
- **The following decades:** The field went through waves of excitement and slowdowns known as "AI winters".
- **2012:** Deep neural networks made a big leap in image recognition, starting the "deep learning" era.
- **2017:** Researchers at Google published a new design called the Transformer, which became the basis of modern language models.
- **Late 2022 onwards:** AI chatbots reached the general public.

## How does it work? Traditional programming vs machine learning

In **traditional programming**, a programmer writes clear rules: "if this happens, do that".

In **machine learning**, we don't write the rules ourselves. Instead, we give the system thousands or millions of examples, and it works out the patterns on its own. To build a spam filter, for example, we show it a huge number of emails already labelled "spam" or "not spam", and over time it learns the signs that separate them.

### Neural networks

The most popular machine learning tool today is the **artificial neural network**, loosely inspired by brain cells. It is made of layers of connected "nodes", and each connection has a numerical weight. During **training**, these weights are adjusted bit by bit until the network's answers are as accurate as possible.

### Language models and chatbots

Chatbots that write and answer questions are powered by **large language models** trained on enormous amounts of text. The core idea is predicting the next word (or piece of a word) in a text; by repeating this, the model can write full sentences and paragraphs. The model is then trained further to follow instructions and give helpful, safe answers.

> **Did you know?** Language models don't necessarily "look up" answers in a database. They generate text word by word based on patterns they learned, which is why they can sometimes be confidently wrong.

## Examples from everyday life

- Unlocking your phone with **face recognition**.
- **Recommendations** for videos, products and songs.
- **Machine translation** between languages.
- **Map apps** that predict traffic and suggest the fastest route.
- **Spam filters.**
- **Voice assistants** and chatbots.
- Helping doctors **read medical images** such as X-rays.

## Types of AI

- **Narrow AI:** excels at a specific task or set of tasks. This is all the AI we use today.
- **Artificial general intelligence (AGI):** a hypothetical system that could learn and reason in any field the way a person can. Scientists still disagree on when it might arrive, and even on how to define it.

## Limits and risks to know about

1. **Confident mistakes:** a system can present wrong information very convincingly.
2. **Bias:** if the training data is biased, the results can reflect that bias.
3. **Privacy:** what you type into some tools may be stored or used, so be careful with sensitive information.
4. **Misinformation:** the technology can be used to create fake images or voices.

## Tips for using AI tools wisely

- Always double-check important information, especially medical, legal and financial facts.
- Never share passwords or sensitive personal data.
- Ask clearly and give enough context to get better results.
- Treat it as an assistant that speeds up your work, not as the final authority.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will AI take my job?

It is more likely to change many jobs than to erase them: some tasks will be automated, and new tasks and roles will appear. The best preparation is learning to use these tools in your own field.

### Is AI conscious or does it have feelings?

There is no scientific evidence that today's systems have consciousness or feelings like humans, and the question is still debated by scientists and philosophers.

## Key takeaway

AI systems learn from data instead of following hand-written rules, and the most powerful today are neural networks and large language models. They are very powerful tools, but they still need a thoughtful human to check and decide.
