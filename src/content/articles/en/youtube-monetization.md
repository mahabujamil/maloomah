Plenty of people start a channel hoping the money will follow quickly. It can, eventually: millions of creators earn real income from the platform. But knowing how to make money on YouTube starts with understanding that ads only switch on once a channel meets the **YouTube Partner Program** requirements, and that ads are just one of several income streams. Some of those streams work fine even if you never appear on camera.

## How does YouTube pay creators?

The Partner Program (YPP) is the official way in. Once you're accepted, YouTube places ads on your videos and splits what advertisers pay: creators keep 55% of the ad revenue on regular long-form videos. The money itself is paid out through a Google AdSense account.

## YouTube monetisation requirements

To earn ad revenue, a channel has to hit one of two thresholds. Long-form creators usually aim for watch hours, while Shorts creators aim for views:

| Requirement | Option 1 | Option 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscribers | 1,000 | 1,000 |
| Views | 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months | 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days |

There's also an earlier tier that opens at 500 subscribers. It gives access to some fan-funding features, such as memberships and tips, but not to ad revenue.

The numbers aren't the only condition. Your channel has to follow YouTube's policies and Community Guidelines, you need to live in a country where the programme is available (YouTube keeps an official list), and you must link an AdSense account and turn on two-step verification.

> **Note:** YouTube's requirements can change, so always check the official help pages for the latest details.

## Ways to make money on YouTube

Most successful channels combine several of these rather than relying on ads alone:

1. Ads: the best-known source, driven by views and audience type.
2. Channel memberships: fans pay a monthly fee for special perks.
3. Super Chat and Super Thanks: viewer tips during live streams or on videos.
4. YouTube Premium revenue: a share of subscription fees when Premium members watch your videos.
5. Sponsorships: brands pay you directly to feature their products.
6. Affiliate marketing: product links in your description that earn a commission on each sale. Our [affiliate marketing guide](/en/articles/affiliate-marketing-guide/) covers how it works.
7. Selling your own products: courses, books, services or merchandise.

The last three don't depend on YouTube's approval at all. A channel with a loyal audience can land a sponsor or sell a course well before it qualifies for ads.

## How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?

There's no fixed rate, because the advertiser sets the price and YouTube shares it with you. Where your viewers live matters most: views from countries where advertisers pay more earn more. Your niche matters too, since topics like finance, tech and education tend to attract pricier ads. Video length, how engaged viewers are, and even the time of year all shift the figure. That's why two channels with similar view counts can earn very different amounts per 1,000 views.

## Making money on YouTube without showing your face

Not everyone wants to be on camera, and plenty of channel formats don't need you to be:

1. Voice-over explainers: informative videos narrated over images and graphics.
2. Screen-recorded tutorials teaching software, apps and games.
3. Storytelling and documentary channels told through narration.
4. Animation and infographics.
5. Cooking channels that only show hands and food.
6. Calm, ambient or study-music channels with good visuals (using properly licensed audio).

> **Watch out:** YouTube doesn't pay for **repetitive, copied or mass-produced** content without real value, even if you use AI tools. Your videos need to add something clear for the viewer.

## Tips to grow your channel

Start with a clear niche you enjoy and can keep up. A channel that jumps between cooking, gaming and news gives viewers no reason to subscribe. Then upload on a steady schedule, at least one video a week, so people know when to come back.

Titles and thumbnails are what viewers see before they click, so they deserve real effort. After the click, the first 30 seconds usually decide whether someone stays, so open strongly. Use the words people actually search for in your title and description, and use Shorts to pull new subscribers towards your longer videos.

Reply to comments; that's how a community forms around a channel. And respect copyright. Using other people's clips or music without permission can cost you monetisation.

## Questions beginners ask about YouTube monetisation

### How long does it take to qualify?

It varies a lot. Some channels qualify within months; others take a year or more. Sticking with it and improving each video matters most.

### Do I need expensive equipment?

No. Many successful channels started with a smartphone, a cheap microphone and free editing software. Clear audio matters more than an expensive camera.

### Can I make money on YouTube from any country?

Only where the Partner Program is available, which covers most countries. Check YouTube's official list for yours.

Getting to 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 watch hours or 10 million Shorts views is the gate to ad revenue. Pick a niche you care about, publish regularly and give viewers something worth their time, and the other income streams tend to open up along the way.
