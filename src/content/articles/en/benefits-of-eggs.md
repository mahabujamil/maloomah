Eggs are cheap, quick to cook and a breakfast staple in homes from Cairo to Amman. But what are the real **benefits of eggs**? Do they raise your cholesterol, as people used to warn? And how many can you eat in a day? Here is what reliable nutrition sources say, without the hype.

## Nutrition in one large egg

A large hard-boiled egg (about 50 g) contains roughly the following, based on USDA FoodData Central. Values vary a little with egg size and the hen's diet:

| Nutrient | Approximate amount in one large egg |
| --- | --- |
| Calories | about 78 kcal |
| Protein | about 6 g |
| Fat | about 5 g (about 1.6 g saturated) |
| Cholesterol | about 186 mg (almost all in the yolk) |
| Choline | about 147 mg |
| Vitamin B12 | about 0.5 mcg |
| Vitamin D | about 1 mcg (roughly 40 IU) |
| Selenium | about 15 mcg |
| Lutein + zeaxanthin | about 200–250 mcg |

## Benefits of eggs: what boiled eggs do for your body

- **Complete protein:** eggs supply all the essential amino acids, which makes them one of the best protein sources for building and keeping muscle.
- **Keeps you full:** protein is satisfying, so an egg breakfast may help some people manage how much they eat later. It is not a weight-loss treatment on its own, though.
- **Choline for the brain:** eggs are one of the richest food sources of choline, a nutrient important for the brain and nerves, and many adults don't get enough of it.
- **Eye health:** the yolk contains **lutein and zeaxanthin**, antioxidants that collect in the retina and are linked to healthy vision as we age.
- **Vitamins and minerals:** including vitamin B12, selenium and some vitamin D, one of the few foods that contain it naturally.

> **Did you know?** Nearly all of an egg's fat and cholesterol is in the **yolk**, while the **white** is mostly water and protein. Yet the yolk is also where the choline, lutein and most of the vitamins are.

## Eggs and cholesterol: what the evidence says

For decades people were told to cut back hard on eggs because of the cholesterol in the yolk. Newer research has changed that picture considerably:

- In December 2019, the **American Heart Association** published a science advisory concluding that current evidence does not support a specific numeric limit on dietary cholesterol. It said **one egg a day** is reasonable for healthy people as part of a healthy diet.
- The same advisory still recommends an overall diet low in cholesterol, and urges caution for people with high LDL ("bad") cholesterol, especially those with diabetes.
- Nutrition experts at Harvard point out that, for most people, **saturated and trans fats** have a bigger effect on blood cholesterol than the cholesterol in food.

The bottom line: **what you eat with your eggs** matters. A boiled egg with wholegrain bread and vegetables is very different from eggs fried in butter next to processed meats.

## How many eggs a day is healthy?

There is no single number for everyone, but common general guidance looks like this:

| Group | What sources generally suggest |
| --- | --- |
| Healthy adults | up to about one egg a day within a healthy diet |
| Older adults with normal cholesterol | up to two a day, according to the American Heart Association |
| People with high cholesterol or diabetes | be cautious and ask a doctor about the right amount |

The research isn't fully settled: some observational studies have linked higher egg intake with heart risk, while others have not. Moderation is the safest approach.

## Boiled vs fried eggs

The egg itself is nutritionally similar either way; the difference is **how you cook it**:

- **Boiled eggs** need no oil or butter, so a large one stays at about 78 calories.
- **Fried eggs** pick up extra calories and fat from the oil or butter. If you fry, use a small amount of a vegetable oil such as olive oil.

### How long to boil an egg

Times depend on egg size and heat, but as a rough guide once the water is boiling:

| Result | Approximate time |
| --- | --- |
| Runny yolk (soft-boiled) | about 6 minutes |
| Jammy, half-set yolk | about 8 minutes |
| Fully firm yolk (hard-boiled) | about 10–12 minutes |

Then move the eggs into cold water to stop the cooking and make peeling easier.

## Egg safety and how to tell if an egg is fresh

Eggs can occasionally carry **Salmonella**, a bacterium that causes food poisoning. Food safety agencies such as the USDA advise:

- **Keep eggs refrigerated.**
- **Cook eggs until both the yolk and the white are firm**, especially for pregnant women, young children, older adults and anyone with weak immunity.
- **Use hard-boiled eggs within about a week**, stored in the fridge.
- Avoid cracked eggs, and wash your hands after handling raw eggs.

**The float test:** place the egg in a bowl of cold water.

- If it **sinks and lies flat**, it is fresh.
- If it **sinks but stands upright**, it is a bit older but usually still fine.
- If it **floats**, it is old, because the air pocket inside grows over time.

The test tells you an egg's **age**, not necessarily whether it is safe. The clearest sign of a bad egg is a **foul smell** when you crack it; if you notice one, throw it away.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of eggs in the morning?

There is no special proven benefit to eating eggs at a particular time. Still, boiled eggs at breakfast provide protein and keep you full, which helps many people start the day with a balanced meal.

### Do eggs raise cholesterol?

For most healthy people, eggs affect blood cholesterol less than was once thought, and the American Heart Association considers one egg a day reasonable. People with high cholesterol or diabetes should check with their doctor.

### How many eggs a day is too many?

General guidance suggests about one egg a day suits most healthy adults, and up to two for older adults with normal cholesterol. Regularly eating much more than that isn't supported by current advice, so moderation is wise.

### Are boiled eggs healthier than fried eggs?

Boiled eggs are lower in calories and fat because no oil is added. Fried eggs can still fit a healthy diet if you use only a little vegetable oil.

### Do boiled eggs have side effects?

Eggs are safe for most people in moderation. However, egg allergy is one of the most common food allergies, especially in children, and undercooked eggs can carry Salmonella.

## Key takeaway

Eggs are an affordable, nutritious food, rich in complete protein, choline, lutein and several vitamins. Current guidance suggests about one egg a day is reasonable for healthy people, as long as you watch how they are cooked and what they're served with. Boil rather than fry when you can, cook them until firm, and keep them in the fridge. If you have a specific health condition, your doctor is the right person to set your limit.
