Crocodiles are in the news again after some strange headlines, and people are searching for **crocodile facts**: how big do they get, what do they eat, and do they lay eggs? Here are the most reliable facts about this ancient reptile, whose ancestors shared the planet with the dinosaurs.

## Crocodile facts: what kind of animal is it?

The crocodile is a **reptile** in the order Crocodilia, which also includes alligators, caimans and the gharial. There are **about 25 living crocodilian species**, depending on how they are classified, and roughly half of them are true crocodiles.

Crocodiles live in the warm regions of Africa, Asia, the Americas and Australia, in rivers, lakes and swamps. Some species cope well with salt water.

Key features:

- **Thick, armoured skin** reinforced by small bony plates under the scales.
- **Eyes and nostrils on top of the head**, so it can see and breathe with almost its whole body underwater.
- **A powerful tail** that drives it through the water like an oar.
- **Cold-blooded**, which is why crocodiles bask in the sun to warm up and slip back into the water to cool down.

## The largest crocodile species

| Species | Where it lives | Typical adult male length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saltwater crocodile | Southeast Asia and northern Australia | About 4–5 m, rarely over 6 m |
| Nile crocodile | Sub-Saharan Africa and the Nile valley | About 3.5–5 m |
| Orinoco crocodile | Venezuela and Colombia | About 4 m or more |

The **saltwater crocodile** is the largest living reptile in the world, and the very biggest males can approach a tonne. The **Nile crocodile** is Africa's largest crocodilian; National Geographic gives its average lifespan in the wild as about 45 years, and it can live longer in captivity.

## What do crocodiles eat, and how do they hunt?

Crocodiles are **predators** that eat fish, birds, reptiles and mammals. Hatchlings start with insects and small fish, and prey gets bigger as they grow.

Their strategy is the **ambush**: a crocodile waits motionless just below the surface with only its eyes and nostrils showing, then lunges. Because it **cannot chew**, it swallows food in large chunks and may spin its body in the water to tear prey apart.

Crocodiles also **swallow stones**. Scientists think these help grind food in the stomach and may also help with balance in the water.

## Crocodile bite force

In a well-known 2012 study, Gregory Erickson and colleagues measured the bite force of crocodilian species. A **saltwater crocodile** produced the strongest bite then recorded for a living animal, about **3,700 pounds per square inch** (around 16,000 newtons).

The twist: the muscles that **open** the jaws are relatively weak, while the muscles that close them are enormously strong.

> **Did you know?** The sex of baby crocodiles is decided by the **temperature of the nest** during incubation, not by genetics as in humans. A difference of a few degrees can make most of a clutch male or female.

## Do crocodiles lay eggs?

**Yes, crocodiles lay eggs.** The female digs a nest in sand or builds a mound of vegetation near the water, lays **dozens of hard-shelled eggs**, and guards the nest throughout incubation, which lasts around three months in the Nile crocodile.

When the young are ready to hatch they call from inside the eggs. The mother opens the nest and may **gently carry the hatchlings in her mouth** to the water. Even so, many babies are eaten in their first weeks by birds of prey, large fish and other predators.

## Crocodile vs alligator: how to tell them apart

| Feature | Crocodile | Alligator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Snout | Longer and narrower, V-shaped | Broader and rounded, U-shaped |
| Teeth with mouth closed | Lower teeth visible, including the large fourth tooth | Lower teeth mostly hidden |
| Salt water | Some species tolerate it well | Prefers fresh water |
| Range | Africa, Asia, the Americas and Australia | Only the United States and China |

## Surprising facts about crocodiles

- **A crocodile can't stick its tongue out**; a membrane holds it to the floor of the mouth.
- **It can go months without food** when it has to, because a cold-blooded body burns little energy.
- **It's a long-distance diver**: dives usually last minutes, but a resting crocodile can stay under far longer.
- **Its teeth are replaced**: when one falls out a new one grows in, many times over a lifetime.
- **It's faster in water than on land**, though some species can make a short dash on the ground.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do crocodiles live?

National Geographic puts the Nile crocodile's average lifespan in the wild at **about 45 years**. Other sources report longer lives for large individuals, especially in zoos.

### What is the largest crocodile in the world?

The largest species is the **saltwater crocodile**, which is also the largest living reptile. Some males exceed 6 metres.

### Are there still crocodiles in the Nile in Egypt?

Their range in Egypt has shrunk greatly. Today they live mainly in **Lake Nasser**, south of the Aswan High Dam, while they remain common in rivers across sub-Saharan Africa.

### Are crocodiles dangerous to people?

Yes. Large species such as the Nile and saltwater crocodiles are **very dangerous**, so stay well away from riverbanks where they live and follow local safety advice.

## Key takeaway

The crocodile is an ancient, cold-blooded ambush hunter with one of the strongest bites in the animal kingdom. It lays eggs, and nest temperature decides the sex of its young. The saltwater crocodile is the biggest of all, while the Nile crocodile rules Africa's rivers.
