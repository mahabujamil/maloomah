When you eat a sandwich or an apple, your body starts a precise job that goes on for hours. The **human digestive system** breaks food down into particles small enough for the blood to absorb and carry to every cell, then gets rid of whatever the body doesn't need. In this lesson we'll look at the organs of the digestive system and what each one does, and follow a mouthful of food step by step.

## What is the digestive system?

The digestive system is a group of organs that work together on three jobs:

1. **Digestion:** breaking food down into tiny molecules.
2. **Absorption:** moving those useful molecules into the blood.
3. **Elimination:** getting rid of the leftovers the body doesn't need.

It has two parts:

- **The digestive tract (alimentary canal):** a long tube that food passes through, from the mouth to the anus.
- **The accessory organs:** food doesn't pass through them, but they release juices that help digestion. They are the salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas.

## Organs of the digestive tract and their functions

| Organ | What it does | Key fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mouth | Teeth cut and grind food; saliva moistens it | Adults usually have 32 teeth; children have 20 milk teeth |
| Pharynx | Shared passage for food and air | The epiglottis covers the windpipe when you swallow |
| Oesophagus | Muscular tube that carries food to the stomach | About 25 cm long in adults |
| Stomach | Stores food and mixes it with gastric juice | Releases hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin |
| Small intestine | Finishes digestion and absorbs nutrients | The longest part of the tract, about 6 to 7 metres |
| Large intestine | Absorbs water and salts; forms waste | About 1.5 metres long, and wider than the small intestine |
| Rectum and anus | Store waste, then expel it | The end of the digestive tract |

The **small intestine** has three parts: the **duodenum** (the first part, where pancreatic juice and bile flow in), the **jejunum** and the **ileum**. Its lining is covered with huge numbers of tiny finger-like bumps called **villi**, which greatly increase the surface area for absorbing food.

## The accessory organs

- **Salivary glands:** make saliva, which moistens food and contains the enzyme **amylase**, which starts digesting starch in the mouth.
- **Liver:** the largest internal organ in the human body and its largest gland. It makes **bile**, which helps digest fats, and does many other jobs, such as storing some vitamins and clearing some harmful substances from the blood.
- **Gallbladder:** a small sac under the liver that stores bile and releases it into the duodenum when needed.
- **Pancreas:** releases a juice full of enzymes that digest starch, protein and fat. It also makes the hormone **insulin**, which controls blood sugar.

## The journey of food, step by step

1. **In the mouth:** teeth cut and grind the food, and the tongue mixes it with saliva into a soft lump called a **bolus**.
2. **Swallowing:** the tongue pushes the bolus into the pharynx, and the **epiglottis** closes over the windpipe so food doesn't go into the lungs.
3. **In the oesophagus:** waves of muscle contraction called **peristalsis** push the food down to the stomach.
4. **In the stomach:** the stomach muscles churn the food with acidic gastric juice for several hours until it becomes a thick liquid called **chyme**. A layer of mucus protects the stomach wall from the acid.
5. **In the small intestine:** pancreatic juice, bile and intestinal juice finish the digestion, and the villi absorb the nutrients into the blood.
6. **In the large intestine:** most of the remaining water and salts are absorbed, and waste is formed.
7. **Elimination:** waste is stored in the rectum and then leaves the body.

How long the whole journey takes varies a lot from person to person and meal to meal. It can take anywhere from about a day to a few days.

> **Did you know?** The small intestine is called "small" because it's narrower than the large intestine, not because it's shorter. In fact it is several times longer.

## Mechanical and chemical digestion

Digestion happens in two ways that work together:

- **Mechanical digestion:** breaking food into smaller pieces without changing what it's made of, for example chewing and the churning of the stomach.
- **Chemical digestion:** turning food into simpler substances using **enzymes** and digestive juices.

| Enzyme or juice | Made in | What it digests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salivary amylase | Salivary glands | Starts digesting starch |
| Pepsin | Stomach | Starts digesting protein |
| Bile | Liver (stored in the gallbladder) | Breaks fat into small droplets |
| Lipase | Pancreas | Digests fat |
| Pancreatic amylase | Pancreas | Finishes digesting starch |
| Trypsin | Pancreas | Continues digesting protein |

## How to keep your digestive system healthy

- Chew your food well and don't eat too fast.
- Eat plenty of vegetables, fruit and whole grains, which are rich in fibre.
- Drink enough water through the day. Read: [How much water should you drink a day?](/en/articles/how-much-water-should-you-drink/)
- Wash your hands before eating, and wash fruit and vegetables well.
- Stay active and exercise regularly.

If you have tummy pain that keeps coming back or won't go away, tell your parents so they can take you to a doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the longest organ in the digestive system?

The small intestine. In adults it's about 6 to 7 metres long, coiled up to fit inside the abdomen.

### Where does digestion start and end?

Digestion starts in the **mouth**, with chewing and the enzyme in saliva. It is completed in the **small intestine**, where nutrients are absorbed. The large intestine then absorbs water and forms waste.

### Why doesn't the stomach digest itself?

Its wall is covered by a layer of **mucus** that protects it from acid and enzymes, and the cells of its lining are constantly replaced.

### What does the liver do in digestion?

The liver makes bile, which breaks fat into small droplets so the enzyme lipase can digest it more easily. To find out which organ is the largest in the whole body, read: [What is the largest organ in the human body?](/en/articles/largest-organ-in-the-human-body/)

## Key takeaway

The **human digestive system** is a long tube from the mouth to the anus, helped by accessory organs: the salivary glands, liver, gallbladder and pancreas. Food is digested mechanically and chemically, most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine, and the large intestine forms the waste.
