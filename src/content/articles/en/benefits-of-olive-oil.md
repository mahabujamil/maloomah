As the olive harvest gets under way across Jordan and the Levant, one question climbs the search charts: what are the **benefits of olive oil**, and does it deserve its reputation? The short answer is yes. Olive oil is one of the most studied fats in nutrition science and a cornerstone of the Mediterranean diet. This guide covers what the evidence actually shows, which claims are myths, and how to pick a genuine oil.

## What's in olive oil?

Olive oil is pure fat. One tablespoon (about 13.5 g) has roughly **119 calories**. What sets it apart is the type of fat:

| Component | Approximate share or amount |
| --- | --- |
| Monounsaturated fat (mostly oleic acid) | about 73% |
| Polyunsaturated fat | about 10% |
| Saturated fat | about 14% |
| Vitamins E and K | useful amounts |
| Antioxidant polyphenols | mainly in extra virgin oil |

## Olive oil health benefits for the heart

The strongest evidence for the benefits of olive oil concerns heart health:

- **Replacing saturated fat:** swapping butter or ghee for olive oil helps improve LDL ("bad") cholesterol.
- **The PREDIMED trial:** this Spanish study followed thousands of people at high cardiovascular risk. A Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra virgin olive oil was linked to about 30% fewer major cardiovascular events than a reduced-fat diet.
- **Protecting blood lipids:** the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) accepts that olive oil polyphenols help protect blood lipids from oxidative stress, provided the oil contains enough of them.

## Other benefits for the body

- **Anti-inflammatory compounds:** extra virgin oil contains oleocanthal, the compound behind the peppery sting in your throat, which research suggests has anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Better vitamin absorption:** a spoonful on your salad helps your body absorb fat-soluble vitamins such as A and K from vegetables.
- **Fullness:** fat slows stomach emptying, so meals keep you full longer. It's calorie-dense, though, so portions matter.
- **Digestion:** it may have a mild softening effect for some people, but it isn't a treatment for chronic constipation.

> **Did you know?** That slight burning feeling in your throat when you taste fresh extra virgin olive oil is a sign it's rich in polyphenols. Professional tasters actually look for it.

## Olive oil on an empty stomach: fact or hype?

Social media is full of claims that a spoonful of olive oil first thing in the morning, or with lemon, "melts fat" or "detoxes" the body. **There is no strong scientific evidence for that.** The benefits come from eating olive oil regularly as part of a healthy diet, whether that's at breakfast or with dinner.

Many people also use olive oil on **hair and skin** as a natural moisturiser. It may help dry hair, but research is limited, and it may not suit oily, acne-prone skin.

## Types of olive oil and how to tell if it's real

- **Extra virgin:** extracted mechanically, without high heat or chemicals, with free acidity of no more than 0.8%. It has the most flavour and the most polyphenols.
- **Virgin:** slightly lower grade, with acidity up to 2%.
- **Refined or "light" olive oil:** processed oil that loses most of its polyphenols. "Light" refers to flavour, not calories.
- **Olive pomace oil:** extracted from the leftover pulp; the lowest grade.

To find a good oil, read the label for "extra virgin" and a harvest date, buy from a trusted brand or mill, and be wary of prices that look too good to be true. The popular fridge test is unreliable; a lab test of acidity is the real proof.

**Storage:** keep it in a dark glass or metal container, away from light and heat, and use it within about one to two years of pressing.

## How much olive oil per day?

There's no single official amount. Many studies used roughly **1.5 to 4 tablespoons a day**, replacing other fats rather than adding to them. If you're watching your weight, remember each tablespoon is about 120 calories. Extra virgin olive oil is also more heat-stable than its reputation suggests, so it's fine for everyday home cooking. Try it in dishes like [falafel](/en/articles/falafel-recipe/) or [lentil soup](/en/articles/lentil-soup-recipe/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is olive oil good to drink on an empty stomach?
There's no evidence of a special benefit. What matters is eating it regularly with food, at any time of day.

### Does olive oil raise cholesterol?
No. It tends to improve cholesterol when it replaces saturated fats like butter and ghee.

### Can you fry with olive oil?
Yes. Extra virgin olive oil handles normal home cooking temperatures well, though repeated deep frying breaks down some of its beneficial compounds.

### How can you tell if olive oil is real?
Check the label, acidity and harvest date, buy from a trusted source, and look for a fresh, slightly bitter and peppery taste. Lab testing is the only certain method.

### Is olive oil good for hair?
It may moisturise dry hair and reduce breakage, but the scientific evidence is limited.

## Key takeaway

The **benefits of olive oil** are real, and the strongest are for heart health when it replaces saturated fat. Choose extra virgin, store it away from light, and keep portions moderate because it's calorie-dense. Don't expect miracles from a morning spoonful: the payoff comes from steady use within a balanced diet.
