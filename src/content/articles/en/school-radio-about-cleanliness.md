A **school assembly on cleanliness** is one of the scripts teachers need most, because cleanliness is a daily habit that students learn at school and carry home with them. It fits November especially well: **19 November** is **World Toilet Day**, which reminds the world how much sanitation and hygiene matter. Below is a complete, ready-to-read script for the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio": an introduction, Quran recitation, a hadith, a morning talk on personal and school cleanliness, a "Did you know?" segment, a short story, a quiz and a closing.

## Running order for a school assembly on cleanliness

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Teachers can drop or shorten a segment to fit the time.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 1.5 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | One student |
| 4 | Morning talk | 2 min | One student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 min | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Short story | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 7 | Daily hygiene tips | 1 min | Three students |
| 8 | Quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

## Introduction

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
> Good morning, and welcome to a bright, clean new day.
> Our respected head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning to you all.
> Today we want to talk about a habit that is small in size but huge in its effect: **cleanliness**. Clean bodies and clothes, clean classrooms and playgrounds, clean streets and surroundings. Cleanliness is the mark of a civilised person and one of the secrets of good health and happiness.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran recitation on cleanliness and purity

The student recites the following verses. The Arabic text is taken word for word from quran.com; the English meanings follow the Saheeh International translation:

> «وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ» — "And your clothing purify." [Surah Al-Muddaththir, 74:4]

> «...إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ» — "...Indeed, Allah loves those who are constantly repentant and loves those who purify themselves." [Surah Al-Baqarah, from 2:222]

> «...فِيهِ رِجَالٌ يُحِبُّونَ أَن يَتَطَهَّرُوا ۚ وَاللَّهُ يُحِبُّ الْمُطَّهِّرِينَ» — "...Within it are men who love to purify themselves; and Allah loves those who purify themselves." [Surah At-Tawbah, from 9:108]

**Host after the recitation:** And now the hadith, presented by ……

## Hadith on cleanliness

> Abu Malik al-Ash'ari reported that the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said: «الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الْإِيمَانِ» — "Purification is half of faith..." [Sahih Muslim, 223]

**The meaning in brief:** purification means keeping clean and pure, and "half of faith" shows how important it is. Cleanliness is not a side matter; it has a central place in a Muslim's life.

## Morning talk on personal and school cleanliness

> Dear friends,
> Have you noticed how comfortable we feel in a clean, tidy classroom, and how uneasy we feel when paper is scattered on the floor? Cleanliness is not just about looking nice. It is about health, comfort and respect.
> **Personal hygiene** starts with us: bathing regularly, washing our hands with soap and water before eating and after using the toilet, brushing our teeth morning and night, trimming our nails and wearing clean clothes. Germs we cannot see travel from dirty hands to our mouths and eyes and cause many illnesses.
> **School cleanliness** is everyone's job: never dropping paper or sweet wrappers on the floor, putting rubbish in the bin, keeping desks and walls free of graffiti, and leaving the toilets as clean as we would like to find them.
> **A clean environment** is the next step: looking after parks and streets, using less plastic and sorting our rubbish where we can.
> So let our motto today be: "A clean school starts with my hands."

## "Did you know?" facts about cleanliness

Two students can read these in turn:

- **Did you know** that washing your hands with soap and water for at least **20 seconds** is one of the simplest ways to stop germs spreading?
- **Did you know** that 20 seconds is roughly the time it takes to sing a short song twice, a trick teachers use with young children?
- **Did you know** that **Global Handwashing Day** has been marked every year on **15 October** since 2008?
- **Did you know** that in 2013 the UN General Assembly declared **19 November** World Toilet Day?
- **Did you know** that soap removes germs by breaking up the grease and dirt they stick to, so the water can wash them away?
- **Did you know** that brushing your teeth twice a day with fluoride toothpaste helps protect them from decay?

> **Did you know?** Your body sheds millions of dead skin cells every day, which is one reason why regular bathing keeps skin clean and healthy. Find more on our [human body facts page](/en/did-you-know/body/).

## A short story about cleanliness

> **Story: Why did Omar get ill?**
> Omar loved football. At break he ran until he was soaked with sweat, then went back to class and ate his sandwich straight away without washing his hands.
> One day Omar had a stomach ache and missed three days of school. When he came back, his science teacher asked him what had happened, then handed him a magnifying glass and asked him to look at his hands.
> "Germs are too small to see with your eyes or even with this glass," the teacher said, "but they are on everything we touch: the ball, the door handle, the stair rail. When you eat with dirty hands, those germs end up in your stomach."
> From that day Omar was always first at the sink before lunch, and he even reminded his friends: "Soap and water before the sandwich!"
> **The lesson:** one minute at the sink can save you days of being ill.

## Daily hygiene tips

Each student reads one tip clearly:

1. Wash your hands with soap and water before and after eating, after using the toilet and after playing.
2. Brush your teeth in the morning and before bed.
3. Bathe regularly and wear clean clothes every day.
4. Trim your nails every week, because dirt hides under them.
5. Cover your mouth and nose with a tissue or the bend of your elbow when you sneeze or cough, then put the tissue in the bin.
6. Don't share personal items such as towels and water bottles.

## Closing

> To close our assembly, let us remember that cleanliness is not only the caretaker's job. It is the responsibility of every one of us.
> Let's start today: clean hands, a tidy classroom and a playground with no litter.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the main segments of a school assembly on cleanliness?

The core segments are the introduction, Quran recitation, the hadith, the morning talk, "Did you know?" and the closing. If there is time, add the short story and the quiz, which hold younger students' attention.

### How can I adapt the cleanliness assembly for primary school?

Shorten the morning talk, focus on the story and the short tips, and ask two students to act out the correct way to wash hands with soap and water in front of everyone. Acting it out sticks in children's memories better than words.

### What is the difference between personal hygiene and school cleanliness?

Personal hygiene is about the student's own body, clothes and belongings: bathing, handwashing and brushing teeth. School cleanliness is about shared spaces such as classrooms, the playground, the toilets and desks, and every student has a part in it.

### When should I present an assembly on cleanliness?

Any day of the school year works, but it fits best in the week of **19 November** (World Toilet Day) or **15 October** (Global Handwashing Day), or at the start of a term as part of a school clean-up campaign.

## Key takeaway

A **school assembly on cleanliness** reminds students that good health starts with simple habits: washing hands, brushing teeth and looking after the classroom and playground. Use the ready-made segments above as they are or adapt them for your school, and finish with the motto "A clean school starts with my hands."
