A Reel hits 100,000 views and its creator expects a payment to land at the end of the month. That's how a lot of people picture it, and it's mostly wrong. You can **make money on Instagram**, but the money rarely comes from views alone. It comes from a handful of tools with specific requirements, and from deals that happen outside the app's own payment system. Views are where the confusion starts, so that's the place to begin.

## Does Instagram pay for views?

Not in the way YouTube does. **Instagram doesn't automatically pay every creator for views**; there's no equivalent of the YouTube Partner Program that every eligible account can join. Direct payouts from the platform come through specific tools, mainly Gifts and Subscriptions. Performance-based Reels bonuses do exist, but you can't apply for them: they're invite-only and offered in a limited number of countries.

Even Gifts aren't tied to view counts. A viewer has to decide to send one. So for most creators, the real income comes from brand deals, affiliate marketing and selling their own products or services.

## Instagram monetization requirements

Some conditions apply before you even look at a particular tool. You need a **professional account**, either creator or business; a personal account simply doesn't show the monetization options. Most tools also require you to be 18 or older, which is worth knowing early if you're a student hoping to earn this way. And you have to live in a country where the tool you want is available.

The condition people forget is the **Partner Monetization Policies**, along with the Community Guidelines. In practice they ask for two things: content that is genuinely yours, and no bought followers or fake engagement. An account can have plenty of followers and still be locked out because it broke these rules.

## Ways to make money on Instagram and their requirements

Each method has its own bar, and some have no follower minimum at all:

| Method | Common requirements | How you earn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gifts on Reels | Professional account, 18+, about 500 followers, eligible country | Viewers buy "Stars" and send them to you as gifts |
| Subscriptions | Professional account, 18+, about 10,000 followers, eligible country | A monthly fee for subscriber-only content |
| Badges in Live | Professional account and similar requirements, eligible country | Followers buy badges during your live streams |
| Branded content | Professional account and the branded content policies | Companies pay you to promote their products |
| Affiliate marketing | No Instagram follower minimum | A commission on every sale through your link |
| Your own products or services | No minimum | Sell a course, design or service directly to your audience |

That also answers the most common question, how many followers you need. It depends on the tool: Gifts usually open up at around 500 followers and Subscriptions at around 10,000, while affiliate links and selling services need no particular number.

> **Important:** the figures above are the most widely reported requirements at the time of writing, but Meta changes requirements and eligible countries from time to time. The real authority is the Professional dashboard in your own app.

## How to turn on Instagram monetization, step by step

Everything happens inside the app:

1. Switch to a professional account in Settings.
2. Open your Professional dashboard.
3. Go to Monetization status. This page tells you if your account is eligible, which requirements are missing, and if any violations are blocking you.
4. Turn on the tools available to you, such as Gifts or Subscriptions.
5. Add a payout account and any tax details required to receive your earnings.

Sometimes a tool still won't appear even though you've passed the follower count. Usually that means it hasn't launched in your country yet, or your account has broken a policy. The Monetization status page will show you which.

## Making money on Instagram where tools aren't available

Many of the direct-earning tools aren't offered everywhere, which is why so many people search for "Instagram monetization in my country". The indirect routes are a different story. They work almost anywhere, and they don't need Instagram's approval.

Think about a new café in Amman or a small clothing label in Casablanca. The owners want creators with an engaged local following, even a small one, because those followers are their likely customers. [Affiliate marketing](/en/articles/affiliate-marketing-guide/) is another open door: a link in your bio or Stories earns a commission on each sale it brings in. Then there's selling a skill such as design, photography or managing other people's accounts, where your Instagram becomes a portfolio that clients see before they ever message you.

> **Did you know?** Many brands care more about **engagement rate** than follower count. An account with 5,000 real, engaged followers in one city can be worth more to a local shop than one with 100,000 fake followers.

## Growing your account and your earnings

Nobody can give you a fixed figure for what creators earn. It depends on your niche, your audience, how engaged they are and how many deals you land. For most people it starts small and grows slowly, as long as they keep at it. What helps is mostly a set of plain habits.

Pick a clear niche, such as cooking, tech, local travel or education. A focused audience is easier to sell to: a kitchenware brand knows exactly why it wants to work with a cooking account. Put your effort into Reels, since they reach the most people who don't follow you yet. Post on a regular schedule and check your insights to learn which posts worked, rather than guessing.

Once paid posts start, label them with "Paid partnership". Instagram's policies require it, and so do the laws of many countries. It's also sensible not to depend on a single app, so share your work on [TikTok](/en/articles/tiktok-monetization/) and [Facebook](/en/articles/facebook-monetization/) as well. ## Scams to watch out for

Buying followers or engagement does the most damage. The bigger number looks good, but it breaks the rules, can cost you monetization for good, and never brings a real customer, because fake followers don't buy anything.

Be just as wary of anyone who offers to "activate monetization" for a fee. Nobody can do that for you; it only happens inside the app, through the steps above. The same goes for messages announcing that you've won a verification badge. They're usually attempts to steal your account, so don't click their links and never share your login code, however official the message looks.
