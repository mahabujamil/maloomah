Every autumn the same argument breaks out in cafés, playgrounds and group chats: who deserves the **Ballon d'Or** this year? Millions of fans wait for one name, the player crowned the best in the world. Yet plenty of those fans couldn't say exactly what the Ballon d'Or is, who actually decides it, or how it became the individual prize every footballer dreams of winning.

## What is the Ballon d'Or?

The name is French for "Golden Ball", and the award is exactly what it sounds like: **an annual prize for the best football player in the world over the past season**. The French magazine France Football created it in 1956, and the very first winner was England's Stanley Matthews.

For its first few decades it was a European affair, open only to European players. That changed in 1995, when players of any nationality at European clubs became eligible, and Liberia's George Weah promptly became the first non-European winner that same year. From 2007 onwards the door opened completely, and players from anywhere in the world could win it.

There was also a period of partnership. Between 2010 and 2015 the award merged with FIFA's player of the year prize under the name "FIFA Ballon d'Or", before going its own way again. And in 2020 there was no winner at all, because the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted the season.

## How is the Ballon d'Or decided?

Judging by social media you might think fans settle it, but they don't get a vote, and neither do the players. The decision belongs to **sports journalists**, and it happens in three stages:

1. Shortlist: a panel of football experts and journalists names 30 nominees.
2. Voting: one journalist from each of the top 100 nations in the FIFA men's ranking casts a ballot. The women's award uses the top 50 nations in the women's ranking.
3. Scoring: each voter ranks their top 10. First place earns 15 points, second 12, third 10, and so on down to 1 point for tenth. The player with the most points wins.

Because every ballot counts, a winner doesn't need unanimous support. What matters is appearing near the top of as many ballots as possible.

### What are voters looking for?

Three things carry weight. The first is individual performance: goals, assists and decisive moments in big games. The second is team success, meaning the trophies a player won with club and country. The third is sporting conduct, or fair play and sportsmanship. That is why a player with fewer goals can still finish ahead of a prolific scorer if he shaped the matches that mattered or lifted bigger trophies.

Since 2022 the award has covered the football season, August to July, instead of the calendar year. The practical benefit is that summer tournaments such as the World Cup now count towards the same season a player has just finished with his club.

## The other awards on the night

The ceremony has grown well beyond a single trophy. The Women's Ballon d'Or arrived in 2018, and Norway's Ada Hegerberg was its first winner. Alongside it sit the Kopa Trophy for the best young player under 21, the Gerd Müller Trophy for the season's top goalscorer, and the Yashin Trophy for the best goalkeeper, named after the legendary Soviet keeper Lev Yashin.

> **Did you know?** The only goalkeeper ever to win the Ballon d'Or is Lev Yashin, in 1963. That is why the goalkeeper award carries his name.

## Who has won the Ballon d'Or the most?

Argentina's **Lionel Messi** sits at the top of the all-time list, and nobody else is close:

| Player | Wins |
| --- | --- |
| Lionel Messi | 8 |
| Cristiano Ronaldo | 5 |
| Michel Platini | 3 |
| Johan Cruyff | 3 |
| Marco van Basten | 3 |

Below them, Alfredo Di Stéfano, Franz Beckenbauer, Kevin Keegan, Karl-Heinz Rummenigge and Brazil's Ronaldo have each won it twice. Set eight and five next to the three that the great names of earlier decades managed, and you can see how unusual the Messi and Ronaldo era was.

## Ballon d'Or 2026: the 70th edition

The 70th ceremony was held in London on 26 October 2026, the first time it had come to England, as a tribute to the first winner, Stanley Matthews. Fittingly, the trophy stayed in English hands: Bayern Munich striker Harry Kane won ahead of Kylian Mbappé and Lamine Yamal, becoming the first Englishman to win since Michael Owen in 2001. The women's award went to Spain's Alexia Putellas, her third after 2021 and 2022.

## Questions fans ask about the Ballon d'Or

### Is the Ballon d'Or trophy made of solid gold?

No. It is a gold-plated football sculpture rather than solid gold, and its prestige is worth far more than its material value.

### Does the Ballon d'Or come with prize money?

There is no official cash prize. Winning it does boost a player's status and commercial value, though, and many contracts include bonuses for winning it.

### What is the difference between the Ballon d'Or and FIFA's The Best award?

The Ballon d'Or is run by France Football and voted on by journalists alone. The Best FIFA Football Awards are run by FIFA, with votes from national team coaches and captains, journalists and fans.

### Has an Arab player ever won the Ballon d'Or?

Not yet, although several Arab stars have appeared on the nominee list in recent years.
