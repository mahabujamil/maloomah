Every morning the house wakes up to one voice before any other. Someone is calling the children, making breakfast and checking that school bags are packed. Then everyone heads off to school and work, and hardly anyone stops to ask when she got up. That small question is a good way into an **essay about mother**, because a strong essay doesn't stop at general words about love. It notices the things a mother does every day that nobody sees. Below is a full model essay with an introduction, body and conclusion, then a short version for younger pupils, and some tips for making the essay your own.

## Planning your essay about mother

Teachers reward essays that move clearly from one idea to the next, so sort your points before you write. A plan might look like this:

1. Introduction: the place a mother holds in everyone's life.
2. Her sacrifices, from pregnancy until her children are grown.
3. How Islam honours mothers.
4. The mother as a child's first school.
5. What we owe her, now and later.
6. Conclusion.

You don't need every point. For a short piece, keep the introduction, two or three body ideas and the conclusion.

## Introduction

A mother is the first face we see when we open our eyes on the world, the first voice that calms us and the first heart that loves us without asking for anything back. In almost every language, the word for "mother" is among the first a baby says, as if we learn to speak in order to call her. However old we get, however far we travel, part of us still longs for her hug and her prayers. So writing about a mother is never really about one person. It's about what giving means.

## The body: a mother's sacrifices

A mother's gift starts before her child is born. She carries the baby for about nine months, puts up with tiredness and pain, then feeds and watches over the child through long nights. When a child is ill, everyone else in the house may sleep, but she stays awake. When the baby cries at midnight, she is the first one up.

As the child grows and starts school, the sacrifices change shape but never stop. She goes over homework, celebrates good marks as if they were her own and feels her children's disappointments more sharply than they do. Many mothers also work outside the home and come back to a second shift inside it: cooking, cleaning, checking homework and putting off their own rest until everyone else is asleep.

She doesn't ask to be thanked for any of it. That is what makes a mother's giving different. It can't be counted or paid back with money; it can only be answered with kindness and loyalty.

## How Islam honours mothers

Islam places kindness to parents directly after the worship of God. The Quran says in Surat al-Isra (17:23):

> "And your Lord has decreed that you worship not except Him, and to parents, good treatment. Whether one or both of them reach old age [while] with you, say not to them [so much as], 'uff,' and do not repel them but speak to them a noble word." (Saheeh International)

It also mentions a mother's particular hardship in carrying and nursing her child, in Surat Luqman (31:14):

> "And We have enjoined upon man [care] for his parents. His mother carried him, [increasing her] in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years. Be grateful to Me and to your parents; to Me is the [final] destination." (Saheeh International)

A well-known hadith, reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim from Abu Hurayrah, tells of a man who asked the Prophet ﷺ who most deserved his good company. The Prophet answered "your mother" three times, and only on the fourth question said "your father". Naming the mother three times doesn't lessen the father's rights. It shows how great the mother's are.

## A child's first school

At home a child learns their first words, their first table manners and their first lessons in honesty and kindness, and most of those lessons come from their mother long before any classroom. The Egyptian poet **Hafez Ibrahim** put it in a famous line, which roughly translates as:

> A mother is a school: prepare her well, and you prepare a nation of noble character.

His point is that an educated, caring mother raises good people, and in a few years those people are the whole of society. Investing in girls' education and in mothers' wellbeing builds a country from its foundations. Many families know mothers who raised their children alone, and saw those children grow into doctors, engineers and teachers on the strength of her patience.

## What we owe our mothers

Honouring a mother isn't a once-a-year event, even though many Arab countries mark Mother's Day on 21 March. Real respect is a set of small habits repeated daily:

- Listen to her, speak to her politely and never raise your voice at her.
- Help around the house without being asked: tidy your room, set the table.
- Work hard at school, because your success is one of the things that makes her happiest.
- Thank her with kind words, a small gift or a prayer for her.
- Look after her when she is older, as she looked after you when you were small.

A student might feel too young to give anything back. But a glass of water when she comes home tired, or a sincere "How was your day?", can mean more to her than you'd guess.

## Conclusion

A mother is a blessing whose full value we often understand only when she is gone. So let's make the most of having her, and treat kindness to her as a daily habit rather than a special occasion. We'll never repay everything she has given, but we can keep trying, so that she goes to sleep each night content with us. When a mother is happy, the whole house feels right.

## Short essay about mother for younger pupils

My mother is the most precious person in my life. She wakes up before all of us, makes our breakfast and packs our bags, then waves us off with a smile at the door. When I come home from school, she asks about my day and helps me with my homework. When I'm ill, she sits beside me until I fall asleep.

God tells us to be kind to our parents, and the Prophet ﷺ told us to honour our mothers three times over. That's why I try to listen to my mother, help her at home and work hard at school to make her proud. I pray that God keeps her safe and helps me always be good to her.

## Tips for writing your own version

The model above is there to help you, but the essay a teacher remembers is the one with something of you in it. Add a real moment from your own home: a night she stayed up when you were sick, a dish she makes that you love, something she always says before an exam. One true detail brings an essay to life better than ten lines of general praise.

Link your paragraphs with clear transitions such as "then", "that's why" and "even so", and use a quotation only where it fits rather than in every paragraph. Read your work through at the end to catch spelling mistakes. ## Questions students ask about this essay

### How do I start an essay about my mother?

Open with a scene or a question instead of a dictionary-style definition. Describe the house in the morning while your mother gets everything ready, or ask the reader how many hours their mother really sleeps. That pulls a teacher in far better than "A mother is the woman who gave birth to us."

### How long should an essay about mother be?

Your teacher usually decides. In primary school, 8 to 12 lines is often enough. In middle school and above, a page to a page and a half with an introduction, body and conclusion is typical.

### Do I need to include a Quran verse or hadith?

It isn't required, but quotations strengthen the essay if they're accurate. Copy a verse exactly and name the surah, and only quote a hadith if you're sure of its wording.
