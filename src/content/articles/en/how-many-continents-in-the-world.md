**How many continents are there in the world?** It's one of the most common questions in geography lessons. The best-known answer, and the one used in most school curricula, is **seven continents**: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia. But did you know that some countries teach that there are six, or even five? In this lesson we explain what a continent is, list the seven continents from largest to smallest, explain the difference between the Old World and the New World, and finish with a revision table and frequently asked questions.

## What is a continent?

A **continent** is a very large mass of land, usually surrounded by oceans and seas. There is no single scientific definition of how big a continent must be or exactly where its borders lie, so the way we divide the land into continents depends on physical geography, but also on history and culture.

Land covers only about **29%** of the Earth's surface. Together, the seven continents cover roughly **149 million square kilometres**. The rest, about 71% of the planet's surface, is covered by water.

## How many continents are there, and what are their names?

In the most widely used model, there are **7 continents**. Here they are from largest to smallest. The figures are approximate, because sources draw the borders slightly differently:

## A quick profile of each continent

1. **Asia:** the largest continent and the most populated. About 6 in every 10 people on Earth live there. It has the highest peak on the planet, Mount Everest, and includes huge countries such as China and India.
2. **Africa:** second in both area and population, and home to the most countries: **54**. The Nile flows through it, and it contains the Sahara, the largest hot desert in the world.
3. **North America:** stretches from the Arctic Ocean down to Central America and the Caribbean islands, and includes Canada, the United States and Mexico. Greenland, the world's largest island, is geographically part of it.
4. **South America:** home to the Amazon, the river that carries more water than any other, the Amazon rainforest, and the Andes, the longest mountain range on land.
5. **Antarctica:** the coldest, driest and windiest continent. Ice covers about 98% of it. No one lives there permanently, only scientists at research stations, and the Antarctic Treaty, signed in 1959, keeps it for peaceful and scientific use.
6. **Europe:** one of the smallest continents by area but densely populated. In the east, the Ural Mountains and the Ural River separate it from Asia, which is why some geographers treat the two as a single continent called **Eurasia**.
7. **Australia:** the smallest continent, often called **Oceania** when the Pacific islands, such as New Zealand and Papua New Guinea, are included. Australia is the only country in the world that occupies an entire continent.

> **Did you know?** Scientists believe that about 300 million years ago the continents were joined in one giant landmass called **Pangaea**. It began to break apart about 200 million years ago, and the continents are still moving today, very slowly: a few centimetres a year, roughly as fast as your fingernails grow!

## Why the answer varies: 7, 6 or 5 continents?

There's no single worldwide agreement on the number of continents. The answer depends on the country and the curriculum:

| Model | Number | What changes? | Where it's mostly used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seven-continent model | 7 | Every continent counted separately | Most Arab curricula, English-speaking countries and China |
| Six-continent model | 6 | North and South America combined as "America" | Much of Latin America and parts of Europe |
| Six-continent model (Eurasia) | 6 | Europe and Asia combined as "Eurasia" | Russia and parts of Eastern Europe |
| Inhabited-continents model | 5 | The Americas combined, Antarctica left out as uninhabited | Reflected in the five Olympic rings |

**The answer for students:** if your teacher asks "How many continents are there in the world?", the expected answer in most curricula is **seven**, but it's good to know that other divisions exist.

## Old World and New World continents

A common exam question is: **which continents make up the Old World?** The answer:

- **The Old World has three continents:** Asia, Africa and Europe. They're called that because their peoples knew about each other for thousands of years, and the oldest civilisations grew there, such as [ancient Egypt](/en/articles/ancient-egyptian-civilization/), Mesopotamia and China.
- **The New World:** North America and South America. Europe learned of them after Christopher Columbus's voyage in 1492, although Indigenous peoples had lived there for thousands of years before.
- **Australia** is sometimes called the "Newest World", because Europeans reached it later, and **Antarctica** was the last continent to be discovered: it was first sighted in the 19th century.

## Continents and oceans: how water separates the land

**Five oceans** separate the continents. From largest to smallest they are the Pacific, the Atlantic, the Indian Ocean, the Southern Ocean and the Arctic Ocean. The Pacific alone is larger than all the land on Earth put together. Some continents meet through narrow strips of land, such as Asia and Africa at the Sinai Peninsula, and the two Americas at the Isthmus of Panama.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many continents are there, and what are they?

In the most common model there are seven: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia (Oceania).

### What are the largest and smallest continents?

The largest is **Asia**, at about 44.6 million km², and it's also the most populated. The smallest is **Australia**, at about 7.7 million km².

### Which continent has no permanent population?

**Antarctica.** Scientists and researchers live there for limited periods at research stations, and their numbers change between summer and winter.

### Which continents are Arab countries in?

Arab countries are in two continents: **Asia** (for example Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Iraq and Syria) and **Africa** (for example Egypt, Morocco, Algeria and Sudan). Egypt is one of the few countries that spans two continents, because the Sinai Peninsula is in Asia.

### Which continent has the most countries?

**Africa**, with 54 countries, followed by Asia and then Europe.

## Key takeaway

**There are seven continents in the world** in the model used by most curricula: Asia (the largest), Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe and Australia (the smallest). The Old World is made up of three continents: Asia, Africa and Europe. Learn the table above, and remember that some countries count six or five continents depending on how they divide the land.
