First lesson starts in ten minutes and the whole school is lined up in the yard. Those ten minutes are a good illustration of today's topic: fill them with a long speech and the students start shuffling; use them well and everyone walks to class with one clear idea. That is why this **school radio about time and its importance** is written to be short. It has an opening, Surah Al-Asr, an authentic hadith, a talk on time management, a "Did you know?" segment, a story, a quiz and a closing.

## Getting ready: who reads what

An assembly about time can't run late, so the planning matters more than usual. Agree on the order the day before and have each student standing near the microphone before their turn. Keep every segment to a minute or two. The presenter opens, closes and runs the quiz. A student who recites well reads Surah Al-Asr, another reads the hadith, a confident speaker gives the morning talk, two students share the facts, and one tells the story.

If the morning is tight, drop the story or the quiz. Surah Al-Asr and the morning talk are the heart of it.

## Opening

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
> Good morning, school, and good morning to hours that never come back.
> Our respected principal, dear teachers, fellow students, peace be upon you.
> Each of us has exactly twenty-four hours today. The rich don't get more and the poor don't get less. What separates people is not how many hours they have but what they do with them. So our assembly today is about time: why it matters and how to organise it.
> We begin with a short surah in which God swears by time itself, recited by ……

## Surah Al-Asr

The text is taken from quran.com. It has three short verses, and most students already know it by heart.

> ﴿وَالْعَصْرِ ۝ إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ﴾
> "By time, indeed, mankind is in loss, except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience." [Surah Al-Asr, 103:1-3, Saheeh International]

A second verse about day and night works well after it:

> ﴿وَهُوَ الَّذِي جَعَلَ اللَّيْلَ وَالنَّهَارَ خِلْفَةً لِّمَنْ أَرَادَ أَن يَذَّكَّرَ أَوْ أَرَادَ شُكُورًا﴾
> "And it is He who has made the night and the day in succession for whoever desires to remember or desires gratitude." [Surah Al-Furqan, 25:62]

The presenter can add one sentence afterwards: in the best-known explanation, *al-asr* here means time itself, so the whole surah is about how a person gains or loses through the way they spend their time.

## The hadith

> Ibn Abbas reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There are two blessings which many people lose out on: health and free time." [Sahih al-Bukhari, 6412]

The Arabic word translated as "lose out" describes someone cheated in a trade, who sells something valuable for almost nothing. Plenty of people have good health and spare hours at the same time, and let both slip by.

## Morning talk on time management

> Dear friends,
> How many times have we said "I'll study in a minute" and then found ourselves at midnight with the book still closed? Usually the problem isn't a lack of time. Time leaks away in small pieces: one short video that leads to another, a chat that never ends, waiting to be in the mood.
> Organising your time doesn't need a fancy app. Three habits are enough to start. Each evening, write down the three most important things for tomorrow, and begin with the hardest. Break studying into short sessions of about half an hour with a short break between them, because the mind focuses better when it knows rest is close. And keep your phone off the study desk; just having it in sight pulls your attention away.
> Sleep counts too. Getting enough of it is part of using time well, not a waste of it. Stay up until dawn and you lose the whole next morning in class.
> Time is like water in your hand: it slips away unless you hold it carefully. So here is today's motto: "Start now, not in a minute."

## Did you know?

Two students take turns:

- Did you know that the ancient Egyptians measured time with sundials and water clocks thousands of years ago?
- Did you know that Caliph Harun al-Rashid sent the emperor Charlemagne a water clock in 807 CE, and it amazed his court?
- Did you know that the engineer al-Jazari described remarkable water clocks in a book he completed in 1206, the most famous being the "elephant clock"?
- Did you know that a year is about a quarter of a day longer than 365 days, which is why February gets an extra day roughly every four years?
- Did you know that the second is now set by atomic clocks, some of the most precise instruments people have ever built?

> **Did you know?** Since 1967 the second has not been defined as a fraction of a day. Instead it is the time taken by 9,192,631,770 oscillations of the radiation from a caesium atom.

More short facts like these are on the [general knowledge "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/general/).

## A short story: Salma's notebook

This story was written for the assembly. Read it slowly:

> Salma put everything off. Homework was "after my show", revision was "on Thursday", packing her bag was "in the morning". The night before her science test she discovered three lessons she had never opened. She stayed up late, exhausted, and sat the test with her eyes half closed.
> The next week her grandmother gave her a small notebook. "Every evening, write down just three things you want to get done tomorrow," she said, "and tick each one when it's finished." Salma laughed at how simple it was, but she tried it.
> A month later the notebook was full of ticks, and Salma had time for drawing, which she loved, because she no longer spent her evenings worrying about unfinished work.
> The lesson: time is never enough for those who delay, and always enough for those who begin.

## Quiz for the line

The presenter asks, and the first raised hand answers:

1. In which surah does God swear by time? (Surah Al-Asr)
2. Which two blessings do many people lose out on, according to the hadith? (Health and free time)
3. What is the name of al-Jazari's most famous clock? (The elephant clock)
4. Roughly how often does February get an extra day? (Every four years)
5. What did Salma do to stop putting things off? (She wrote three tasks for the next day every evening)

## Closing

> That brings our assembly to an end. It took only a few minutes of your day, and we hope they were useful ones. Pick one small habit and start it today rather than tomorrow.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Questions teachers ask about this assembly

### Does it work for primary school?

Yes, with a few cuts. Use only Surah Al-Asr, since younger children usually know it, shorten the morning talk to the first habit, and keep the story and quiz as they are. Those are the parts small children enjoy most.

### How can I link it to studying?

After the assembly, ask students to draw up a study plan for one week. Our guide to [effective study methods](/en/articles/effective-study-methods/) has practical ideas, and [how to stop procrastinating](/en/articles/how-to-stop-procrastinating/) explains why we delay and what helps.

### Is there a good saying to end on?

A well-known Arabic saying fits nicely: "Time is like a sword: if you don't cut it, it cuts you." Most students have heard it, and it makes a strong last line before the closing greeting.
