Ask a class to name the longest river in the world and nearly every hand will go up with the same answer: **the Nile**. Most reference books agree. Yet some scientists make a serious case that the Amazon is longer, and the reason they disagree is more interesting than either number, because it shows how hard it is to measure a river at all.

## The longest river in the world: the Nile

Most geographical references give the Nile a length of about 6,650 km. It flows north out of the heart of Africa, which surprises students who assume rivers run "down" the map, and empties into the Mediterranean Sea through Egypt's delta.

The Nile is really two rivers that join. The White Nile rises in the Lake Victoria region of central Africa. The Blue Nile rises at Lake Tana in the Ethiopian highlands. They meet at Khartoum, the capital of Sudan, and from there continue as a single river.

Eleven countries share the Nile basin, but they don't contribute equally. Most of the water that reaches Egypt comes from the Ethiopian highlands.

## Nile vs Amazon: which is longer?

By traditional estimates the Amazon in South America is about 6,400 km long, a little short of the Nile. Some studies, though, have measured it as longer than the Nile. Nobody made an arithmetic mistake; the answer depends on two decisions.

First, where does the river begin? A big river doesn't spring from one tap. It gathers water from many tributaries, and if you pick the most distant one as the source, the river gets longer.

Second, where does it end? The mouths of great rivers are wide and split into channels, so the point where river becomes sea is hard to pin down.

That is why the Nile still holds the title in most references, while the debate among scientists is genuine. One thing is not in dispute: **the Amazon is the largest river by volume**. It carries about one-fifth of all the river water that flows into the world's oceans. The Nile may win on length, but on sheer amount of water the Amazon has no rival.

Notice that half of the list is in Asia, the largest continent.

> **Did you know?** The Danube is the longest river in the European Union, and it flows through 10 countries, more than any other river in the world.

## The longest river on each continent

Africa's longest river is the Nile, at about 6,650 km, and South America's is the Amazon, at about 6,400 km. In Asia the record belongs to the Yangtze in China, at about 6,300 km, which also makes it the world's third-longest river.

North America is a slightly odd case. Its length is usually given for the Mississippi–Missouri system as a whole, since the two rivers join and work as one. On its own, the Missouri is actually a bit longer than the Mississippi that usually gets the credit.

Europe's longest river is the **Volga** in Russia, at about 3,500 km. Don't mix it up with the Danube, which is only the longest within the EU. In Australia, the Murray leads at about 2,500 km.

## Questions students ask about rivers

### Is the Nile also the longest river in the Arab world?

Yes. Flowing through Sudan and Egypt, the Nile is the longest river in Africa and in the Arab world too.

### Which is the longest river in the Arab world after the Nile?

The Euphrates, about 2,800 km long. It rises in Turkey and flows through Syria and Iraq.

### What should I write in a quiz?

Write the Nile. It's the answer most references and textbooks accept. If you have room to explain, add that some scientists consider the Amazon longer, and that the Amazon is without question the largest by volume.
