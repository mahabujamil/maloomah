We press a switch and the light comes on, we charge our phones and run the fridge without a second thought. But who invented electricity in the first place? The surprising answer: **nobody**! Electricity is a natural phenomenon, found in lightning and even in our own bodies. What scientists did was **discover, understand and harness** it, step by step, over more than two thousand years.

## The beginning: a piece of amber in ancient Greece

Around **600 BCE**, the Greek philosopher **Thales of Miletus** noticed that rubbing a piece of **amber** (fossilised tree resin) with wool made it attract feathers and bits of straw. It was the first recorded observation of what we now call **static electricity**.

> **Did you know?** The word "electricity" comes from the Greek "elektron", meaning amber. Interestingly, the Arabic word for electricity, **"kahraba"**, also comes from the Persian name for amber, "kahruba", meaning "straw-attractor"!

## William Gilbert: the man who named it

In **1600**, English physician **William Gilbert** published a book on magnetism, studying many materials that behave like amber and using the Latin word "electricus" to describe them. That's where the word for electricity in European languages comes from.

## Benjamin Franklin and the kite

In **1752**, American **Benjamin Franklin** carried out his famous kite experiment during a thunderstorm to show that **lightning is electricity**. His research led to the **lightning rod**, which still protects buildings today.

## Alessandro Volta: the first battery

In **1800**, Italian scientist **Alessandro Volta** built the "voltaic pile", **the first battery** to produce a steady electric current. The unit of electrical voltage, the **volt**, is named after him.

## Michael Faraday: the secret of generators

In **1831**, British scientist **Michael Faraday** discovered **electromagnetic induction**: moving a magnet inside a coil of wire produces an electric current. This discovery is the basis of the **generators** that produce electricity in power stations worldwide, and of **electric motors** too.

## Thomas Edison: electricity comes home

In **1879**, American inventor **Thomas Edison** developed a practical, long-lasting incandescent light bulb, while Britain's **Joseph Swan** was working on a similar invention at the same time. In **1882**, Edison opened the Pearl Street Station in New York, one of the first commercial power stations to bring electricity to homes and shops.

## Nikola Tesla: alternating current

Edison used **direct current (DC)**, but engineer **Nikola Tesla** developed **alternating current (AC)** systems and motors, working with businessman **George Westinghouse**. After a famous rivalry known as the "War of the Currents", AC won because it's easier to transmit over long distances, and it's what power grids still use today.

## Other scientists worth remembering

| Scientist | Contribution |

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is the real inventor of electricity?

There isn't one, because electricity is a natural phenomenon. But the key names are: Thales first observed it, Volta built the first battery, Faraday opened the way to generating it, and Edison and Tesla brought it into everyday life.

### What about the "Baghdad Battery"?

It's a set of ancient clay jars found near Baghdad, which some people think might have produced a weak electric current. But there is no conclusive evidence, and most researchers believe the jars had other uses.

### When did electricity reach people's homes?

It began spreading in big cities in the late 19th century, then took many decades to reach most parts of the world.

## Key takeaway

Electricity wasn't invented by one person. It's a shared story of discovery that began with a piece of amber more than 2,600 years ago, passed through Franklin, Volta and Faraday, and reached our homes thanks to Edison and Tesla. Every time you flip a light switch, remember the centuries of scientific curiosity behind it.
