Every year on **10 November** the world marks **World Science Day for Peace and Development**. It's the perfect week for a **school assembly on science and scientists**, the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio", reminding students that science lights the way and that every great inventor was once a pupil sitting in a classroom just like them. Below is a complete, ready-to-read script: an introduction, Quran and hadith segments, a morning talk, "Did you know?" facts about scientists, a short story, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing the parts.

## Running order for a school assembly on science

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Teachers can drop or add a segment to fit the time.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | One student |
| 4 | Morning talk on science | 2 min | One student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" about scientists | 2 min | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Short story | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 7 | Sayings about knowledge | 1 min | Three students |
| 8 | Assembly quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

## Introduction to the science assembly

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who taught by the pen, taught people what they did not know.
> Good morning to curious minds that ask, search and discover.
> Dear head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning.
> On 10 November the world celebrates World Science Day for Peace and Development. Today we celebrate the knowledge that brought us to this school, opened the doors of books and laboratories for us, and made our lives easier and better.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by our classmate …

## Quran segment about knowledge

A student recites one or more of these verses. The Arabic text is taken from quran.com, and the English meanings below follow the Saheeh International translation. The student should ideally recite from the Mushaf with proper tajweed:

> "Recite in the name of your Lord who created, created man from a clinging substance. Recite, and your Lord is the most Generous, who taught by the pen, taught man that which he knew not." [Surah Al-Alaq, verses 1–5]

> "…Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, by degrees. And Allah is Aware of what you do." [Surah Al-Mujadila, from verse 11]

> "…and say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'" [Surah Taha, from verse 114]

**Host after the recitation:** God has spoken the truth. Next, a hadith presented by …

## Hadith segment about seeking knowledge

> Abu Hurairah (may God be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said: "…Whoever follows a path in search of knowledge, God will make easy for him, through it, a path to Paradise…" [Part of a hadith narrated by Muslim (2699)]

**The meaning in brief:** every step a student takes to seek useful knowledge, from attending a lesson to reading a good book, carries great reward, and the hadith gives good news that God eases the way to Paradise for whoever takes this path.

## Morning talk on science

> Dear classmates,
> Have you ever wondered what life was like before electricity, telephones and modern medicine? Nights were dark, a letter took weeks to arrive, and many illnesses had no cure. Then science came: it lit our homes, brought distant places closer and saved millions of lives.
> Science is not just memorising lessons. It is a way of thinking: asking "why?" and "how?", trying, making mistakes and trying again, and checking a claim before we believe it.
> Arab and Muslim scholars played a big part in this journey. Al-Khwarizmi laid the foundations of algebra, Ibn al-Haytham studied light and how we see, and Ibn Sina wrote a medical encyclopaedia that was taught for centuries.
> So let our motto today be: "I ask, I search, I learn." The scientist of tomorrow may be one of us, standing in this line right now.

## "Did you know?" facts about science and scientists

Two students can read these in turns:

- **Did you know** that UNESCO's General Conference proclaimed 10 November as World Science Day for Peace and Development in 2001, and that it was first celebrated in 2002?
- **Did you know** that the word "algebra" comes from the book of the Muslim scholar Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, and that the word "algorithm" comes from his name?
- **Did you know** that Ibn al-Haytham explained that we see objects because light reflects off them and enters the eye, not because the eye sends out rays as some ancient thinkers believed?
- **Did you know** that Marie Curie was the first person to win two Nobel Prizes, in physics in 1903 and in chemistry in 1911?
- **Did you know** that Thomas Edison was granted more than 1,000 patents in the United States?
- **Did you know** that the periodic table now contains 118 known elements?

> **Did you know?** Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin by chance in 1928, when he noticed that a mould growing on a dish in his lab had stopped the bacteria around it from growing. Careful observation is half of every discovery!

## Short story about science for the assembly

> **Story: Layla's question**
> Layla asked lots of questions. Why is the sky blue? Why does ice float? Why do we see lightning before we hear thunder? Some classmates laughed and said, "Your questions never end!"
> One day the science teacher asked everyone to bring a question that puzzled them. Layla stood up and asked, "Why does ice float when it's made of water?" The teacher smiled, brought a glass of water and an ice cube, and explained that water expands when it freezes, so ice is lighter than the same volume of liquid water, and that is why it floats.
> Then the teacher said, "Every great scientist started with a question like Layla's." From that day on, the students raced to ask questions instead of laughing at them.
> **The lesson:** asking questions is nothing to be shy about. It is the first step on the road to knowledge.

## Sayings about knowledge

Each student reads one line clearly:

1. Knowledge is light, and ignorance is darkness.
2. Whoever works hard will succeed; whoever plants will harvest.
3. Reading feeds the mind, just as food feeds the body.
4. A book is the best companion, and knowledge the best friend.
5. Every great scientist was once a student who asked questions.

## Closing of the science assembly

> As we close, let's remember that science doesn't live only in books. It lives in minds that read, ask and experiment.
> Let's take care of our books, respect our teachers, and make every school day a new step on the road to knowledge.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is World Science Day?

It falls on **10 November** every year, and its full name is World Science Day for Peace and Development. UNESCO proclaimed it in 2001, and it was first celebrated worldwide in 2002.

### Is this assembly suitable for primary school?

Yes. The story, the short sayings and the quiz suit young pupils. For the youngest classes, shorten the morning talk and keep just three facts in the "Did you know?" segment.

### What are the essential segments of a school assembly?

The introduction, Quran recitation, a hadith, the morning talk, a "Did you know?" segment and the closing. A story, a quiz and sayings are extras that make the assembly livelier.

### How can I link the assembly to Arab and Muslim scientists?

Choose one scholar and ask a student to give a one-minute profile. Our articles on [Ibn al-Haytham](/en/articles/ibn-al-haytham/) and [al-Khwarizmi](/en/articles/who-is-al-khwarizmi/) are a good place to start.

## Key takeaway

A **school assembly on science and scientists** is a chance to help students love asking, researching and reading. Use the ready-made segments above as they are or adapt them to your school, and present them in the week of **10 November** to mark World Science Day for Peace and Development.
