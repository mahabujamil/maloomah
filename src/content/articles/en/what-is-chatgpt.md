Type "explain Newton's second law using football" into a chat box and a clear explanation appears a few seconds later. That's the everyday experience of millions of students, office workers and small-business owners. So what is ChatGPT, how does it seem to understand you, and how do you use it well without being caught out by its mistakes?

## What is ChatGPT?

ChatGPT is **an AI chatbot made by the US company OpenAI**, released to the public on 30 November 2022. You write a question or request in ordinary language and it answers with text that reads much like a person wrote it.

It can explain a topic, summarise a document, translate, draft emails and essays, brainstorm and help with computer code, and it works in English, Arabic and many other languages.

The name has two halves. "Chat" is the obvious one. GPT stands for *Generative Pre-trained Transformer*, which is the type of AI model underneath.

## How does ChatGPT work?

ChatGPT runs on a **large language model (LLM)**, and the basic idea is less mysterious than the name suggests.

First, the model was trained on an enormous amount of text, from which it picked up the patterns of language: which words tend to follow which, and how ideas link together. Then, when you send a message, it predicts the most fitting next word (strictly, the next piece of a word), adds it, predicts the next one, and keeps going until it has a complete answer. It's a giant, very well-practised version of finishing someone's sentence. Finally, its answers were refined using feedback from human reviewers who rated responses, which pushed it towards replies that are more helpful and safer.

That explains something important. ChatGPT doesn't think the way a person does and has no awareness; it produces text from learned patterns. For the bigger picture, read [What is artificial intelligence and how does it work?](/en/articles/what-is-artificial-intelligence/)

> **Did you know?** According to estimates published by the bank UBS in early 2023, ChatGPT reached around 100 million monthly users roughly two months after launch, which UBS called the fastest growth of any consumer app in history.

## How to use ChatGPT, step by step

Getting started takes a couple of minutes:

1. Go to chatgpt.com or download the official app from Google Play or the App Store. Check that the publisher is OpenAI, because copycat apps are common.
2. Sign up with an email address or a Google, Microsoft or Apple account.
3. Type your question in the message box and press send.
4. Ask follow-up questions; it remembers the context within the same chat.

### Is ChatGPT free?

Yes. There's a free version open to everyone, plus optional paid plans with extra features and higher usage limits. Plan details and prices change from time to time, so check the official site for what's on offer now.

## How to write better prompts

The answer you get depends heavily on what you ask. "Write about water" produces something generic. "Explain the water cycle to a 10-year-old in 5 bullet points" produces something you can actually use, because the model only knows who you are and what you need from what you type.

It also helps to give it a role and a tone ("act as a patient maths teacher") and to say what format you want, whether that's a table, a list or a short paragraph. And treat the first reply as a draft. Follow up with "make it shorter", "give me an example" or "explain it more simply" until it fits.

## ChatGPT's limits and mistakes

The biggest thing to know is that **it can be confidently wrong**. It sometimes states false facts or cites references that don't exist, a problem usually called "hallucination". Given how it works, that isn't surprising: it predicts what sounds plausible, not what has been checked. So verify anything important against a reliable source such as your textbook or an official website.

It isn't a doctor or a lawyer either, so don't lean on it for serious medical, legal or financial decisions. Guard your privacy too, and never paste in passwords, personal details or confidential work material.

Then there's academic honesty. Use it to understand and revise, not to copy homework, and follow your school's or university's rules. An assignment someone else wrote won't teach you anything, and it won't be sitting next to you in the exam.

## Other questions about ChatGPT

### Who owns ChatGPT?

It was built by OpenAI, an American AI research company based in San Francisco and founded in 2015.

### Does ChatGPT understand Arabic and other languages?

Yes. It understands and replies in many languages, including Arabic, though its accuracy on specialised topics can be higher in English.

### Is ChatGPT connected to the internet?

The model itself relies on what it learned in training, but some versions can search the web to fetch newer information and show their sources.
