The **King Fahd Causeway** regularly trends in Saudi search results, especially during holidays when traffic builds up. It's no ordinary road. It is one of the longest sea crossings in the region and the only land route connecting the island kingdom of Bahrain to the mainland. Here's what to know about its length, history, layout and how to cross it.

## What is the King Fahd Causeway?

The King Fahd Causeway is a sea road linking **Al Khobar** in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province with **Al Jasra** in Bahrain, across the shallow waters of the Arabian Gulf. It's also known as the Bahrain Causeway. Before it opened, Bahrain could only be reached by sea or air, so the causeway transformed travel, trade and tourism between the two countries.

## How long is the King Fahd Causeway?

The causeway is **about 25 km (16 miles)** long. It isn't a single bridge but a chain of **bridges and embankments** (roads built on land reclaimed from the sea):

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Total length | about 25 km |
| Structure | five bridges and seven embankments, according to engineering sources |
| Saudi end | Al Khobar |
| Bahraini end | Al Jasra |
| Official opening | 26 November 1986 |
| Operator | King Fahd Causeway Authority |

## History: when did the King Fahd Causeway open?

The idea of a land link was discussed for decades. The project agreement was signed in 1981 and the foundation stone was laid in November 1982. After about four years of work, the causeway was officially inaugurated on **26 November 1986** by King Fahd bin Abdulaziz and Bahrain's then ruler, Sheikh Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa, and named after King Fahd. Sources put its cost at roughly 3 billion Saudi riyals, funded by Saudi Arabia.

Building in open sea was a serious engineering challenge, requiring land reclamation and large numbers of precast concrete supports.

> **Did you know?** Halfway along the King Fahd Causeway sits an artificial island, often called "Passport Island", where both countries carry out passport and customs checks. It also has a mosque, restaurants and tall towers you can see from far away.

## Passport Island: what's in the middle?

Travellers don't clear formalities at either end. They stop at the artificial island near the middle, where Saudi and Bahraini passport and customs posts sit side by side, along with insurance offices, service stations and rest areas. Procedures have been upgraded over the years to ease congestion, with extra lanes and more online services.

## Practical tips before crossing

- **Check the traffic first:** many people search for live causeway traffic before setting off. The King Fahd Causeway Authority's official channels post updates, and queues usually peak at weekends and on public holidays.
- **Insurance:** your car needs valid insurance covering the other country. It can usually be bought online or at insurance offices on the causeway, but check the current rules before you travel.
- **Documents:** a passport or GCC national ID for Gulf citizens, a visa if you need one, your driving licence and vehicle registration. If the car isn't registered to you, you'll need an authorisation letter.
- **Toll:** vehicles pay a crossing fee. The amount and payment methods can change, so check the official website.
- **Rules:** stick to the speed limit and only stop in designated areas.

## The King Hamad Causeway: a second link

Because of growing pressure on the King Fahd Causeway, Saudi Arabia and Bahrain have announced a **second, parallel causeway**, known as the King Hamad Causeway, planned to include a railway for passengers and freight alongside the road. Its completion dates depend on construction phases announced by the authorities.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is the King Fahd Causeway?
About 25 km, from Al Khobar in Saudi Arabia to Al Jasra in Bahrain.

### When did the King Fahd Causeway open?
It was officially opened on 26 November 1986, about four years after the foundation stone was laid.

### Do I need insurance to drive across the King Fahd Causeway?
Yes. Your car needs valid insurance that covers the other country, which you can usually buy online or on the causeway.

### How long does it take to cross the King Fahd Causeway?
The drive itself takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes, but total time depends on queues at the border posts and can be much longer during holidays.

### Where are passport checks on the King Fahd Causeway?
On the artificial island near the middle, where both countries' border posts are side by side.

## Key takeaway

The **King Fahd Causeway** is a roughly 25 km sea road, opened in 1986, linking Al Khobar in Saudi Arabia with Al Jasra in Bahrain, with border checks on an island halfway across. Before you travel, check traffic, insurance and fees on official channels, and pick a quieter time if you can.
