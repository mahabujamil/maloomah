An **essay on water** is one of the most common writing tasks at school, because water is the secret of life: no person, animal or plant can do without it. Below is a complete model essay with a clear structure: an introduction, a body that covers why water matters, where it comes from, what threatens it and how we can save it, and a conclusion. There's also a short version for primary pupils and some tips to help you write the essay in your own words.

## Essay outline

Before you write, put your ideas in order. Here's a suggested outline for grades 4 to 9:

1. **Introduction:** water is a great blessing and the basis of life on Earth.
2. **Why water matters to people:** for drinking, food, cleanliness and health.
3. **Why water matters to plants, animals, farming and industry.**
4. **Sources of water:** rain, rivers, lakes, groundwater and seas.
5. **Problems facing water:** scarce fresh water, pollution and waste.
6. **How can we save water?** At home, at school and in the community.
7. **Conclusion:** protecting water is everyone's job.

## Introduction

> Water is the most precious thing we have; it is the secret of life on planet Earth. A person can survive only a few days without it, and no seed sprouts and no tree turns green unless it has water to drink. Some of us think water will always be there, because it flows so easily from the tap. In fact, fresh drinking water is scarce, and millions of people around the world do not have enough of it. That is why we must value this blessing and learn how to protect it.

## Body: why water matters

> **Water and people:** water makes up roughly 60% of an adult's body weight. It carries nutrients to the body's cells, helps us digest food, controls body temperature through sweat and carries waste out of the body. We also need water to cook, to wash fruit and vegetables, and to keep ourselves, our homes and our streets clean, and cleanliness protects us from many diseases.
>
> **Water, plants and animals:** plants take in water from the soil through their roots and use it, together with sunlight and carbon dioxide, to make their food by photosynthesis. Without water, plants wilt and die, and animals lose their food. Every animal drinks water, and some, like fish, live in it all their lives.
>
> **Water in farming and industry:** farming uses more fresh water than anything else in the world. The wheat, rice, fruit and vegetables we eat all need irrigation. Factories need water for cooling, cleaning and making many products, and the power of water flowing through dams is used to generate electricity.

## Sources of water on Earth

> Water covers more than 70% of Earth's surface, but most of it is salt water in the seas and oceans, which we cannot drink directly. Fresh water is only about 3% of all the water on Earth, and most of that is frozen in ice sheets and glaciers or stored underground. We get fresh water from rain, from rivers and lakes, and from wells that reach groundwater. Some countries desalinate seawater to make drinking water, but this is expensive and uses a lot of energy.

| Source | Type | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rain | Fresh | Fills rivers, dams and groundwater |
| Rivers and lakes | Fresh | The Nile, the Jordan River |
| Groundwater | Usually fresh | Wells and springs |
| Seas and oceans | Salt | The Mediterranean Sea |

## Problems facing water

> Important as it is, water faces serious threats. The first is **water scarcity**: some regions get little rain while their populations keep growing, so demand for water keeps rising. The second is **water pollution**: when rubbish, factory waste and chemicals are dumped in rivers and seas, fish die and diseases spread. The third is **waste**: leaving the tap running while brushing your teeth, washing the car with a hose, or watering the garden at midday so that much of the water evaporates. [Climate change](/en/articles/climate-change/) makes these problems worse, since it is linked to longer droughts in some regions.

> **Did you know?** The United Nations marks **World Water Day** on 22 March every year to remind people how important fresh water is and why it must be managed wisely.

## How can we save water?

> Saving water is everyone's job, and we can start with simple steps:
> - Turn off the tap while brushing your teeth and soaping your hands.
> - Fix leaking taps and pipes straight away; small drips waste a lot of water over time.
> - Wash the car with a bucket instead of a hose.
> - Water plants early in the morning or in the evening, when less water evaporates.
> - Never throw rubbish into rivers, seas or onto beaches.
> - Run the washing machine and dishwasher only when they are full.
> - Help family members and classmates understand why saving water matters.

## Conclusion

> In conclusion, water is a priceless blessing and the basis of life for people, animals and plants. We have seen that fresh water is scarce and that pollution and waste threaten it. Let us all be guardians of this blessing: use it wisely, protect it from pollution and teach others its value, so that it reaches future generations just as it reached us. Every drop we save today is a life we protect tomorrow.

## A short essay on water for primary pupils

> Water is the secret of life. We drink water to live, and we use it to wash our hands and our food. Animals drink water, and plants need it to grow. We get water from rain, rivers and wells. We must take care of water: we should not leave the tap running, and we should not throw rubbish into water. Water is a great blessing, and we must be thankful for it and protect it.

## Tips for writing an excellent essay

| Tip | How to apply it |
| --- | --- |
| Start with a strong opening | Use a question or a surprising fact about water |
| Split the body into paragraphs | One paragraph per idea: importance, sources, problems, solutions |
| Use linking words | First, also, in addition, in conclusion |
| Add a scientific fact | Such as how much of the body is water, or World Water Day |
| End with a clear message | A call to save water |
| Check your work | Spelling and punctuation |

## Frequently asked questions

### How many paragraphs should an essay on water have?

Usually 5 to 7: an introduction, 3 to 5 body paragraphs and a conclusion. Younger pupils can write a single short paragraph.

### What are the main points of a "water is life" essay?

Why water matters to people, animals and plants; where water comes from; the problems it faces, such as scarcity, pollution and waste; and how we can save it.

### Can I copy this essay word for word?

It's better to use it as a model and then write your own essay in your own style, adding examples from where you live. That will raise your mark and improve your writing.

### When is World Water Day?

It's on 22 March every year, and the United Nations has marked it since 1993.

## Key takeaway

A good **essay on water** opens with an engaging introduction about water as the secret of life, explains why it matters, where it comes from and what threatens it, and ends with practical ways to save it. Use the outline and paragraphs above as a guide, then write your essay in your own words.
