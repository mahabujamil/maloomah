Millions of views pour into TikTok every minute, and some creators have become stars with serious incomes. But does TikTok actually pay for views? What are the requirements? Can you earn without going live? Here's the full truth.

## Does TikTok pay for views?

Yes, but **not in every country**. TikTok runs a programme called the **Creator Rewards Program**, which pays creators for qualified views on videos longer than one minute.

### Creator Rewards Program requirements

- Being **18 or older**.
- Having at least **10,000 followers**.
- Getting at least **100,000 views** in the last 30 days.
- Posting **original videos longer than one minute**.
- Living in a **country where the programme is available**; it's currently offered in a limited number of countries, so check inside the app.

> **Note:** TikTok's requirements and eligible countries change from time to time, so always check the Creator tools section in the app.

## Ways to make money on TikTok

### 1. LIVE gifts

During a LIVE stream, followers can send **virtual gifts** that turn into a balance you can withdraw. Going LIVE usually requires a minimum number of followers, and you must be 18 or older to receive gifts.

### 2. Brand deals and sponsorships

Once you have an engaged audience, brands pay you to feature their products. This is one of the **biggest income sources** for creators, and it's available almost everywhere.

### 3. Affiliate marketing

Show products in your videos and put the link in your bio to earn a commission on each sale. See our [affiliate marketing guide](/en/articles/affiliate-marketing-guide/).

### 4. Selling your own products and services

TikTok is a great shop window for your business: handmade products, home cooking, courses or design services. A catchy short video brings real customers. Need ideas? See our [home business ideas](/en/articles/home-business-ideas/).

### 5. Moving your audience to other platforms

Many creators use TikTok to attract an audience, then bring it to **YouTube**, where monetisation is available in more countries. Read our [guide to making money on YouTube](/en/articles/youtube-monetization/).

## Making money on TikTok without going LIVE

Yes, it's possible through **brand deals, affiliate marketing and selling products**, plus the Creator Rewards Program where available. LIVE is just one method among many.

## What about "getting paid to watch ads"?

Apps and ads keep promising money for **watching videos or ads** on TikTok. The truth:

- Some official versions have offered **tiny token rewards** in a few countries, which are nowhere near a real income.
- Many apps making these promises are **fake or scams** that harvest your data, charge fees or ask you to recruit friends.

**The rule:** real money on TikTok comes from **creating** content, not watching it.

## Tips to grow fast on TikTok

1. **Grab attention in the first 3 seconds**; they decide whether viewers keep watching.
2. **Pick a clear niche** people know you for.
3. **Use trends and trending sounds**, with your own twist.
4. **Post consistently**, so the algorithm understands your content.
5. **Add on-screen text**; many people watch without sound.
6. **Engage with comments** and reply with videos.
7. **Deliver value**: information, a laugh, an idea or inspiration.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does TikTok pay per million views?

There's no fixed figure; it varies by country, content type, watch time and how many views qualify.

### Do I need professional equipment?

No. A smartphone with good lighting and clear sound is enough to start; good content matters more than gear.

### Can I make money on TikTok with few followers?

Yes, through selling your own products or services and affiliate links, though bigger opportunities like brand deals grow with your audience.

## Key takeaway

Making money on TikTok is real, but it comes in several forms: the Creator Rewards Program where available, LIVE gifts, brand deals, affiliate marketing and selling products. Be wary of promises about earning from watching ads; the real money is in making content, not consuming it.
