If an electricity company technician has recently swapped the old meter outside your flat for a small box with a digital screen, you're not alone. Across the Middle East and much of the world, spinning-disc meters are being replaced, and the same questions follow every installation: what is a smart electricity meter, how does it work, and will it change the bill?

## What is a smart electricity meter?

**A smart electricity meter is an electronic device that measures how much electricity a home or shop uses and sends the readings automatically to the electricity company over a communications network.** Nobody has to visit to read it.

The old mechanical meter is a different machine. Inside it, a metal disc turns faster the more power you use, a set of dials counts the turns, and a meter reader comes round each month to write the numbers down. Here are the two side by side:

| Comparison | Old meter | Smart meter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it measures | Spinning disc and mechanical dials | Electronic circuits and a digital display |
| Reading | Manual, by a meter reader | Automatic and remote |
| Usage detail | One running total | Detailed usage at short intervals |
| Disconnect and reconnect | Manual, on site | Can be done remotely |

## How does a smart meter work?

Its job breaks down into three stages:

1. Measuring: it keeps measuring voltage and current and works out the energy used in **kilowatt-hours (kWh)**.
2. Recording: it stores consumption at regular intervals, so it knows *when* you used electricity, not just how much.
3. Sending: it passes the data to the company's systems by radio, mobile networks or even the power lines themselves. The whole setup is called advanced metering infrastructure (AMI).

The second stage is what earns the word "smart". An old meter is like a notebook with only the grand total written in it. A smart meter can see, for example, that a household's use climbs in the evening when everyone is home and the air conditioner and washing machine are running at once.

The connection is also **two-way**. Data doesn't only flow out of the meter; the company can send commands back, such as a tariff update or an instruction to switch the supply off and on, without sending anyone round.

## Smart meters in the Arab world

In **April 2021** the Saudi Electricity Company announced that it had installed or replaced more than 10 million smart meters across the Kingdom, at no cost to customers.

Jordan has moved quickly too. Figures from the Jordan Electric Power Company (JEPCO) in 2026 show about 1.56 million smart meters installed, close to 95% of the subscriptions in its service area, and other distribution companies are running similar projects.

> **Did you know?** Saudi Arabia's smart meter project, covering more than 10 million meters, took only about 13 months, finishing well ahead of its planned 2023 deadline. Around 4 million of the meters were made locally.

## How to read a smart meter

Every manufacturer designs its display a little differently, but the logic is the same. The screen doesn't sit on one number. It cycles through several readings on its own every few seconds, or each time you press the meter's button. The one you want is the number labelled kWh. That's the cumulative reading: everything the household has used since the meter went in.

Because it's a running total, that figure on its own won't tell you about a single month. A bit of subtraction will. Write down the reading on a chosen day, say the first of the month, then subtract it from the reading on the same day the following month. What's left is your monthly use, and working it out together is a good way for a student to see where the family's bill actually comes from.

Prepaid meters show one more figure: your remaining credit. If the codes on your screen still don't make sense, check your electricity company's guide or official app. Many companies offer apps that show daily usage.

## What is a prepaid electricity meter?

Anyone with a pay-as-you-go phone already understands it. With a prepaid meter you top up credit before you use any power, and the balance falls as you go. When it reaches zero, the meter cuts the supply automatically until you top up again.

The appeal is control over spending: no surprise bill at the end of the month. Prepaid and smart are separate ideas, though. Not every prepaid meter is smart, and some are topped up with a card or a typed code with no network connection at all.

## What do consumers gain?

The most immediate gain is an accurate bill, based on actual readings rather than estimates. Daily tracking comes next. If your usage jumps on a particular day, you have a clue that some appliance, perhaps the water heater or a space heater, is pushing the bill up. The company also finds faults and power cuts faster than it could with old meters.

Further ahead, smart meters make time-of-use tariffs possible, where power costs less outside peak hours and you save by running the washing machine later in the evening. The meter itself won't cut your bill; it shows you where the electricity goes, and the rest is up to you. ## Questions people ask after a smart meter is installed

### Does a smart meter increase my electricity bill?

A smart meter doesn't increase your consumption; it measures it more accurately. Some customers see a higher bill after installation because the old meter was less accurate or their bills had been estimated. If you doubt a reading, you can ask your electricity company to test the meter.

### Are smart meter radio waves harmful?

Smart meters send low-power radio signals in short bursts. Regulators say they fall within international exposure limits, and the exposure is far lower than from a mobile phone held to your ear.

### Can a smart meter be tampered with?

Smart meters alert the company when someone tries to open them or interfere with them. Tampering with a meter is also against the law, and it is dangerous for the person doing it and for everyone living in the home.
