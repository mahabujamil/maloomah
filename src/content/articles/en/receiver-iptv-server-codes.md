A big match is a few days away, and the searches start: "free server code", "free server renewal", "IPTV codes 100% working". These phrases get searched constantly, especially around major football tournaments. So what are IPTV server codes and receiver "servers", why do they keep dying, and are they safe or legal? We'll answer plainly. **We won't publish any codes**, and once you see how they work, the reason will be obvious.

## What is a "server" on a satellite receiver?

Pay channels, such as sports and movie channels, are broadcast encrypted. A receiver can only open them with decryption keys, and the normal way to get those keys is a subscription from the company that owns the channels, through an approved card or device.

What people call a "receiver server" is usually a service that takes those keys and passes them around the internet to many devices, **without permission from the rights holder**. That explains the constant need for "codes" and "renewals". Every time the channels change their encryption, the server breaks, and whoever runs it has to find a new way in.

## What is IPTV?

IPTV just means TV delivered over the internet rather than by satellite. There's nothing shady about the technology itself; plenty of official providers use it.

The trouble is **unlicensed IPTV services**, which sell or hand out thousands of pay channels for very little or nothing, even though they don't own the rights to show them.

## Why we don't publish server codes

Unofficial codes almost always mean watching paid content without the owner's permission. In many countries that's against the law, because it infringes broadcasting rights and intellectual property. It also takes money away from channels and the people who make the shows, who pay huge sums for match and film rights. And search engines and ad networks penalise sites that publish this kind of material, so we'd be harming the site as well.

## The risks of server codes and free renewals

A free code looks like a bargain. The cost tends to show up later.

Malware is the first problem. Many "free renewal" sites tell you to download unknown apps or files onto your receiver, phone or smart TV, and those files can carry software that steals your data or spies on you.

Some sites go straight for your money. They ask for your bank card details "to subscribe", then vanish or take payments you never agreed to.

Paying for an unofficial service doesn't protect you either. People buy a "one-year" plan, the service dies after a few weeks, and there's nobody to complain to.

Then there's the viewing itself. These services go down often, and usually at the worst possible moment of a match, when demand spikes or the stream gets blocked.

Finally, in some countries the viewer, not just the seller, can face legal action.

> **Did you know?** Major sports broadcasters pay hundreds of millions for tournament rights, and use advanced technology to trace pirate streams and shut them down. That's why unofficial servers keep breaking.

## Legal ways to watch channels and matches

Watching legally doesn't have to be expensive:

1. Official apps and subscriptions from the channels that show what you like. Many have monthly plans or special deals during tournaments.
2. Free-to-air satellite channels. Satellites such as Nilesat and Arabsat carry hundreds of free, unencrypted channels, including sports, news and entertainment.
3. Free official streams online. Many public broadcasters stream for free on their own websites and apps.
4. Official YouTube channels run by clubs and leagues, which post highlights and goals legally.

## How to tell a streaming service is illegal

Pirate services tend to share the same warning signs. The price is far below the official subscription, or there's a "lifetime" plan on offer. The package bundles thousands of channels from many different companies, something no legitimate seller does. You can't get it from an official app store, only through links and download files, and it wants payment by personal transfer or top-up cards. There's also no clear company name, no address and no real customer service. If a service ticks several of these boxes, walk away.

## Tips if you have a satellite receiver at home

Only update your receiver's software from the manufacturer's official website, since unofficial updates are an easy way in for malware. Use the automatic channel scan to pick up all the free channels. And don't install files from unknown sites on your receiver or smart TV, however many extra channels they promise.

## Common questions about servers and IPTV

### Is using free IPTV codes illegal?

Watching pay channels through unlicensed services breaks intellectual-property laws in many countries.

### Why does the server stop working after a short time?

Channels change their encryption keys regularly and track down and block illegal streams, so the "renewals" never end.

### Is IPTV itself illegal?

No. It's a legal technology used by many official companies. The illegal part is distributing pay channels without owning the rights. For a safe setup, an official subscription or the many free, legal channels on satellite and online will cover most of what you want to watch.
