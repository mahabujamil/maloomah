Ask a class where a camel keeps its water, and almost every hand goes up with the same answer: in its hump. It's wrong. That moment, when a child is sure and then finds out otherwise, is exactly why **animal quiz questions and answers** work so well in classrooms, school assemblies and family evenings. Below are more than 40 questions graded from easy to hard, a true-or-false round that tackles common myths, and a table for classifying animals. Each answer has a short explanation, because the aim is for children to understand something, not just memorise a word.

## Easy animal quiz questions for kids

These suit primary pupils and younger children. The answers are short, and many kids will know them from picture books and nature programmes:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is the fastest land animal? | The cheetah, which can reach about 100 km/h or a little more |
| What is the largest animal in the world? | The blue whale |
| What is the tallest animal in the world? | The giraffe |
| Which animal is called the "ship of the desert"? | The camel |
| How many legs does a spider have? | Eight |
| How many legs does an insect have? | Six |
| Which insect makes honey? | The honeybee |
| What is a baby lion called? | A cub |
| What is a baby horse called? | A foal |
| What is the largest bird in the world? | The ostrich |
| Which bird can't fly and lives in Antarctica? | The penguin |
| Where does the polar bear live? | In the Arctic, around the North Pole |

The spider and insect questions trip up a lot of children, so we'll come back to them further down. If you want animal speeds in more detail, see our lesson on the [fastest animal in the world](/en/articles/fastest-animal-in-the-world/).

## Medium animal quiz questions with answers

This set is aimed at middle-school students. Instead of just asking for a name, these questions ask how an animal lives or why something is true:

1. What is the only mammal capable of true flight?
   The bat. Some mammals, such as flying squirrels, only glide; bats actually fly by flapping their wings.
2. How many hearts does an octopus have?
   Three: two pump blood to the gills, and one pumps it around the rest of the body.
3. What does a camel store in its hump?
   **Fat**, not water. It uses the fat for energy when food is scarce, which settles the question we started with.
4. Is a whale a fish?
   No. Whales are mammals: they breathe air with lungs, give birth to live young and feed them milk.
5. Which bird can fly backwards?
   The hummingbird. It beats its wings so fast that it can hover in place and even fly backwards.
6. What is the giant panda's main food?
   Bamboo.
7. What is the fastest bird in the world?
   The peregrine falcon, which can exceed 300 km/h when it dives on its prey.
8. Which animal is famous for changing colour?
   The chameleon.
9. About how long is an elephant's pregnancy?
   About 22 months, one of the longest of any animal.
10. Which group does a frog belong to?
    Amphibians, because it spends part of its life in water and part on land.
11. What is a shark's skeleton made of?
    **Cartilage**, a flexible material like the one in human ears and noses, not bone.
12. How do fish breathe?
    With gills, which take in oxygen dissolved in the water.

The whale and frog questions are really about animal groups rather than individual animals, and that is the kind of question that turns up again and again in science tests.

## Hard animal quiz questions for contests

Secondary students and general-knowledge contests need questions that only someone who has read beyond the textbook will get:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Which mammals lay eggs? | The platypus and the echidnas, known as monotremes |
| How many compartments does a cow's stomach have? | Four, because cows are ruminants |
| Which microscopic animal can survive extreme cold and drying out? | The tardigrade (water bear) |
| How many eyes do most spiders have? | Eight |
| What is the change from caterpillar to butterfly called? | Complete metamorphosis: egg, larva, pupa, adult |
| Which animal feeds mostly on eucalyptus leaves? | The koala |
| Which living bird lays the largest egg? | The ostrich |
| How do bats find their way in the dark? | Echolocation: they make sounds and listen for the echoes |
| What are "cold-blooded" animals? | Animals whose body temperature follows their surroundings, such as reptiles, amphibians and most fish |
| What is the largest living reptile? | The saltwater crocodile |

The egg-laying mammals question catches adults out too, since most of us learned that mammals give birth to live young. If the platypus and the water bear caught your interest, there are plenty more like them in [the strangest animals in the world](/en/articles/strangest-animals-in-the-world/).

> **Did you know?** A blue whale's tongue alone can weigh about as much as an elephant, and its heart is roughly the size of a small car. Yet this giant feeds on tiny animals called krill.

## True or false animal questions

Some wrong ideas get passed down so long that they sound like facts. A true-or-false round is the quickest way to expose them, because students have to commit to an answer before they hear the truth:

1. Bats are blind. ❌ False. Bats can see, but many rely on echoes in the dark.
2. Ostriches bury their heads in the sand when scared. ❌ False. It's a famous myth: ostriches lower their heads to turn their eggs in the nest, or press flat to the ground to hide.
3. Dolphins are mammals. ✅ True. They breathe air and feed their young milk.
4. Penguins are birds. ✅ True. They have feathers and beaks and lay eggs, but they swim instead of flying.
5. A spider is an insect. ❌ False. Spiders are arachnids with eight legs; insects have six.
6. A camel stores water in its hump. ❌ False. The hump stores fat.
7. Giraffes are mammals. ✅ True.
8. The blue whale is the largest animal known to have lived on Earth. ✅ True. It is bigger than any known dinosaur.

## Table: classifying animals with examples

Plenty of quiz questions begin with "Which group does … belong to?" This table pulls the main groups together, and it works well printed out on a classroom wall:

| Group | Key features | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mammals | Feed their young milk; most have hair or fur | Lion, whale, bat, camel |
| Birds | Feathers and a beak; lay eggs | Eagle, penguin, ostrich |
| Reptiles | Dry, scaly skin; most lay eggs | Crocodile, tortoise, chameleon |
| Amphibians | Start life in water, then usually live on land | Frog, salamander |
| Fish | Live in water and breathe with gills | Shark, salmon |
| Insects | Six legs and a body in three parts | Bee, butterfly, ant |

What makes a mammal a mammal is feeding its young milk. It isn't about giving birth or living on land, which is why whales, dolphins and bats are all mammals, and so is the platypus, eggs and all.

## How to run an animal quiz in class

A good quiz needs a clear plan more than it needs equipment. These steps work for a full lesson or half of one:

1. Split the class into teams of 4 or 5, and give each team an animal name.
2. Start with the easy questions, then move to medium and hard ones, and make hard questions worth two points.
3. Run a quick true-or-false round where students hold up a green or red card.
4. Give a bonus point to the team that explains why, not just what.
5. Finish with a surprising fact from our [animals "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/animals/), so students leave with something new.

Step four matters most. Once students know a reason earns a point, they stop guessing and start thinking, and the quiz becomes a proper science lesson rather than a memory game.

## Questions teachers and parents ask about animal quizzes

### What are the easiest animal quiz questions for kids?

Questions about names, sounds and sizes, such as: What is the largest animal? What is a baby lion called? How many legs does a spider have? They're short, and the answers are easy to remember.

### Is a bat a bird?

No. A bat is a mammal: it gives birth to live young, feeds them milk and has fur, not feathers. It is the only mammal capable of true flight.

### What is the difference between an insect and a spider?

An insect has six legs and a body in three parts (head, thorax and abdomen), and many have wings. A spider has eight legs, a body in two parts and no wings. Counting the legs gives the answer away.

### How many questions suit a school assembly quiz?

Four to six short questions are usually enough, because the quiz segment of an assembly lasts about two minutes. Include at least one true-or-false question; it gets the whole line of students paying attention.
