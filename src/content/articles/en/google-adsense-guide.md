Say you run a small blog about cooking or tech, and people have started finding it through search. Sooner or later you'll ask whether those pages could earn something. For most site owners the first stop is **Google AdSense**, the world's best-known advertising programme for websites and blogs. The basic deal is easy to grasp: you show Google's ads on your pages and receive part of what advertisers pay. What trips beginners up is getting approved, avoiding rejection and earning more.

## How does Google AdSense work?

Three parties are involved. Advertisers pay Google to show their ads. Google places those ads on sites in the AdSense network, picking ones that suit the page and the visitor, so a review of a laptop might sit next to an electronics shop's ad. The site owner then gets a share of the revenue when visitors view or click those ads.

That is why two things drive your income above all: how many people visit, and how much advertisers care about your subject.

## AdSense approval requirements

You need a website of your own that you control and can edit, since you'll be adding Google's code to it yourself. You also need to be at least 18 years old.

Content matters most. It has to be original and useful, written by you rather than copied from elsewhere. Advertisers don't want their brands appearing next to harmful material, so Google also requires you to follow its policies: no adult content, violence, pirated material or promotion of illegal activities.

Reviewers also look for signs that a real, accountable person or organisation stands behind the site. About, Contact and Privacy Policy pages provide that. Finally, the design should be clear and easy to find your way around, and it has to work well on phones, where much of your audience will be reading.

## How to apply for AdSense

Once the site is ready, applying takes five steps:

1. Sign up on the AdSense website with your Google account.
2. Add your site's URL and choose your country.
3. Place the AdSense code in the head section of your pages.
4. Add an **ads.txt** file to your site's root folder. This small file confirms you're authorised to show ads.
5. Request a review and wait for Google's decision, which can take anywhere from days to a few weeks.

## Why AdSense rejects some sites

The most common reason is low-value content: too few pages, or pages so thin they don't help anyone. Close behind is copied content, including text that has been automatically reworded from other sites.

Some rejections come from clear policy violations, such as pirated content or illegal download links. Others are technical. Broken menus, empty pages, or a site that is unreachable during the review can all lead to a no.

Usually the fix is to add more quality content, solve whatever problem Google pointed to, and apply again.

## How do AdSense payments work?

Money doesn't go straight from your dashboard to your bank. First, Google checks your address. When your earnings reach a small verification threshold of about $10, Google mails a PIN to you. The letter can take 2 to 3 weeks to arrive, and no payment can be issued until you enter that PIN in your account.

Second, you need to reach the **payment threshold**, which depends on your account currency. For accounts in US dollars it's $100, and the Payments section shows the exact figure for yours. If your balance reaches the threshold by the end of a month, Google issues the payment between the 21st and the 26th of the following month. If it doesn't, your earnings simply roll over until it does.

Payment methods depend on your country, and bank transfer is the most common.

## How to earn more with AdSense

More visitors means more income, so start with **search engine optimisation** (SEO): write about topics people actually search for. It also helps to work in niches advertisers value, such as finance, technology, education and health.

Quality comes next. Long, genuinely helpful articles that fully answer a reader's question keep people on the page. Speed matters too, along with the mobile experience, because visitors leave pages that load slowly. You can turn on Auto ads and let Google choose placements, or place ad units yourself where they make sense.

And publish regularly. Active sites attract more visitors.

### Strictly forbidden

A few mistakes can undo all of that:

- Clicking your own ads.
- Asking friends or visitors to click ads.
- Using tools or services to fake traffic or clicks.

Advertisers pay for genuine interest, and these violations can get your account closed permanently, along with your earnings.

## Questions beginners ask about AdSense

### Can I make money with AdSense without a website?

Yes, through YouTube. [YouTube Partner Program](/en/articles/youtube-monetization/) earnings are paid through an AdSense account.

### How much does AdSense pay per 1,000 visits?

There's no fixed number. Earnings depend on your niche, where your visitors are, the ads shown and how visitors interact with them.

### What percentage of ad revenue does AdSense pay?

Since 2024, Google splits the revenue share in two. For display ads, publishers receive 80% of the revenue left after the advertiser-side platform takes its fee. Google said this works out to roughly the same 68% publishers received on average before. Payments are also now mostly per impression rather than per click.

### How many articles do I need to get approved?

Google doesn't set an official number, but having dozens of original, helpful articles greatly improves your chances.

### Can I apply with a free website?

It's better to have your own domain, like maloomah.com. It looks more professional and tends to make approval easier. After approval, steady publishing and strict respect for the policies keep the income coming.
