Hotter summers, less rain or heavier downpours, and droughts that keep coming back. Climate change is no longer a distant topic we only read about in the news; it has become part of daily life. So what exactly is climate change, why is it happening, and how does it affect Jordan and the Arab region?

## What is climate change?

**Climate change** means long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns on Earth. The climate has changed naturally throughout history, but the change we have seen since the 19th century is mainly caused by **human activity**, especially burning fossil fuels: coal, oil and gas.

It's important to separate **weather** from **climate**:

| | Weather | Climate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time span | Hours, days or a week | An average over 30 years or more |
| Example | "Rain in Amman tomorrow" | "Jordan has cool, rainy winters and hot, dry summers" |
| How fast it changes | From one day to the next | Slowly, over decades |

## How does the greenhouse effect work?

Earth is wrapped in a layer of gases that acts like a blanket, letting sunlight in and trapping some of the heat. This natural process is called the **greenhouse effect**, and without it Earth would be too cold for life.

The problem is that humans have greatly increased these **greenhouse gases**, making the "blanket" thicker and warming the planet. The main ones are:

1. **Carbon dioxide (CO₂):** from burning fuel and cutting down forests. Its concentration has risen from about 280 parts per million before the Industrial Revolution to about **423 parts per million** in 2024, the highest level on record, and that year's rise was the largest since modern measurements began in the 1950s.
2. **Methane:** from farming, livestock, landfills and gas leaks.
3. **Nitrous oxide:** from fertilisers and some industries.

## Causes of climate change

- **Generating electricity** from coal, oil and gas.
- **Transport:** cars, trucks, planes and ships.
- **Industry:** such as cement and steel production.
- **Deforestation**, since trees absorb carbon dioxide.
- **Agriculture and waste**, both major sources of methane.

## How much has Earth warmed?

According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), global average temperature in 2011–2020 was about **1.1 °C** higher than before the Industrial Revolution. According to the World Meteorological Organization:

| Fact | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Hottest year on record | 2024, about 1.55 °C above pre-industrial levels |
| Where 2025 ranks | Second or third hottest, depending on the dataset |
| Hottest years on record | The eleven years from 2015 to 2025 |
| Estimated long-term warming | About 1.3–1.4 °C |

**An important note for students:** one year above 1.5 °C doesn't mean the Paris Agreement goal has been missed, because that goal is measured as an average over about twenty years, not a single year.

One and a half degrees may sound small, but it's enough to cause major changes in weather systems around the world.

> **Did you know?** Scientific studies suggest the Eastern Mediterranean and Middle East is warming at nearly **twice the global average rate**, making it one of the regions most exposed to climate change.

## Effects of climate change

1. Longer, more intense **heatwaves**.
2. More frequent **droughts** in some places, and sudden **floods** and heavy rain in others.
3. **Rising sea levels** from melting ice and warming oceans expanding; sea level has risen by about 20 cm since 1900.
4. **Melting glaciers** and polar ice.
5. **Threats to food and water security** as farming and water sources are affected.
6. **Health impacts**, such as heat stress and the spread of some diseases.
7. **Risks to biodiversity**, such as coral bleaching.

## Climate change in Jordan

Jordan is one of the **most water-scarce countries in the world**, which makes it very sensitive to any change in climate. Key challenges include:

- **Declining and erratic rainfall**, putting pressure on dams and groundwater.
- **Rising temperatures** and more summer heatwaves.
- **Impacts on agriculture**, especially rain-fed crops.
- **Higher demand for water and electricity** in summer.

In response, Jordan's 2021 climate plan update raised its emissions-reduction target to **31% by 2030** (part of it conditional on international support), has expanded **solar and wind power** significantly, and is working on the **National Water Carrier** project to desalinate Red Sea water in Aqaba and pump it to Amman and other governorates.

## What is the world doing?

In 2015, countries signed the **Paris Agreement**, which aims to keep warming well below 2 °C while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C. Arab countries have hosted the UN climate summits, including Qatar in Doha in 2012, Morocco in Marrakech in 2016 and Egypt in Sharm El-Sheikh in 2022.

## How can you help?

- **Save electricity:** switch off unused devices and use LED bulbs.
- **Use water wisely**; it's precious in our region.
- **Drive less** when you can, or share rides.
- **Consider solar power** for your home if possible.
- **Reduce waste and recycle**, and don't waste food.
- **Plant a tree**, and raise awareness among family and friends.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the difference between global warming and climate change?

Global warming is the rise in Earth's temperature caused by greenhouse gases. Climate change is broader: it includes the warming plus all the resulting changes in rainfall, wind and extreme events.

### What is the Paris Agreement?

An international agreement signed in 2015 in which countries commit to cutting emissions to keep global warming well below 2 °C, while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5 °C above pre-industrial levels.

### Which greenhouse gas causes the most human-made warming?

**Carbon dioxide** is responsible for the largest share of human-caused warming, because huge amounts come from burning fuel and it stays in the air for a long time. **Methane** comes second.

### Is it too late to stop climate change?

No. Every fraction of a degree avoided makes a big difference, and the shift to clean energy is speeding up worldwide.

## Key takeaway

Climate change is driven mainly by greenhouse gases from human activity, and its effects are clear in heatwaves, droughts and rising seas. Jordan and the Arab region are among the hardest hit, so solutions, from solar power to saving water, are not a luxury but a necessity.
