Ask a class to write an **essay about technology** and you'll get thirty versions of the same paragraph: phones are useful, phones are bad, use them wisely. The topic is so familiar that students stop thinking about it. That's a pity, because they know more about it than almost any other essay subject. They take lessons on learning platforms, message friends late into the night and know exactly what a screen does to their sleep. The model below uses that knowledge. It comes with an outline, a full essay with an introduction, body and conclusion, a short 150-word version for younger pupils, and some advice on making your own essay stand out.

## An outline that keeps the essay organised

Spend five minutes on an outline before you write. It stops you from repeating one idea twice and forgetting another completely. This plan suits most students aged roughly 11 to 15:

1. Introduction: what technology is and how present it is in daily life
2. How technology has changed over time
3. Advantages in education
4. Advantages in health, communication and work
5. Disadvantages when it's misused
6. Using technology sensibly
7. Conclusion: a double-edged sword, and we choose which edge we use

## Introduction

This morning my phone's alarm woke me up. The same screen told me it would rain, and I watched a short video to revise for a science quiz before leaving the house. At no point did I think, "I'm using technology." Technology simply means the tools, machines and methods people build, using scientific knowledge, to make life easier. It has become so ordinary that we hardly notice it, the way we hardly notice the air. That closeness raises a fair question, though. Are we using technology, or has it started using us?

## Body: what technology gives us and what it costs

### Technology has always been with us

Technology isn't new. The wheel was technology, and so were the plough, paper, the clock and the printing press that put books into ordinary hands. What has changed is the speed. A computer that filled a whole room in the middle of the twentieth century had far less power than the phone in a student's pocket today. A letter that once took weeks to cross the world now arrives in under a second.

### Advantages in education

For students, the biggest gift is access to knowledge. Not long ago the textbook and the teacher were almost the only sources available. Now a student can watch a chemistry experiment their school lab could never afford, tour a museum on another continent, or follow lessons from home when illness keeps them away. During the COVID-19 pandemic, when schools in many countries closed, remote learning is what kept classes going.

### Advantages in health, communication and work

Medicine has changed just as much. Modern scanners let doctors see inside the body without surgery, so many illnesses are caught earlier and treated more easily. Communication has shrunk distances: someone working abroad can see their mother's face and hear her voice every evening. At work, machines save time and effort, and whole new careers have appeared, like app design and data analysis, that our grandparents never heard of.

### Disadvantages when it's misused

There's another side, of course. Hours in front of screens strain the eyes and keep the body still. Scrolling late at night steals the sleep a student needs to concentrate in class. A family can sit in the same room with everyone lost in a separate device, close together yet barely talking. The internet is also full of false information and rumours, and some people online try to trick children or steal their personal details. This is why technology is so often called a **double-edged sword**: the same tool can help or harm, depending on how it's used.

### Using technology sensibly

Giving technology up isn't the answer. It's impossible, and it wouldn't help anyway. The answer is to stay in charge. A student can set separate times for schoolwork and games on a device, leave the phone outside the bedroom at night, check a claim against a reliable source before sharing it, and never give personal information to strangers. Families can keep one screen-free hour a day for talking and eating together.

## Conclusion

Technology is neither a perfect friend nor a sworn enemy. It's a tool the human mind created, and the human mind still decides how it's used. People who use it to learn, work and stay close to others gain a great deal. People who let it control their time, sleep and friendships lose more than they realise. I hope my generation will be one that builds technology and uses it well, not one that only consumes it.

> **Did you know?** The world's first website was launched in 1991 by the British scientist Tim Berners-Lee at CERN, the physics laboratory near Geneva. It was a plain page explaining what the World Wide Web was and how to use it.

## A short essay about technology (about 150 words)

Technology means the machines and tools people make to help them in daily life, such as computers, phones and televisions. It helps us in many ways. Students use it to search for information and watch lessons. Families use it to talk to relatives who live far away. Doctors use it to find illnesses and treat patients. However, too much technology can be harmful. Long hours in front of screens tire our eyes, keep us from moving and playing outside, and cut into our sleep. Sometimes it even stops us from talking to the people sitting next to us. That's why we should use technology at set times and for useful things, such as learning and keeping in touch. Technology is a very good servant, but it becomes a bad master when we let it control us.

## How to make your essay stand out

The most common weak essay is a list of gadgets: phone, laptop, TV, fridge. A list isn't an argument. Pick one real example and explain what it changed, such as how a revision app finally made fractions click for you, or how your grandparents now video-call the family every Friday.

Pair each advantage with a matching disadvantage. That shows the marker you're weighing ideas, not reciting them. Avoid numbers you aren't sure of, because one wrong statistic weakens an essay more than none at all. If you'd like an accurate detail about the history of the internet, see [who invented the internet](/en/articles/who-invented-the-internet/), and for a related topic on smart machines, [what is artificial intelligence](/en/articles/what-is-artificial-intelligence/) has plenty of ideas.

## Questions students ask about this essay

### How long should an essay about technology be?

Follow your teacher's instructions first. Younger pupils usually write one or two paragraphs, around 150 words. Older students are often expected to write 250 words or more, with an introduction, at least three body paragraphs and a conclusion.

### What if the title is "Technology is a double-edged sword"?

The model above fits that title as it stands, because it balances benefits against harms. Try opening the introduction and closing the conclusion with the phrase itself, so the essay feels like one connected piece.

### Should I use memorised quotes?

One good quote is enough, as long as you understand it and it connects to the paragraph around it. Markers reward a clear idea in your own words far more than a borrowed line that doesn't fit.
