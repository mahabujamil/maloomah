Ask a class to name the largest organ in the human body and plenty of hands will go up for the liver. The real answer is closer than they think: it's **the skin**, covering the very hands they're raising. Surprises like that are what make good **human body quiz questions and answers**, and you'll find more than 45 of them below, for a classroom quiz, a school assembly or a family game night. They run from easy to hard, and there's a set of true-or-false myths and a table of the body's systems after them. Most answers carry a line explaining why, because a student who understands the reason remembers the fact far longer than one who memorised a single word.

## Easy human body quiz questions for kids

Primary-school children already know a lot about their own bodies. They feel their heart thumping after a race in the playground, they can tell sweet from salty with their tongue, and they lose baby teeth one by one. These questions build on that, and they're short enough to read straight from the table:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Which organ pumps blood around the body? | The heart |
| What do we breathe with? | Our lungs |
| Which organ do we think and remember with? | The brain |
| What is the largest organ of the human body? | The skin |
| What are the five senses? | Sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch |
| What do we taste food with? | The tongue |
| How many baby (milk) teeth does a child have? | 20 |
| How many teeth does an adult have? | 32, including wisdom teeth, which not everyone gets |
| Where does digestion begin? | In the mouth, with chewing and saliva |
| What protects the brain? | The skull |
| How many chambers does the human heart have? | Four: two atria and two ventricles |
| What red liquid flows through our veins and arteries? | Blood |

The teeth questions make a nice follow-up discussion. Twenty baby teeth are replaced by up to 32 adult ones, so children usually want to know where the extra twelve will fit.

## Medium human body quiz questions and answers

By middle school, students are learning about bones, blood and the heart in more detail, so these questions lean on numbers and proper names. Each answer sits under its question, with a short note the quizmaster can read out once a team has answered.

1. How many bones does an adult human have?
   Answer: usually 206. Babies have more, and our lesson [how many bones are in the human body?](/en/articles/how-many-bones-in-the-human-body/) explains why.
2. What is the longest bone in the body?
   Answer: the femur, or thigh bone.
3. What is the smallest bone in the body?
   Answer: the stapes, in the middle ear. It's only about 3 millimetres long, shorter than a grain of rice.
4. How many bones are in the middle ear?
   Answer: three tiny ones, the malleus (hammer), incus (anvil) and stapes (stirrup). Together they pass sound vibrations on to the inner ear.
5. What is the largest internal organ?
   Answer: the liver. Note the word "internal": the skin is the largest organ overall, the liver the largest inside us.
6. What substance in red blood cells carries oxygen?
   Answer: haemoglobin, which is also what makes blood red.
7. Which cells defend the body against germs?
   Answer: white blood cells.
8. Where are blood cells made?
   Answer: in the red bone marrow inside our bones. Bones are a factory as well as a frame.
9. About how long does a red blood cell live?
   Answer: about 120 days. After that the body removes it and makes new ones.
10. About how much blood is in an adult's body?
    Answer: roughly 5 litres, depending on body size.
11. What is the largest artery in the body?
    Answer: the aorta, which leaves the heart's left ventricle.
12. What is the coloured part of the eye called?
    Answer: the iris. The black opening in its centre is the pupil.
13. What is a normal resting heart rate for an adult?
    Answer: about 60 to 100 beats per minute. There's more on this hard-working organ in our [human heart facts](/en/articles/human-heart-facts/).
14. Where is most digested food absorbed?
    Answer: in the small intestine.
15. What are the tiny air sacs in the lungs called?
    Answer: alveoli. This is where the blood picks up oxygen and gets rid of carbon dioxide.

## Hard human body trivia questions and answers

These are meant for secondary-school students and the final round of a quiz. That said, how hard a question feels depends on the age group. The kidney's working unit and the eye layer with no blood vessels will stump most middle-school students but are fair game for older ones, so pick from this list to suit your class rather than going straight down it.

1. Which organ produces the hormone insulin?
   Answer: the pancreas. Insulin helps control the level of sugar in the blood.
2. Which tube carries urine from the kidney to the bladder?
   Answer: the ureter. We have two, one for each kidney.
3. What is the working unit of the kidney called?
   Answer: the nephron. Each kidney has roughly a million of them.
4. Which part of the brain helps with balance and coordinating movement?
   Answer: the cerebellum.
5. Which clear layer at the front of the eye has no blood vessels?
   Answer: the cornea. Since blood doesn't reach it directly, it gets its oxygen mainly from the air and the fluids around it, and having no vessels is part of what keeps it clear.
6. Which gland is called the "master gland"?
   Answer: the pituitary gland, because its hormones control other glands.
7. About how many bones is a baby born with?
   Answer: about 270 to 300 bones and bone parts, many of which fuse as the child grows.
8. Which organ makes bile, which helps digest fats?
   Answer: the liver. Bile is then stored in the gallbladder until it's needed.
9. Which valve separates the left atrium from the left ventricle?
   Answer: the mitral valve.
10. Which vitamin does the skin make with the help of sunlight?
    Answer: vitamin D.

## True or false: human body myths

Some things "everybody knows" about the body simply aren't true. True-or-false statements are a good way to catch them, because students commit to an answer before they hear the truth, and that small moment of surprise makes it stick.

| Statement | Answer | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The left lung is slightly smaller than the right | True | It leaves room for the heart |
| Blood in our veins is blue | False | Blood is always red, just darker in veins; veins only look blue through the skin |
| We only use 10% of our brains | False | A popular myth: brain scans show all parts are active at different times |
| The heart is fully on the left side of the chest | False | It sits roughly in the middle of the chest, tilted to the left |
| Babies have more bones than adults | True | Some bones fuse as we grow |
| Even identical twins have different fingerprints | True | Every person's fingerprints are unique |
| Fingernails grow faster than toenails | True | Fingernails grow noticeably faster |

The blue blood myth is worth a minute of class time. Students look at the veins on their wrists, see blue, and draw the obvious conclusion. In fact the blood inside is dark red, and the blue colour comes from the way light passes through skin.

> **Did you know?** Estimates suggest bone marrow makes around two million red blood cells every second. Because each one lives only about 120 days, this constant production keeps their number in the blood steady.

## Table of human body systems and their jobs

Almost every question above belongs to one of the body's systems. The ureter is part of the urinary system and the alveoli belong to the respiratory system, for example. This table puts the whole picture in one place. Put it on the board for revision before the quiz, or use it to write new questions of the "which system does this organ belong to?" kind.

| System | Main organs | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skeletal system | Bones and joints | Supports the body and protects organs |
| Muscular system | Muscles | Movement |
| Circulatory system | Heart, blood vessels and blood | Carries oxygen and nutrients |
| Respiratory system | Nose, windpipe and lungs | Takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide |
| Digestive system | Mouth, stomach, intestines and liver | Digests and absorbs food |
| Nervous system | Brain, spinal cord and nerves | Controls the body and senses the world |
| Urinary system | Kidneys, ureters and bladder | Filters the blood and removes urine |

## How to run a human body quiz that works

A good quiz depends as much on how you ask as on what you ask. A few simple steps help:

1. Split students into teams of 3 to 5 and take turns asking each team, so everyone gets a go.
2. Start with the easy questions, then move to medium and hard ones. Give double points for hard questions, so a team that falls behind still has a chance.
3. Use pictures or a body model, and ask students to point to the organ instead of just naming it.
4. Read the explanation after each answer. That's what turns a quiz into a lesson.
5. Finish with true or false, because it's fun and clears up widespread myths.

For a school assembly, pick 5 or 6 questions at the right level for the quiz segment, or turn a few of the answers into a "Did you know?" slot.
