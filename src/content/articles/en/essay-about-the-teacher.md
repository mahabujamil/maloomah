Writing an essay about your teacher is slightly awkward, because the person marking it is the subject. Teachers have read "a teacher is a candle that burns itself to light the way for others" more times than they can count, so that line won't win you any marks. What does stand out is an essay that explains why teachers matter, uses real examples from school life, and moves from one idea to the next in a sensible order. Below you'll find an outline, a full model essay suitable for middle school, a shorter version of about 150 words for younger pupils, and some tips.

## Outline for an essay about my teacher

Plan before you write. These points work as a starting skeleton, and you can cut or add to them:

1. Why societies have always respected teachers.
2. What a teacher teaches beyond the textbook.
3. How every profession starts in a classroom.
4. What makes a good teacher.
5. What we owe our teachers, and how to show it.

Each point becomes one paragraph of the body. If your essay has to be short, points 2 and 5 are enough.

## Model essay about my teacher (introduction, body and conclusion)

### Introduction

On our first day of school, most of us couldn't hold a pencil properly or tell one letter from another. Then someone stood at the front of the room and wrote on the board, explaining the same thing again and again until the letters became words and the words became sentences we could read on our own. That person was a teacher, and nearly everything we know now passed through a teacher's hands at some point. To write about teachers is really to write about where all learning begins.

### Body

#### A place of honour

Respect for teachers runs deep in almost every culture. In Arabic, one of the most quoted lines of poetry, by Ahmad Shawqi, tells students to stand up for their teacher and honour him, because a teacher is almost a messenger. The comparison makes sense: a messenger carries a message to people, and a teacher carries knowledge and good character to every new generation. The Quran, too, speaks of God raising "those who were given knowledge, by degrees" (Al-Mujadila, 58:11), and teachers are the ones who pass that knowledge on.

#### Lessons that aren't in the textbook

Some people think a teacher's work ends when the lesson is explained and the homework is marked. In fact, teachers teach us things no book contains. A teacher who asks you to own up to a mistake instead of making excuses is teaching honesty. One who starts every lesson on time is teaching discipline, and one who treats every student the same is teaching fairness. Plenty of shy students who never raised a hand have become confident speakers because a teacher noticed them and said one encouraging word at the right moment.

#### Every career starts in a classroom

The doctor treating patients, the engineer designing bridges and the pilot flying a plane all once sat at a school desk in front of a teacher. That's why countries that care about their future invest in training and supporting teachers: the quality of teaching today decides the quality of doctors and engineers twenty years from now. It's also why teachers have their own international day, marked by schools in many countries.

#### What makes a good teacher

A good teacher knows their subject well and enjoys it, and that enthusiasm spreads to the class. They're patient with the question asked for the third time, because not every student learns at the same speed. They're fair, firm without being harsh, and they know when to encourage and when to hold someone to account. Above all, they set an example, since students learn more from what a teacher does than from what a teacher says.

#### What we owe our teachers

Thanking a teacher shouldn't be limited to a card on Teachers' Day. It shows in small, daily things: listening when they explain, handing work in on time, speaking politely, and keeping the classroom tidy. The best gift we can give is to do well and behave well, because our success is the result of their effort. And the debt doesn't end at graduation. We can still speak well of our teachers and greet them warmly when we meet them years later.

### Conclusion

A teacher sees in us what we can't yet see in ourselves, and is patient with us until we grow into it. We may forget many of the lessons, but we rarely forget the teacher who believed in us. So let's give our teachers the respect they deserve now, rather than understanding their value only when we're grown, and let our success be our thank-you.

> **Did you know?** World Teachers' Day has been celebrated on 5 October since 1994. The date was chosen because on 5 October 1966, UNESCO and the International Labour Organization signed a joint recommendation on the status of teachers, setting out their rights, responsibilities and training.

## A short essay about my teacher for younger classes

My teacher taught me how to read and write. Every day she explains our lessons with patience and kindness. She comes to school early, prepares her lessons, marks our notebooks, and smiles when we get an answer right.

My teacher doesn't only teach us maths and science. She also teaches us good manners, like telling the truth, keeping things tidy and respecting other people. Without teachers, there would be no doctors, engineers or pilots.

That's why I respect my teacher. I listen to her, I do my homework on time, and I help keep our classroom calm. Thank you to every teacher for helping us learn and grow.

(If your teacher is a man, just change "she" and "her" to "he" and "his".)

## Tips for a better essay about your teacher

- Open with a scene or a question instead of a dictionary definition. The first day of school works well.
- Don't pile up quotations. One well-chosen line, explained in your own words, beats five in a row.
- Back every idea with an example from school life. Examples are what make an essay feel real.
- Watch your punctuation and end every complete sentence with a full stop.
- Keep the conclusion short. Sum up the main idea without repeating the introduction word for word.
