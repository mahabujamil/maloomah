Who invented the telephone? Most people answer straight away: **Alexander Graham Bell, in 1876**. That answer is right as far as it goes. What it leaves out is a race to the patent office, a rival who filed on the very same day, and an Italian inventor who couldn't afford to keep his paperwork going. Then, a century later, the phone left the wall for good.

## Who invented the telephone, and when?

Bell (1847–1922) was born in Edinburgh, Scotland, and later lived in Canada and the United States. Sound and hearing were personal subjects for him. His mother was deaf, so was his wife, and he worked as a teacher of deaf students. It's not hard to see why a man like that would spend years asking how a voice could travel.

On 7 March 1876 he was granted US patent No. 174,465 for a method of transmitting speech electrically. Three days later, on 10 March, the idea worked. His assistant Thomas Watson was in another room when Bell's voice came through the device: "Mr. Watson, come here, I want to see you." That is the best-known account of the first words ever spoken on a telephone.

## How did the first telephone work?

The trick was to **turn sound into an electric current and then back into sound**. When you speak, your voice pushes against a thin membrane called a diaphragm and makes it vibrate. Those vibrations change the strength of a current flowing along a wire. At the far end, the changing current moves a second diaphragm in the same pattern, which shakes the air and recreates your voice.

If you've ever made a tin-can phone with a piece of string, you already know half of it. The difference is that Bell's wire didn't carry the vibration itself; it carried an electrical copy of it. That's why the telephone had to wait until people understood electricity and magnetism, a story told in [who invented electricity](/en/articles/who-invented-electricity/).

## The telephone dispute: Gray and Meucci

Bell wasn't working alone in the world. On 14 February 1876, the same day Bell's application was filed, the American inventor Elisha Gray filed a document at the patent office describing his own telephone design. Historians argued for a long time about who got there first. The courts, at least, settled it in Bell's favour.

Earlier still was Antonio Meucci, an Italian inventor living in the United States, who built a voice-transmitting device years before Bell. He was poor, though, and couldn't renew his provisional patent filing, so he lost any official claim. In 2002 the US House of Representatives passed a resolution recognising his work and his contribution to the invention.

The fairest way to put it, then, is that the telephone grew out of several inventors' work, and Bell was the one who secured the patent and built a business on it.

> **Did you know?** Many historical accounts say that during Alexander Graham Bell's burial in 1922, telephone service across North America fell silent for a minute in his honour.

## Who invented the mobile phone?

For about a hundred years, every phone was tied to a cable. Then on 3 April 1973 **Martin Cooper**, an American engineer at Motorola, stood on a New York street and made the first call from a handheld mobile phone. The first one you could actually buy, the DynaTAC 8000X, went on sale in 1983. It was heavy and expensive, and the battery lasted for only a fairly short call.

## Who invented the smartphone?

The next step was to turn the phone into a small computer. Many people count IBM Simon, released in 1994, as the first smartphone, since it combined calling with a touchscreen, email and a few simple apps. Apple's first iPhone arrived in 2007 and changed what people expected a smartphone to be, and Android phones, now found almost everywhere, followed.

## Questions students ask about the telephone

### Who invented the landline telephone?

The landline is simply the original telephone, the one Bell patented in 1876, with contributions from Elisha Gray and Antonio Meucci.

### Who invented the cell phone?

"Cell phone" is another name for the mobile phone, credited to Martin Cooper and his team at Motorola, who made the first handheld call in 1973.

### Did Bell really invent the telephone?

Bell held the official **patent** and made the first known successful call, so the credit goes to him. Historians also recognise the people who came before or alongside him, especially Meucci and Gray. The device in your pocket owes something to all of them.
