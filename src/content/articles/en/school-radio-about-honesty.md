The homework sheet is half empty, and the teacher asks, "Where's your answer, Ahmed?" Ahmed can invent a story about a notebook left at home, or he can simply admit he didn't finish. Every school sees that moment a dozen times a week, and it is where this **school radio about honesty** begins. Below is a complete script for a morning assembly: an opening, Quran verses, an authentic hadith, a morning talk, a "Did you know?" segment, a short story, a quiz and a closing. The language is simple enough for primary pupils, and older students can read it as written.

## Sharing the segments out

A good assembly is short. Ten minutes is plenty; every minute beyond that is felt by the people standing in the yard. The script splits into small parts so that several students get a turn:

1. Opening: the presenter, about one minute.
2. Quran: a student who recites well, two minutes.
3. Hadith: one student, one minute.
4. Morning talk: a confident speaker, two minutes.
5. Did you know?: two students taking turns, ninety seconds.
6. Short story: one student, two minutes.
7. Quiz and closing: the presenter, two minutes.

On a tight morning, keep the opening, the Quran, the morning talk and the closing. Those four carry the whole message.

## Opening

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
> Good morning, school, and good morning to every honest word and clear heart.
> Our respected principal, dear teachers, fellow students, peace be upon you.
> Today we talk about a quality that is small to spell and huge in its effect, one we look for in a friend, a neighbour, a shopkeeper and a teacher: honesty. An honest person sleeps easily, is trusted by everyone around them, and never has to remember what they said yesterday.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran verses about honesty

The Arabic text of each verse is taken word for word from quran.com, and the meanings below follow the Saheeh International translation. The student should recite from a printed Quran.

> "O you who have believed, fear Allāh and be with those who are true." [Surah At-Tawbah, verse 119]

> "O you who have believed, fear Allāh and speak words of appropriate justice." [Surah Al-Ahzab, verse 70]

> "And the one who has brought the truth [i.e., the Prophet (ﷺ)] and [they who] believed in it - those are the righteous." [Surah Az-Zumar, verse 33]

After the recitation the presenter says "Sadaqa Allahu al-Azim" and hands over to the hadith.

## Hadith about honesty

> Abdullah ibn Mas'ud reported that the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "Hold fast to truthfulness, for truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man keeps telling the truth and striving for it until he is recorded with God as truthful…" [Sahih Muslim 2607; also in Sahih al-Bukhari 6094 with similar wording]

The student can explain it in two sentences. Honesty never stays a single act; it pulls a person towards other good deeds, and those lead to Paradise. "Striving for it" means aiming for the truth on purpose until it becomes a habit.

## Morning talk on honesty

> Dear classmates,
> Some of us think a small lie does no harm. "I left my notebook at home." "My mum didn't help with the drawing." "It wasn't me talking." But a small lie always needs another one to protect it, and then a third, until you are caught in a net you can't climb out of.
> The honest road is short and clear. Owning up might cost you a mark or a telling-off, but it earns you something worth far more than a mark: the trust of your teacher, your family and your friends. Once trust breaks, it is very hard to mend.
> Honesty at our school looks like ordinary things: doing your own test without copying, handing back the pen you found, saying "that was my fault", and repeating what you heard without adding to it.
> So let's take this as today's motto: "I tell the truth, even when it's hard."

## Did you know? Facts about honesty

Two students take turns reading one line each:

- Did you know that before his prophethood, the Prophet Muhammad was known among his people as "al-Sadiq al-Amin", the truthful and trustworthy one?
- Did you know that the caliph Abu Bakr was given the title "al-Siddiq", an Arabic form meaning someone who is truthful through and through?
- Did you know that the Arabic words for honesty (sidq) and charity (sadaqah) come from the same three-letter root, ṣ-d-q?
- Did you know that an old Arabic proverb says "honesty is a way to safety"?

> **Did you know?** Psychology research suggests that lying takes more mental effort than telling the truth. A liar has to build the story, remember it and watch every word so as not to contradict it. An honest person only has to remember what happened.

## A short story: the ball and the window

This story was written for the assembly and isn't a real event. It works best read slowly, with the voice changing at each turn:

> Layla and her little brother Yusuf were playing football in the courtyard when Yusuf kicked the ball too hard. It smashed the neighbour's window. They ran inside, and Yusuf whispered, "Nobody will know it was us."
> Layla thought for a moment. "He'll know someone broke it, and he'll suspect every child on the street. Let's tell him."
> They knocked on the door, and Yusuf apologised in a shaky voice. The neighbour smiled. "A window can be fixed. An honest boy can't be bought at any price." That evening their father helped repair the glass, and from then on the neighbour invited them to play in his big garden.
> The lesson: owning up is scary for a minute; lying is scary for days.

## Assembly quiz

The presenter asks, and students raise their hands:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Complete the verse: "Fear Allāh and be with …" | those who are true (At-Tawbah 9:119) |
| What was the Prophet called before his prophethood? | al-Sadiq al-Amin, the truthful and trustworthy |
| Which companion was titled al-Siddiq? | Abu Bakr |
| According to the hadith, where does honesty lead? | To righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise |
| What did Layla do in the story? | She and her brother told the neighbour the truth |

## Closing

> As our assembly ends, remember that honesty isn't a lesson we hear in the yard and leave at the classroom door. It is a choice we make every day: in tests, on the playground and at home.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Questions teachers ask about this assembly

### Does it suit primary school?

Yes. The story and quiz are pitched at younger pupils. For the first two grades, cut the morning talk to three sentences and use a single verse the child knows well.

### Can I combine honesty and trustworthiness?

Easily, because the two go together: an honest person doesn't betray a trust, and a trustworthy person doesn't lie. Add one example of trustworthiness to the morning talk, such as returning a wallet found in the yard. If you want students to write on the theme too, the [essay about honesty](/en/articles/essay-about-honesty/) is a full model with an introduction, body and conclusion.

### How do I help students speak confidently?

Rehearse at least a day ahead, and give each student one short part rather than several long ones. You could follow up later in the week with an assembly on [good manners](/en/articles/school-radio-about-good-manners/), or pick extra short facts from the [general knowledge "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/general/).
