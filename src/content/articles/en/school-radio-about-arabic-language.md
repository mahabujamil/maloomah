Every year on **18 December** the world marks **World Arabic Language Day**, and many teachers start preparing weeks ahead. This **school assembly on the Arabic language** is a complete, ready-to-read script for the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio". It works on any day of the year and includes an introduction, Quran verses, a morning talk, poetry, "Did you know?" facts, a "say this, not that" corner for common mistakes, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing out the parts.

## Running order for a school assembly on the Arabic language

This running order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Teachers can shorten it by dropping one or more segments.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 minute | Host |
| 2 | Quran verses | 2 minutes | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Morning talk on Arabic | 2 minutes | A student |
| 4 | Poetry | 1 minute | A confident reader |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 2 minutes | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Say this, not that | 1 minute | A student |
| 7 | Quiz | 2 minutes | Host |
| 8 | Closing | 1 minute | Host |

## Introduction

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who created man and taught him eloquence.
> Good morning, and welcome to a morning of beautiful letters and kind words.
> Dear principal, dear teachers, fellow students, may your morning be full of good things.
> Today we celebrate the Arabic language: the language of the Quran, of poetry, of science and of civilisation, the language we think in, dream in and write our best thoughts in. The world celebrates it every 18 December, and we celebrate it every day when we speak it, read it and look after it.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by …

## Quran verses about the Arabic language

The student chooses one or more of these verses. The Arabic text is taken from quran.com, and the English meanings follow the Saheeh International translation:

> "Indeed, We have sent it down as an Arabic Qur'ān that you might understand." [Surah Yusuf, 12:2]

> "And indeed, it is the revelation of the Lord of the worlds. The Trustworthy Spirit has brought it down upon your heart, that you may be of the warners, in a clear Arabic language." [Surah Ash-Shu'ara, 26:192–195]

> "The Most Merciful. Taught the Qur'ān. Created man. Taught him eloquence." [Surah Ar-Rahman, 55:1–4]

**A note for teachers:** we have not included a hadith segment about the Arabic language, because many sayings that circulate about its virtues are not authentic. A school may choose an authentic hadith about knowledge or kind speech, after checking its text and grading with qualified scholars.

## Morning talk on the Arabic language

> Dear friends,
> Did you know that the words we say every morning, *sabah al-khair*, carry a history thousands of years long? Arabic is one of the oldest living languages whose old texts people can still understand today. We can read poetry composed more than 1,400 years ago and follow most of it.
> Arabic is also rich and precise. It has many names for the lion and for the sword, and fine words that separate meanings which are close to each other. It was the language of science in the golden age of Islamic civilisation: Al-Khwarizmi wrote about mathematics in Arabic, Ibn Sina about medicine and Ibn al-Haytham about optics, and their books were translated into the languages of Europe.
> But a language doesn't live in books alone. It lives on our tongues. When we speak standard Arabic in class, write without spelling mistakes and read a good book every week, we keep our language strong.
> So let today's motto be: "My language is my identity. I speak it with pride and write it with care."

## Poetry about the Arabic language

One of the most famous poems about Arabic is by the Egyptian poet **Hafez Ibrahim**, known as "the Poet of the Nile". In it, the Arabic language speaks about itself. The Arabic verses are read aloud; here is their meaning in English:

> I looked into myself and doubted my own mind, then called to my people and mourned my life.
> I held the Book of God in word and purpose, and never fell short of its verses and lessons.
> So how could I fall short today of describing a machine, or of naming new inventions?
> I am the sea, with pearls hidden in its depths. Did they ever ask the diver about my shells?

**In simple words:** Arabic says it was wide enough to carry the words and meanings of the Quran, so it cannot be too narrow to name modern machines and inventions. It compares itself to a sea hiding pearls: only those who dive into it discover its treasures.

## "Did you know?" facts about the Arabic language

Two students can read these in turn:

- **Did you know** that on 18 December 1973 the UN General Assembly decided to make Arabic one of its official and working languages? That's why this date was chosen as World Arabic Language Day.
- **Did you know** that Arabic is one of the six official languages of the United Nations, along with English, French, Spanish, Russian and Chinese?
- **Did you know** that the Arabic alphabet has 28 letters and is written from right to left?
- **Did you know** that Arabic is nicknamed "the language of *ḍād*", because the sound of the letter ض is one of its best-known features?
- **Did you know** that Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi founded the science of Arabic poetic metre and wrote *Kitab al-Ayn*, considered the first Arabic dictionary?
- **Did you know** that Arabic speakers are estimated in the hundreds of millions, and that Arabic is an official language in more than 20 countries?

> **Did you know?** Many English words come from Arabic: *algebra* from *al-jabr*, *algorithm* from the name of the scholar Al-Khwarizmi, *sugar* from *sukkar* and *coffee* from *qahwa*. You'll find more facts like these on our [Arabic language did-you-know page](/en/did-you-know/arabic/).

## Say this, not that: common mistakes in Arabic

A short, fun corner that teaches correct usage. The examples are in Arabic, since they are about Arabic spelling and grammar:

| Say or write | Not | Why, briefly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| إنْ شاء الله | إنشاء الله | «إنشاء» means "building"; «إنْ شاء» means "if God wills" |
| شكراً جزيلاً | شكراً جزيلٌ | The adjective follows the noun's case |
| هذا، ذلك، لكنّ | هاذا، ذالك، لاكنّ | These words are pronounced with an alif but written without one |
| هؤلاء | هاؤلاء | The alif is pronounced but not written |
| ذهبتُ إلى المدرسة | colloquial forms in formal speech | Standard Arabic suits assemblies and school writing |

Teachers can swap in mistakes they notice in their own students' notebooks to make the segment more personal.

## Assembly quiz on the Arabic language

The host asks the questions and students raise their hands to answer:

1. **When is World Arabic Language Day?** On 18 December.
2. **How many letters are in the Arabic alphabet?** 28.
3. **Why is Arabic called "the language of ḍād"?** Because the sound of ض is one of its most distinctive features.
4. **Which poet wrote "I am the sea, with pearls hidden in its depths"?** Hafez Ibrahim.
5. **Who wrote *Kitab al-Ayn*?** Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi.
6. **Where does the English word *algebra* come from?** From the Arabic word *al-jabr*.

## Closing

> To close our assembly, let's remember that the Arabic language is a trust in our hands. We protect it when we read, write and speak it well, and we make it beautiful when we choose kind words.
> Thank you for listening, and thank you to everyone who took part today.
> Peace be upon you, and God's mercy and blessings.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is World Arabic Language Day?

It falls on **18 December** every year, the day in 1973 when the UN General Assembly made Arabic one of its official and working languages.

### How long is this school assembly on the Arabic language?

The full script takes about 10 to 12 minutes. If time is short, keep the introduction, the Quran verses, the morning talk, the "Did you know?" facts and the closing.

### Is this script suitable for primary school?

Yes, with small changes: shorten the morning talk, use two lines of poetry instead of four and choose the easier quiz questions. The full version suits middle and secondary school.

### Why is there no hadith segment?

Most of the sayings that circulate about the virtues of the Arabic language are not authentic, and we only publish texts we have verified. A school can add an authentic hadith about knowledge or good speech instead.
