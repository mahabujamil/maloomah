On Teachers' Day, or on any morning when students want to say thank you, the person running the assembly usually needs a full script they can hand out in a few minutes. This is a complete **school assembly about teachers**, written for the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio". It runs in the order it's presented: an introduction, Quran verses, an authentic hadith, a morning talk, "Did you know?" facts, a true story, a poem, a quiz and a closing. A short table at the start helps you share out the parts and keep to time.

## Running order for a school assembly about teachers

The order below fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. If you're short of time, the poem and the quiz are the easiest pieces to cut; the Quran and the morning talk are the heart of it.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | One student |
| 4 | Morning talk about teachers | 2 min | One student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 2 min | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | A true story about a patient teacher | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 7 | Poem and sayings | 1 min | Two students |
| 8 | Assembly quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 9 | Closing and thanks to teachers | 1 min | Host |

## Introduction to the teachers' assembly

The host reads this calmly and fills in the name of the student who will recite at the end:

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who taught by the pen, taught people what they did not know.
> Good morning, and a morning of gratitude to everyone who has taught us.
> Our respected principal, our dear teachers, fellow students: good morning.
> Today we stand to say thank you to the people with the noblest job of all: those who stay up late preparing our lessons, who are patient with our questions and our mistakes, and who celebrate our success as if it were their own. Today's assembly is about the teacher, a small gift to every teacher in our school.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran segment on knowledge and teaching

These verses tie knowledge to teaching. The Arabic text is taken from quran.com and the English meaning from the Saheeh International translation. One verse is enough if time is tight, and it's best recited from the Mushaf with proper tajweed:

> "Just as We have sent among you a messenger from yourselves reciting to you Our verses and purifying you and teaching you the Book and wisdom and teaching you that which you did not know." [Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 151]

> "Say, 'Are those who know equal to those who do not know?' Only they will remember [who are] people of understanding." [Surah Az-Zumar, part of verse 9]

> "Who taught by the pen, taught man that which he knew not." [Surah Al-Alaq, verses 4–5]

After the recitation the host says: Next, the hadith, presented by ……

## Hadith segment on the virtue of teaching

> Uthman ibn Affan (may God be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "The best of you are those who learn the Quran and teach it." [Sahih al-Bukhari, 5027]

Most students have heard this hadith many times, so a two-sentence explanation helps it land. It praises someone who does two things: learns the Quran, then teaches it to others. Teaching earns that praise because its benefit doesn't stop with the teacher; it passes on to everyone they teach. The early scholar Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami taught the Quran for many years, and he said this very hadith was what kept him in his teaching seat.

## Morning talk about teachers

This talk takes about two minutes to read aloud. Only the youngest classes will need a shorter version.

> Dear friends,
> Imagine a school without teachers: closed books, an empty board, and plenty of questions with no answers. A teacher turns a book into understanding, understanding into skills, and skills into a future.
> Every doctor who treats the sick, every engineer who builds bridges and every pilot who flies a plane once sat at a school desk in front of a patient teacher who taught them to read and count. Teachers are partners in every success we see around us.
> And teachers don't only teach us lessons. They teach us to respect time, to be honest in our work, to cooperate with classmates, and to believe in ourselves when we make a mistake and try again.
> So the least we can give our teachers is respect: listening when they explain, doing our homework carefully, and thanking them with a kind word. Let today's motto be: "Thank you to everyone who taught me a single letter."

## "Did you know?" facts about teachers

This segment works best with two students swapping after each fact:

- Did you know that the world celebrates World Teachers' Day on 5 October every year, and that it was first marked in 1994?
- Did you know that the day commemorates the signing of the 1966 ILO/UNESCO Recommendation concerning the Status of Teachers?
- Did you know that the Greek philosopher Aristotle was called "the First Teacher", and that the Muslim scholar Abu Nasr al-Farabi was called "the Second Teacher" for his commentaries on Aristotle?
- Did you know that the famous Arabic line "Stand up for the teacher and honour him; the teacher is almost a messenger" was written by the poet Ahmed Shawqi, known as the Prince of Poets?
- Did you know that UNESCO estimates the world needs about 44 million additional teachers by 2030 so that every child can get a good education?
- Did you know that many countries also hold their own national Teachers' Day, on a date that differs from one country to another?

> **Did you know?** The Arabic word *ustadh* (teacher, professor) comes from Persian. It once meant a master of a craft, and later became a title for teachers and for anyone who masters a field and teaches it.

There are plenty more short, assembly-sized facts on our [Arabic language facts page](/en/did-you-know/arabic/).

## A true story about a teacher for the assembly

A real story holds a crowd of students better than anything else in the programme, and this one is well known and easy to tell:

> Story: the teacher who taught Helen the word "water"
> Helen Keller was born in the United States in 1880. Before she was two, an illness left her unable to see or hear. She lived in a silent, dark world and was often angry, because she couldn't understand anyone and no one could understand her.
> In 1887 a young teacher named **Anne Sullivan** arrived. Anne spelled letters into Helen's palm with her fingers, but Helen didn't yet understand that everything has a name.
> One day the teacher took her to the water pump. She held one of Helen's hands under the cold water and spelled "water" into the other, again and again. Suddenly Helen understood that those finger movements were the name of the cool thing flowing over her hand. She was overjoyed, and that same day she learned the names of many more things.
> Anne stayed with her for many years, and in 1904 Helen graduated from college. She went on to become a well-known author and speaker around the world.
> The lesson: a teacher's patience can work wonders, and one word taught by a devoted teacher can change a whole life.

## Poem and sayings about teachers

The first student opens with Ahmed Shawqi's line, which most Arabic speakers know by heart:

> "Stand up for the teacher and honour him; the teacher is almost a messenger."

The second student follows with these short sayings, one at a time:

1. A teacher is a candle that lights the way for others.
2. Thank you to everyone who taught me a letter and opened a door to knowledge.
3. Behind every successful student is a teacher who believed in them.
4. Respecting your teacher is the first step on the road to learning.
5. Teachers sow today, and the whole country harvests tomorrow.

## School assembly quiz about teachers

Every answer comes from earlier in the assembly, so anyone who listened can win. The host asks, and students raise their hands:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| When is World Teachers' Day? | 5 October |
| Which poet wrote "Stand up for the teacher and honour him"? | Ahmed Shawqi |
| Who was called "the Second Teacher"? | Abu Nasr al-Farabi |
| What was the name of Helen Keller's teacher? | Anne Sullivan |
| What was the first word Helen Keller understood? | Water |
| Complete the hadith: "The best of you are those who learn the Quran …" | "… and teach it" |

## Closing the assembly

> To end our assembly, we say to every teacher in our school: thank you for your patience, thank you for your knowledge, and thank you for believing in us even on the days we forget to believe in ourselves.
> We promise to be hard-working students who respect our lessons, keep good order and make our school proud.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Questions teachers ask when preparing this assembly

### When is Teachers' Day?

World Teachers' Day falls on **5 October** every year, and UNESCO has celebrated it since 1994. Plenty of countries, in the Arab world and elsewhere, also keep a national Teachers' Day on another date, so check which one your country uses. The script works on any school day, though; a thank-you doesn't need an occasion.

### Is this assembly suitable for primary school?

Yes. The story, the short sayings and the quiz are all pitched well for younger pupils. For the first grades, trim the morning talk and keep just three of the "Did you know?" facts.

### How can I make a teachers' assembly more moving?

Add a "Letter to my teacher" slot, where a student reads a short thank-you note they wrote themselves to a teacher who made a difference. A student's own words reach teachers in a way a polished script can't. Students can also hand teachers a flower or a card as the assembly ends.

### Can I combine this with an assembly about science?

Easily, since there's no learning without someone to teach it. Borrow segments from our [school assembly on science and scientists](/en/articles/school-radio-about-science/), or use our [essay about reading](/en/articles/essay-about-reading/) as the basis for a second morning talk. Either way, the message stays the same: respecting and appreciating teachers is the first step to success.
