Sooner or later, almost every student is asked to write an **essay about pollution**. It's a topic you can see from the classroom window: exhaust fumes from the cars queuing at the school gate, plastic bags caught on a fence, a beach left covered in bottles after a busy weekend. Below is a complete model essay with an introduction, a body covering the types, causes, effects and solutions of pollution, and a conclusion. After it come a short version for primary pupils and some advice on making the essay your own.

## Outline of an essay about pollution

An essay is much easier to write once you know where each idea belongs. Plan it first and you won't repeat yourself or run out of things to say halfway through. The model follows this outline:

1. Introduction: what the environment and pollution are, and why the topic matters.
2. Types of pollution: air, water, soil, noise and plastic pollution.
3. Causes: vehicle and factory fumes, waste, pesticides and burning rubbish.
4. Effects: on human health, on animals and plants, and on the climate.
5. Solutions: what individuals, schools and governments can do.
6. Conclusion: a summary and a call to action.

## Introduction to the essay about pollution

The Earth gives us clean air to breathe, fresh water to drink and fertile soil to grow our food. Together these make up the environment we live in. But as people have used more and more resources and cared less about keeping their surroundings clean, they have upset much of this balance. The result is **environmental pollution**: harmful substances entering the air, water or soil in amounts that damage living things. The Quran describes this when it says: "Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned…" [Surat al-Rum, verse 41]. Because pollution affects every one of us, we need to understand its types and causes, and how we can protect our planet from it.

## Body: the main types of pollution

Pollution takes different forms depending on which part of the environment it reaches. The best known are these:

| Type of pollution | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air pollution | Harmful gases, smoke and dust entering the air | Car exhausts, factory smoke, burning rubbish |
| Water pollution | Harmful substances mixing into rivers, seas and groundwater | Sewage, factory waste, oil spills |
| Soil pollution | Harmful substances building up in farmland | Too many pesticides and fertilisers, buried waste |
| Noise pollution | Loud, disturbing sounds that go on for a long time | Car horns, building machinery, loudspeakers |
| Plastic pollution | Plastic bags and bottles piling up on land and at sea | Bags, bottles and single-use items |

Air pollution is among the most dangerous of these, if only because we can refuse a glass of dirty water but we cannot stop breathing. The World Health Organization reports that about 99% of the world's population breathes air that exceeds its recommended pollution limits. Plastic is a growing problem too: the UN Environment Programme estimates that 19 to 23 million tonnes of plastic leak into lakes, rivers and seas every year.

## Causes of pollution

Most pollution comes from human activity. Cars, factories and power stations burn fuel and release harmful gases into the air. Rubbish thrown into streets, valleys and onto beaches adds to the damage, and burning it in the open air does not make it disappear; it simply moves the problem from the ground into the sky.

Chemicals are another cause. Farmers who overuse pesticides and fertilisers, and factories that pour untreated waste into rivers, both poison the land and water around them. Cutting down trees makes things worse, because trees clean the air, and every one we fell takes away one of the environment's best defenders. Finally there are our own wasteful habits: buying things we don't need, and using plastic items once before throwing them away.

## The effects of pollution on people and nature

The harm pollution does reaches every kind of living thing, and people are among the first to feel it. Air pollution causes diseases of the lungs and heart, and the World Health Organization links it to about 7 million early deaths worldwide each year. Polluted water spreads many diseases as well.

Animals suffer alongside us. Sea turtles and seabirds swallow pieces of plastic, mistaking them for food, and many fish die in polluted water. On land, polluted soil grows weaker crops, and harmful substances can find their way from the soil into our food. The effects even reach the climate, since some polluting gases add to global warming, which we explain in our article [what is climate change](/en/articles/climate-change/).

> **Did you know?** Some plastic bottles take hundreds of years to break down in nature. Meanwhile they crumble into tiny pieces called "microplastics", which have been found in the deepest ocean trenches and in Arctic ice.

## How to reduce pollution

Protecting the environment is a shared responsibility, and everyone has a part to play. As individuals, we can put rubbish in the bin, use fewer plastic bags, carry a refillable water bottle, switch off devices we aren't using, and walk or take public transport whenever possible.

Schools can organise clean-up and tree-planting campaigns, provide separate bins for paper and plastic, and hold a school assembly about the environment. Governments and factories carry the heaviest load: they can pass laws against dumping waste, treat sewage, and support clean energy such as solar and wind power.

**Recycling** ties all of these together. Turning used paper, glass and metal into new products means less waste and fewer resources used up.

## Conclusion of the essay about pollution

Pollution threatens our health, our food, our water and the future of the generations to come, but it is not something we must simply accept. If each of us changed one small habit, such as putting a wrapper in the bin, saying no to a plastic bag we don't need, or planting a tree, our whole environment would change for the better. Let us look after the Earth we live on, because it has been placed in our care, and we have no other.

## Short essay about pollution for primary pupils

The environment is everything around us: the air, water, soil, plants and animals. Pollution is when harmful things such as smoke, rubbish and plastic get into the environment. There are different kinds of pollution, such as air pollution, water pollution, soil pollution and noise. Pollution is bad for people's health, and it hurts animals and plants. I can protect my environment by putting rubbish in the bin, keeping my classroom and school clean, planting a tree, and not wasting water or electricity. Let's all keep our environment clean and beautiful.

## Tips for writing an excellent essay about pollution

The model shows you the order, but top marks usually go to an essay that sounds like the student actually wrote it. A few things help:

- Start with a short introduction that grabs the reader, then move on to the body.
- Give each idea its own paragraph: types, then causes, then effects, then solutions.
- Use linking words such as "on the other hand", "in addition" and "finally".
- Add examples from your own town or village, like a road jammed with traffic or a patch of wasteland where people dump rubbish.
- Support your points with a verse or a reliable fact, and never make up numbers.
- End with a call to action, and write the essay in your own words instead of copying it.

## Questions students ask about pollution essays

### What is a short definition of pollution?

Pollution is harmful substances entering the air, water or soil in amounts that harm people, animals and plants, and upset the balance of the environment.

### Are there other types of pollution besides these five?

Yes. Alongside the five best-known types, some teachers also mention light pollution, caused by too much artificial light at night.

### How many paragraphs should an essay about pollution have?

At primary level, one to three paragraphs is enough. In middle and secondary school, aim for five to seven: an introduction, three or more body paragraphs and a conclusion.

### How do I write an essay about plastic pollution?

Use the same outline, but focus the body on plastic: where it comes from, how it reaches the sea, how it harms sea creatures, and solutions such as cloth bags and recycling.
