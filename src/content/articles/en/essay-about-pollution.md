Pollution is one of the most common topics teachers set for writing practice, so many students look for a well-organised **essay about pollution** written in clear language. Here is a complete model essay: an introduction, a body that covers the types, causes, effects and solutions of pollution, and a conclusion. After it you'll find a short version for primary pupils and some tips for writing the essay in your own words.

## Outline of an essay about pollution

Before you write, organise your ideas into clear points. A model essay follows this outline:

1. **Introduction:** what the environment and pollution are, and why the topic matters.
2. **Types of pollution:** air, water, soil, noise and plastic pollution.
3. **Causes:** vehicle and factory fumes, waste, pesticides and burning rubbish.
4. **Effects:** on human health, on animals and plants, and on the climate.
5. **Solutions:** what individuals, schools and governments can do.
6. **Conclusion:** a summary and a call to action.

## Introduction to the essay about pollution

The Earth gives us clean air to breathe, fresh water to drink and fertile soil to grow our food. Together these make up the **environment** we live in. But as people have used more and more resources and cared less about keeping their surroundings clean, they have upset much of this balance. The result is **environmental pollution**: harmful substances entering the air, water or soil in amounts that damage living things. The Quran describes this when it says: "Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned…" [Surat al-Rum, verse 41]. Because pollution affects every one of us, we need to understand its types and causes, and how we can protect our planet from it.

## Body: the main types of pollution

Pollution takes many forms. These are the best known:

| Type of pollution | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air pollution | Harmful gases, smoke and dust entering the air | Car exhausts, factory smoke, burning rubbish |
| Water pollution | Harmful substances mixing into rivers, seas and groundwater | Sewage, factory waste, oil spills |
| Soil pollution | Harmful substances building up in farmland | Too many pesticides and fertilisers, buried waste |
| Noise pollution | Loud, disturbing sounds that go on for a long time | Car horns, building machinery, loudspeakers |
| Plastic pollution | Plastic bags and bottles piling up on land and at sea | Bags, bottles and single-use items |

**Air pollution** is one of the most dangerous. The World Health Organization reports that about **99%** of the world's population breathes air that exceeds its recommended pollution limits. As for **plastic pollution**, the UN Environment Programme estimates that **19 to 23 million tonnes** of plastic leak into lakes, rivers and seas every year.

## Causes of pollution

Most pollution comes from human activity. The main causes are:

- **Smoke and exhaust fumes:** cars, factories and power stations burn fuel and release harmful gases.
- **Waste:** throwing rubbish into streets, valleys and beaches, or burning it in the open air.
- **Chemicals:** overusing farm pesticides and fertilisers, and pouring untreated factory waste into water.
- **Cutting down trees:** trees clean the air, so felling them takes away one of the environment's best defenders.
- **Wasteful habits:** buying things we don't need, and using plastic items once before throwing them away.

## The effects of pollution on people and nature

Pollution harms every kind of living thing:

- **Human health:** air pollution causes diseases of the lungs and heart, and the World Health Organization links it to about **7 million** early deaths worldwide each year. Polluted water also spreads many diseases.
- **Animals:** sea turtles and seabirds swallow pieces of plastic, mistaking them for food, and many fish die in polluted water.
- **Plants and soil:** polluted soil grows weaker crops, and harmful substances can find their way into our food.
- **Climate:** some polluting gases add to global warming, which we explain in our article [what is climate change](/en/articles/climate-change/).

> **Did you know?** Some plastic bottles take hundreds of years to break down in nature. Meanwhile they crumble into tiny pieces called "microplastics", which have been found in the deepest ocean trenches and in Arctic ice.

## How to reduce pollution

Protecting the environment is a shared responsibility, and everyone has a part to play:

- **Individuals:** put rubbish in the bin, use fewer plastic bags, carry a refillable water bottle, switch off devices you aren't using, and walk or take public transport whenever possible.
- **Schools:** organise clean-up and tree-planting campaigns, provide separate bins for paper and plastic, and hold a school assembly about the environment.
- **Governments and factories:** pass laws against dumping waste, treat sewage, and support clean energy such as solar and wind power.
- **Recycling:** turning used paper, glass and metal into new products means less waste and fewer resources used up.

## Conclusion of the essay about pollution

In conclusion, pollution threatens our health, our food, our water and the future of the generations to come, but it is not something we must simply accept. If each of us changed one small habit, such as putting a wrapper in the bin, saying no to a plastic bag we don't need, or planting a tree, our whole environment would change for the better. Let us look after the Earth we live on, because it has been placed in our care, and we have no other.

## Short essay about pollution for primary pupils

The environment is everything around us: the air, water, soil, plants and animals. Pollution is when harmful things such as smoke, rubbish and plastic get into the environment. There are different kinds of pollution, such as air pollution, water pollution, soil pollution and noise. Pollution is bad for people's health, and it hurts animals and plants. I can protect my environment by putting rubbish in the bin, keeping my classroom and school clean, planting a tree, and not wasting water or electricity. Let's all keep our environment clean and beautiful.

## Tips for writing an excellent essay about pollution

- Start with a short introduction that grabs the reader, then move on to the body.
- Give each idea its own paragraph: types, then causes, then effects, then solutions.
- Use linking words such as "on the other hand", "in addition" and "in conclusion".
- Support your points with a verse or a reliable fact, and never make up numbers.
- End with a call to action, and write the essay in your own words instead of copying it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a short definition of pollution?

Pollution is harmful substances entering the air, water or soil in amounts that harm people, animals and plants, and upset the balance of the environment.

### What are the types of pollution?

The five best-known types are air, water, soil, noise and plastic pollution. Some teachers also mention light pollution, caused by too much artificial light at night.

### How many paragraphs should an essay about pollution have?

At primary level, one to three paragraphs is enough. In middle and secondary school, aim for five to seven: an introduction, three or more body paragraphs and a conclusion.

### How do I write an essay about plastic pollution?

Use the same outline, but focus the body on plastic: where it comes from, how it reaches the sea, how it harms sea creatures, and solutions such as cloth bags and recycling.

## Key takeaway

A good **essay about pollution** opens by defining the environment and pollution, then sets out the types, causes, effects and solutions in well-ordered paragraphs, and ends with a call to action. Use this model to organise your ideas, add examples from your own town or village, and write it in your own words for the best mark.
