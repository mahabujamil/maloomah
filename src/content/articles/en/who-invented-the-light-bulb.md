We flick light switches dozens of times a day without a thought, but **who invented the light bulb**? The usual answer is the American **Thomas Edison** in 1879. The real story is longer and richer, with many scientists and inventors involved before and after him. Here it is.

## Who really invented the light bulb?

There's no single inventor. Making light from electricity was decades older than Edison. The real challenge was a bulb that would **last a long time, cost little and be safe for homes**. Two names rose to the top at almost the same moment:

- **Joseph Swan** (1828–1914), a British chemist and physicist, demonstrated a carbon-filament bulb in Newcastle around 1879, and homes and buildings in Britain were soon lit with it.
- **Thomas Edison** (1847–1931), an American inventor, developed a carbon-filament bulb at his Menlo Park laboratory that passed its key test in October 1879. He received his patent in January 1880.

That's why credit usually goes to both. Their British companies even merged in 1883 as "Ediswan".

## Before Edison: the early attempts

| Approx. year | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| Early 1800s | Britain's Humphry Davy demonstrates the arc lamp, a spark between two carbon rods |
| 1840 | Warren de la Rue tries a platinum coil in a vacuum tube, but platinum is too expensive |
| 1878–1879 | Joseph Swan shows his carbon-filament bulb |
| 1879 | Edison successfully tests a carbon-filament bulb lasting many hours |
| 1880 | Edison is granted his patent and moves to carbonised bamboo, which lasts far longer |

Arc lamps were extremely bright, so they lit streets and theatres, but they weren't practical for homes. Other early bulbs burned out within minutes.

## What exactly did Edison add?

Edison didn't just make a bulb. He thought about the **whole system**:

1. **The filament:** his lab tested thousands of materials before settling on carbon fibres, then carbonised bamboo that lasted hundreds of hours.
2. **The vacuum:** better pumps removed more air from the bulb so the filament didn't burn up.
3. **The grid:** he designed generators, wiring, meters and switches, and in 1882 opened the Pearl Street Station in New York to supply homes with power.

That's what turned the bulb from a lab invention into something in every home. To understand electricity itself, read [who invented electricity](/en/articles/who-invented-electricity/).

> **Did you know?** Lewis Latimer, an American engineer who later worked with Edison, patented an improved way of making carbon filaments in 1882, helping bulbs last longer and cost less.

## How the light bulb evolved after Edison

- **Tungsten filaments:** in the early 1900s tungsten replaced carbon because it tolerates higher temperatures and glows brighter. In 1910 William Coolidge found a way to draw it into fine wire.
- **Gas-filled bulbs:** filling bulbs with inert gas instead of a vacuum made filaments last longer.
- **Fluorescent lamps:** sold commercially from the late 1930s, they use far less energy.
- **LEDs:** Nick Holonyak created the first visible-light LED in 1962. In 2014, three Japanese-born scientists won the Nobel Prize in Physics for the blue LED, which made white LED lighting possible.

Many countries have now phased out traditional incandescent bulbs in favour of LEDs, because an incandescent bulb turns most of its energy, roughly 90%, into heat rather than light. Switching is one of the easiest ways to [save electricity at home](/en/articles/how-to-save-electricity-at-home/).

## Did Edison invent the light bulb?

Not alone, and not first. But Edison, alongside Swan, made the first practical, long-lasting bulbs, and Edison built the electrical system that made them useful. So "Edison invented the light bulb" is a simplification of a team effort spread over most of the 19th century.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who invented the light bulb and when?
Thomas Edison and Joseph Swan, working separately, around 1879. Edison's patent was granted in 1880.

### Was Edison the first to invent the light bulb?
No. Many inventors came before him, but he and Swan produced the first practical bulbs that lasted long enough for homes.

### Who invented the neon light?
French engineer Georges Claude, who demonstrated neon lamps in 1910.

### Who invented the LED light?
Nick Holonyak made the first visible-light LED in 1962, and many scientists developed the technology after him.

### Why are incandescent bulbs being phased out?
They waste most of their energy as heat, while LEDs use far less power and last much longer.

## Key takeaway

The **light bulb** grew out of many steps: Davy's arc lamp, then the first practical carbon-filament bulbs from Swan and Edison in 1879, with Edison also building a complete electricity system around them. Tungsten, fluorescent and finally LED lighting followed, which lights our homes today with far less energy.
