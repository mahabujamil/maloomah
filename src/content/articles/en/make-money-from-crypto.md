"Buy this coin now and it'll grow tenfold in a week!" Messages like that land in Telegram and WhatsApp groups every day, and the right reaction is suspicion. You can make money with crypto, and some people have. Far more have lost money, though, through not knowing enough, through greed, or through plain fraud. So the useful question isn't "how much could I make?" It's "what are the real methods, and what could each one cost me?" That is what the sections below try to answer honestly.

> **Important:** this article is for education only and is not investment advice. Crypto is one of the riskiest assets there is, and you could lose everything you put in.

## Understand it before you pay for it

If cryptocurrency and blockchain are still fuzzy to you, read [What Is Cryptocurrency?](/en/articles/what-is-cryptocurrency/) before thinking about profit. Someone who doesn't understand what they've bought can't tell an opportunity from a trap, and has no idea when to get out. That's why the oldest rule in investing applies here too: never put money into something you don't understand.

## How to make money with crypto, and how risky each method is

The methods work in different ways. Some depend on the price going up, some on rewards paid by a network or a platform, and only one doesn't require you to risk your own money at all.

### Long-term investing

You buy a well-known coin and hold it for years, hoping it becomes worth more. Because prices swing so violently, many people use **dollar-cost averaging**, buying a small fixed amount every month rather than putting in a lump sum, so they don't commit everything at a peak. That smooths out the swings without removing the danger. The risk is still high: prices can fall for years before they recover, and they may never recover.

### Day trading and speculation

Traders buy and sell quickly to catch price moves over hours or days. It looks tempting when people post their wins online, but it is one of the hardest approaches there is, and many studies suggest most individual traders lose money over time. The most dangerous version is **leveraged trading** (futures). Leverage magnifies losses as much as gains, so a small price move can wipe out your entire position. Overall, the risk is very high.

### Staking

Some coins let you "lock up" what you hold to help run the network, and you earn regular rewards in return. On the surface it resembles interest on a savings account, but the differences matter. The coin's price can drop by more than the reward you earned, your funds may be stuck for a set period, and the platform you use could fail. Risk ranges from medium to high.

### "Fixed return" and lending programmes

You deposit your coins with a platform that promises a yearly return. Several large platforms of this kind collapsed in 2022, and their users lost their money. The lesson keeps repeating: the higher the promised return, the bigger the risk, because a platform paying you a lot is gambling with your coins to do it. Risk is high.

### Mining

Miners run specialised machines that confirm transactions in exchange for rewards. For Bitcoin, that now takes expensive hardware and very cheap electricity. Someone running a rig in a flat in Amman or Cairo will usually find the power bill eats whatever the machine earns, so mining is no longer profitable for individuals at home in most cases.

### Working in the crypto industry

This is one of the safest options because you aren't investing anything. You sell your skills to crypto companies and projects instead: writing, design, programming, community management, marketing. In practice it's a form of [freelancing online](/en/articles/freelancing-for-beginners/). The risk is low, provided you check that whoever you work for is trustworthy.

## The most common crypto scams

Scammers know that most newcomers arrive hoping to get rich fast, and they build their tricks around that hope. These six come up again and again:

1. "Double your money": anyone promising guaranteed or large fixed profits is almost certainly running a scam.
2. Pump-and-dump groups: Telegram and WhatsApp groups hype a tiny coin, then the organisers sell and leave members holding the losses.
3. Fake platforms: websites that look real, show profits on screen, then refuse withdrawals.
4. Fake support agents: someone contacts you and asks for your "recovery phrase" so they can "help". That's outright theft.
5. Romance scams: someone builds a relationship with you online for weeks, then talks you into investing on a platform they control.
6. Anonymous new coins: projects launched with huge hype whose creators then vanish with the money.

> **Did you know?** Legitimate platforms will never ask for your recovery phrase or password. Anyone who does is a scammer, however convincing they sound.

## Rules that protect your money

None of these guarantees a profit, because nothing does. What they do is keep you clear of the mistakes beginners make most often:

1. Only invest money you could lose completely without it affecting your life.
2. Never borrow to invest in crypto.
3. Use only well-known, licensed platforms, and turn on two-factor authentication.
4. Never share your recovery phrase with anyone.
5. Keep large amounts in a cold wallet.
6. Avoid leverage if you're a beginner.
7. Ignore social media hype and do your own research.
8. Check the law where you live, since the rules vary widely between countries.

## Questions beginners ask first

### Can you make money from crypto without any capital?

Realistically, only by working in the industry as a designer, writer, developer or community manager. Offers of free coins for nothing are mostly worthless or scams.

### How much can I make from crypto?

Nobody can promise you a number. Profits are possible, but losses are just as possible, or more so, and anyone guaranteeing profits is lying.

### What's the best cryptocurrency to invest in?

There's no guaranteed answer. Large, established coins are relatively less risky than small new ones, but all of them are volatile.

Put together, the picture is clear enough: crypto profits are **neither easy nor guaranteed**. Small, long-term investing is less risky than day trading, working in the industry is the safest route, and "guaranteed profits" is the first sign of fraud. If you do go in, learn first, start small, and never risk money you need.
