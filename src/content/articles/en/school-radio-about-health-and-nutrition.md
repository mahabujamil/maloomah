The food we eat each morning is the fuel that powers our bodies and minds through the school day, and the eating habits we learn as children tend to stay with us for life. That makes a **school assembly on health and nutrition**, the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio", one of the most useful assemblies students can present at any time of year. Below is a complete, ready-to-read script: an introduction, Quran and authentic hadith segments, a morning talk on healthy eating, "Did you know?" facts from reliable sources, a short story, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing out the parts.

## Running order for a health and nutrition assembly

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Drop or add a segment to fit the time you have.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | One student |
| 4 | Morning talk on healthy food | 2 min | One student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 2 min | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Story: "Sara's school bag" | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 7 | Quick nutrition tips | 1 min | Three students |
| 8 | Assembly quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

## Introduction to the assembly

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God who gave us food and drink, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.
> Good morning, and welcome to a morning of health and energy.
> Our respected head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning to you all.
> They say "a healthy mind lives in a healthy body", and a healthy body starts with a balanced plate, a glass of water and a breakfast we never skip. Today's assembly is about **health and nutrition**: how to choose our food, why our bodies need fruit and vegetables, and how to look after the blessing of good health.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran segment: eating good things

The student recites one or more of these verses. The Arabic text is taken from quran.com, and the English meaning is from the Saheeh International translation. It's best for the student to read the Arabic from a printed Mushaf.

> "O you who have believed, eat from the good [i.e., lawful] things which We have provided for you and be grateful to Allāh if it is [indeed] Him that you worship." [Surah Al-Baqarah 2:172]

> "O children of Adam, take your adornment [i.e., wear your clothing] at every masjid, and eat and drink, but be not excessive. Indeed, He likes not those who commit excess." [Surah Al-A'raf 7:31]

> "Then let mankind look at his food." [Surah Abasa 80:24]

**Host after the recitation:** And now the hadith, presented by ……

## Hadith segment: the blessing of health

> Ibn Abbas (may God be pleased with them both) reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said: "There are two blessings which many people lose: health and free time." [Sahih al-Bukhari 6412]

**Meaning in brief:** Health and free time are two great blessings that many people don't value until they have wasted them. A student who protects their health with good food, enough sleep and exercise, and who uses their time for learning and useful work, shows gratitude for both blessings through their actions.

## Morning talk on healthy food

> Dear friends,
> A car won't move without fuel, and a phone switches off when its battery runs flat. Our bodies are the same: they need energy, and that energy comes from food. But not all food gives us the same kind of energy.
> Healthy food means a balanced diet: bread, rice and grains for energy; beans, meat, eggs and fish to build our muscles; milk and dairy to strengthen our bones and teeth; and fruit and vegetables, full of vitamins and fibre.
> The first step of the day is **breakfast**. A student who eats even a simple breakfast before school starts the day with more energy and better focus than one who arrives on an empty stomach.
> Let's also think about what we buy from the school canteen. Fizzy drinks, sweets and salty crisps taste good, but too much of them harms our teeth and our bodies. Water is the best drink of all, and we should always wash our hands before and after eating.
> So let today's motto be: "My food is my health, and my health is my success."

## "Did you know?" facts about health and nutrition

Two students read these facts in turn. They all come from reliable sources such as the World Health Organization (WHO):

- Did you know that the **World Health Organization** recommends eating at least **400 grams** of fruit and vegetables a day, about five portions? Potatoes and other starchy roots don't count towards this.
- Did you know that the WHO recommends adults eat less than **5 grams of salt a day**, about one teaspoon?
- Did you know that it recommends keeping **free sugars** (sugars added to food and drink, plus those in honey and fruit juice) below **10%** of daily energy, and that going below 5% brings extra benefits?
- Did you know that **water** makes up more than half of an adult's body weight? That's why we need to drink throughout the day.
- Did you know that **sunlight** helps the skin make **vitamin D**, which the body needs to absorb calcium and build strong bones?
- Did you know that the **fibre** in vegetables, fruit and whole grains helps the gut work regularly?
- Did you know that oranges, lemons and sweet peppers are rich in **vitamin C**, and that the body can't store large amounts of it, so it needs a steady supply from food?

> **Did you know?** The WHO works out that 10% of a 2,000-calorie day is about 50 grams of sugar, roughly 12 level teaspoons. A single can of fizzy drink can supply a large share of that on its own.

To find out how much water your body needs, read our lesson on [how much water you should drink](/en/articles/how-much-water-should-you-drink/), and follow food's journey through the body in [the human digestive system](/en/articles/human-digestive-system/).

## Short story: Sara's school bag

> Every day, Sara felt tired and sleepy by third period and couldn't concentrate in maths. Her teacher asked her kindly, "What did you have for breakfast, Sara?" Sara replied, "Nothing. I wake up late, and I buy crisps and a fizzy drink from the canteen."
> The teacher suggested a simple two-week experiment: go to bed earlier, eat a light breakfast of bread with cheese or an egg, and pack an apple and a bottle of water instead of sweets.
> After two weeks, Sara noticed the difference herself. She had more energy, joined in eagerly in third period, and even started encouraging her friends to take the "healthy bag challenge".
> **The lesson:** small changes in our daily eating habits make a big difference to our energy and focus.

## Quick nutrition tips for students

Each student reads two tips:

1. Don't leave home without breakfast, even a simple one.
2. Fill half your plate with vegetables and fruit.
3. Drink water instead of fizzy drinks and sweetened juices.
4. Cut down on fried and very salty foods.
5. Wash your hands with soap and water before and after eating.
6. Move and play for an hour a day, and get enough sleep.

## Assembly quiz on food and health

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |

| Which vitamin does the skin make with the help of sunlight? | Vitamin D |
| About how many portions of fruit and vegetables are recommended each day? | About five (at least 400 grams) |
| Which mineral, plentiful in milk, strengthens bones and teeth? | Calcium |
| Which meal should students not skip before school? | Breakfast |
| Name a fruit rich in vitamin C. | Orange, lemon or guava |

## Closing the health and nutrition assembly

> To close our assembly, let's remember that good health is a crown that only the sick can see, and that looking after it starts with small choices we make every day: breakfast, a colourful plate of fruit and vegetables, and a glass of water instead of a fizzy drink.
> Thank you for listening, thank you to our teachers for their support, and we wish you all an active and successful school day.
> Peace be upon you.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start a school assembly on health and nutrition?

Begin with the basmala and a greeting, then a line linking food to energy and focus at school, such as "a healthy mind lives in a healthy body". Then introduce the Quran segment, as in the ready-made introduction above.

### What are the main segments of a healthy eating assembly?

Introduction, Quran, hadith, morning talk, "Did you know?", a story, a quiz and a closing. You can also add quick tips or show a real "healthy plate" to the students.

### Can I use this assembly with primary pupils?

Yes. For younger classes, shorten the morning talk, swap the numbers in the facts segment for simpler points, and run the quiz with pictures: a fruit, a vegetable and a glass of water.

### Does this assembly replace a doctor's advice?

No. It gives general information about healthy eating from reliable sources. Anyone with a particular health condition or food allergy should follow their doctor's guidance.

## Key takeaway

A **school assembly on health and nutrition** teaches students that a balanced diet, water and a morning breakfast are the foundations of energy and focus. Use the segments above as they are or adapt them to your students' age, and add more facts from our [health "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/health/) to make the assembly even richer.
