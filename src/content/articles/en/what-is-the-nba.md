What is the NBA? It's the league behind those late-night basketball highlights that fill your feed every autumn, when a new season tips off and searches climb everywhere from the US to Saudi Arabia and across the Arab world. If you've ever watched a game and wondered why there are so many of them, or what a "play-in" is, this guide walks through the whole thing from the start.

## What is the NBA?

NBA stands for the **National Basketball Association**, the world's best-known and strongest professional basketball league. It has 30 teams: 29 in the United States and one in Canada, the Toronto Raptors.

The league was founded in New York on 6 June 1946 as the Basketball Association of America (BAA). In 1949 it absorbed teams from a rival league and took the name NBA. Since then it has become the place every top player wants to be, and rosters now include players from every continent.

## East and West: how the teams are organised

The 30 teams are split into two conferences of 15 teams each, the Eastern Conference and the Western Conference. Each conference is then divided into three divisions of five teams, grouped mostly by geography. That makes practical sense in a country as wide as the US, where keeping neighbours together cuts down on long trips.

## The season: October to June

An NBA season is long, and it moves through four main stages:

| Stage | Roughly when | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular season | October to April | Each team plays 82 games |
| NBA Cup | November and December | An in-season tournament; most of its games count in the standings |
| Play-In Tournament | Mid-April | Teams seeded 7 to 10 in each conference fight for two spots |
| Playoffs and Finals | April to June | 16 teams, every series best of seven |

When the regular season ends, the top six teams in each conference go straight to the playoffs. Seeds seven and eight are settled by the **play-in**, a last chance for teams that finished just short.

Four rounds follow, and each series goes to the first team to **four wins**. A best-of-seven format takes a lot of luck out of it: a strong team can drop one game, but losing four is much harder. Eventually the East and West champions meet in the NBA Finals, and the winner lifts the Larry O'Brien Trophy.

## Key NBA rules

Each team has five players on the court, with unlimited substitutions. A game is four 12-minute quarters, 48 minutes in all, which is longer than international games at 40 minutes.

There are no draws. If the score is tied, the teams play five-minute overtime periods until somebody wins.

Points depend on where you shoot from: two inside the arc, three from beyond it, and one for a free throw. A team has a 24-second shot clock on each possession. And players have to watch their fouls, because six personal fouls means fouling out of the game.

If you enjoy understanding the rules of other sports, see our guide to [football's offside rule](/en/articles/offside-rule-explained/).

> **Did you know?** The **24-second shot clock** didn't exist in the league's early years, and some teams would simply hold the ball instead of shooting. When it was introduced in 1954, scoring jumped sharply and the game became faster and far more exciting.

## Questions new fans ask

### When does the NBA season start?

The regular season usually starts in the second half of October, and the Finals normally end in June of the following year. Because a season spans two calendar years, it's written as something like "2026–27".

### Why are NBA games on so early in the Middle East?

Most games are played in the evening in North America, which lines up with the early-morning hours in the Gulf, the Levant and Egypt because of the time difference.

### What is the NBA Cup?

An in-season tournament launched in 2023. All 30 teams play in groups, then a knockout bracket leads to a final. Results of most of its games also count toward the regular-season standings, so they're far from friendlies.

### How many games are in an NBA season?

Each team plays 82 regular-season games: 41 at home and 41 away. Once you know the shape of it, from the regular season through the play-in and the playoff series to the Finals, following the league gets a lot more fun.
