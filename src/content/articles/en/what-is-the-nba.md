Every autumn a new season tips off and the **NBA** shoots up the search charts, from the US to Saudi Arabia and the UAE. But what is the NBA exactly, how does its long season work, and what are the rules that make it different? Here is a clear, beginner-friendly guide.

## What is the NBA?

**NBA** stands for the **National Basketball Association**, the world's best-known and strongest professional basketball league. It has **30 teams**: 29 in the United States and one in Canada, the **Toronto Raptors**.

The league was founded in New York on **6 June 1946** as the Basketball Association of America (BAA). In **1949** it absorbed teams from a rival league and took the name NBA. Since then it has become the destination for top players from every continent.

## How the league is organised

The 30 teams are split into two **conferences** of 15 teams each:

- the **Eastern Conference**
- the **Western Conference**

Each conference is divided into **three divisions** of five teams, grouped mostly by geography.

## The season: October to June

An NBA season has four main stages:

| Stage | Roughly when | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular season | October to April | Each team plays **82 games** |
| NBA Cup | November and December | An in-season tournament; most of its games count in the standings |
| Play-In Tournament | Mid-April | Teams seeded 7 to 10 in each conference fight for two spots |
| Playoffs and Finals | April to June | 16 teams, every series best of seven |

The top **six teams** in each conference go straight to the playoffs, and the **play-in** decides seeds seven and eight. Four rounds follow, each won by the first team to **four wins**, until the East and West champions meet in the **NBA Finals**. The winner lifts the **Larry O'Brien Trophy**.

## Key NBA rules

- **Five players** per team on the court, with unlimited substitutions.
- **Four 12-minute quarters**, 48 minutes in all (international games last 40 minutes).
- **Five-minute overtime** periods if the score is tied, repeated until there is a winner. Basketball has no draws.
- A **24-second shot clock** for each possession.
- **Scoring:** two points inside the arc, three from beyond it, one for a free throw.
- **Six personal fouls** and a player fouls out of the game.

If you enjoy understanding the rules of other sports, see our guide to [football's offside rule](/en/articles/offside-rule-explained/).

> **Did you know?** The **24-second shot clock** didn't exist in the league's early years, and some teams would simply hold the ball instead of shooting. When it was introduced in **1954**, scoring jumped sharply and the game became faster and far more exciting.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does the NBA season start?

The regular season usually starts in **the second half of October**, and the Finals normally end in **June** of the following year. That is why seasons are written with two years, such as "2026–27".

### Why are NBA games on so early in the Middle East?

Most games are played in the evening in North America, which lines up with the early-morning hours in the Gulf, the Levant and Egypt because of the time difference.

### What is the NBA Cup?

An in-season tournament launched in **2023**. All 30 teams play in groups, then a knockout bracket leads to a final. Results of most of its games also count toward the regular-season standings.

### How many games are in an NBA season?

Each team plays **82 regular-season games**: 41 at home and 41 away.

## Key takeaway

The NBA is North America's professional basketball league, founded in 1946, with 30 teams in two conferences. Its season runs from October to June: 82 regular-season games, then the play-in and best-of-seven playoffs, all the way to the Finals and the Larry O'Brien Trophy. Once you know that simple structure, following the league becomes much more fun.
