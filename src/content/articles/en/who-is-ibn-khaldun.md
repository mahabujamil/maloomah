Ask a history or social studies teacher to name the greatest Arab historian and the answer is very often **Ibn Khaldun**. So who was Ibn Khaldun? He was a Tunisian-born scholar of the fourteenth century who wrote the *Muqaddimah*, a book many researchers believe laid the foundations of **sociology** centuries before the subject took shape in Europe. This article gives a short account of his life, his main books and his key ideas in a way students can follow, with a timeline table and frequently asked questions.

## Who was Ibn Khaldun? Quick facts

| Fact | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn Khaldun, known as Abu Zayd and given the title Wali al-Din |
| Born | Tunis, 27 May 1332 (Ramadan 732 AH) |
| Died | Cairo, March 1406 (808 AH) |
| Family origin | An Arab family from Hadramawt in Yemen that settled in Seville in al-Andalus and later moved to Tunis |
| Best-known works | *Kitab al-'Ibar*, its introduction the *Muqaddimah*, and his autobiography *al-Ta'rif* |
| Fields | History, sociology, politics, economics and Maliki law |

## Ibn Khaldun's childhood and education in Tunis

Ibn Khaldun was born in **Tunis** in 1332 into a family known for scholarship and public office. His ancestors came from Yemen and lived for centuries in **Seville**, in al-Andalus, before moving to North Africa ahead of the city's fall to the Spanish kingdoms in the thirteenth century.

As a boy he studied with his father and the scholars of Tunis. He memorised the Quran and learned hadith, law, the Arabic language and poetry, and later logic and philosophy. When he was about seventeen, the **Black Death** swept the Mediterranean in 1348 and took both his parents and many of his teachers. That hardship deeply shaped the way he later thought about how states and societies rise and fall.

## Life between the Maghreb, al-Andalus and Egypt

Ibn Khaldun moved often and served several rulers:

1. **Fez, Morocco:** he worked as a secretary and adviser to the Marinid sultans.
2. **Granada, al-Andalus:** its sultan sent him as an envoy to the King of Castile in Seville in 1364, the very city his ancestors had lived in.
3. **Bejaia and the central Maghreb (today's Algeria):** he held administrative and political posts.
4. **Qal'at Ibn Salama (in today's Algeria):** he withdrew from politics for about four years and wrote the *Muqaddimah* there, finishing his first draft in 1377.
5. **Cairo:** he moved there in 1382, taught at al-Azhar and other colleges, and was appointed **chief Maliki judge** by the Mamluk sultan Barquq, a post he held several times until his death.

One of the most remarkable episodes of his life came in 1401, when he met the Mongol-Turkic conqueror **Timur (Tamerlane)** outside Damascus while Timur's armies were besieging the city. He recorded the meeting in detail in his autobiography.

## The Muqaddimah: the book that made him famous

Ibn Khaldun wrote a huge history of the world called ***Kitab al-'Ibar*** ("The Book of Lessons"). He opened it with a long introduction explaining his method for understanding history and society. That introduction became so famous that it is read as a book in its own right: the ***Muqaddimah***, which simply means "Introduction".

What made it new? Ibn Khaldun didn't just list events, as many earlier historians had done. He asked **why** states rise and flourish, **why** they weaken and fall, and how the environment, the economy and customs shape people's lives. He called this new field **'ilm al-'umran**, "the science of human civilisation", which roughly matches what we now call sociology.

> **Did you know?** The British historian Arnold Toynbee called the *Muqaddimah* "a philosophy of history which is undoubtedly the greatest work of its kind that has ever yet been created by any mind in any time or place."

## Ibn Khaldun's key ideas made simple

| Idea | Simple explanation |
| --- | --- |
| *Asabiyyah* (group solidarity) | The bond of loyalty and cooperation within a group, which he saw as the force that builds states |
| The life cycle of states | A state goes through stages like a human life: birth, strength, luxury and decline, often lasting about three generations |
| Desert and city life | Desert peoples tend to be tougher and more united, while city dwellers have more learning and crafts, but luxury weakens societies |
| Checking reports | A historian shouldn't believe every report, but should test it against the nature of things and ask whether it could really have happened |
| Work and wealth | Human labour is the real source of wealth and earnings |
| Taxes | When taxes become too heavy, people produce less, so in the end the state collects less |

One of his most quoted lines, which many students look up, is the title of a chapter in the *Muqaddimah*: **"The vanquished always want to imitate the victor in his distinctive marks, his dress, his beliefs and his other customs and habits."** In other words, defeated peoples tend to copy the stronger side, believing its strength comes from those habits.

He also wrote about language, arguing that it is a skill acquired through practice and repetition, as you'll find among our [Did You Know facts about Arabic](/en/did-you-know/arabic/).

## Ibn Khaldun's place in the world today

Ibn Khaldun is still very much present today:

- His ideas are taught at universities around the world in history, sociology and economics.
- The *Muqaddimah* has been translated into many languages, including English and French.
- His statue stands on the best-known avenue in central Tunis, and schools, universities and research centres in many countries carry his name.
- Many researchers see him as a pioneer of sociology and one of the earliest writers on the philosophy of history and on economics.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who was Ibn Khaldun, in short?

Ibn Khaldun (1332–1406) was an Arab historian and scholar, born in Tunis and buried in Cairo. He wrote the *Muqaddimah*, in which he set out the "science of civilisation", and is therefore seen as a pioneer of sociology.

### Where did Ibn Khaldun write the Muqaddimah?

He wrote it at **Qal'at Ibn Salama**, in what is now Algeria, after withdrawing from politics. He finished the first draft in 1377 and kept revising and adding to it after he moved to Cairo.

### What are Ibn Khaldun's most famous books?

The best known are *Kitab al-'Ibar*, his history of the world, with its famous introduction, the *Muqaddimah*, and his autobiography, *al-Ta'rif bi-Ibn Khaldun*. Other works on law and logic are also attributed to him.

### Why is Ibn Khaldun called a founder of sociology?

Because he studied human society systematically: how people come together, how states rise and fall, and how the environment and the economy shape their lives. He called this "the science of civilisation", about four centuries before modern sociology appeared in Europe.

### What does asabiyyah mean in Ibn Khaldun's thought?

*Asabiyyah* is the bond of solidarity and loyalty that holds a group together and makes its members cooperate and defend one another. In his view it is the foundation of a state's rise and strength.

## Key takeaway

**Ibn Khaldun** was an Arab scholar born in Tunis in 1332 who died in Cairo in 1406. He moved between the Maghreb, al-Andalus and Egypt, and wrote the *Muqaddimah*, where he founded the "science of civilisation" and explained ideas such as *asabiyyah*, the life cycle of states and the critical testing of historical reports. That is why he ranks among the greatest historians of all time and is seen as a pioneer of modern sociology.
