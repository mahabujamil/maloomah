Most classroom quarrels end the same way: someone says sorry, someone else lets it go, and by the next break they're playing together again. A **school assembly on tolerance** takes that everyday moment and puts it in front of the whole school. The best week for it is the one around **16 November**, when the world marks the International Day for Tolerance, but the message suits any morning. The script below is written for the programme that Arab schools call "school radio", and it's complete: an introduction, Quran verses, hadith, a morning talk, "Did you know?" facts, a short story, sayings, a quiz and a closing.

## Running order for a school assembly on tolerance

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes, and you can drop or add a segment to fit the time. Spreading the parts across as many students as you can gives more of them a turn at the microphone.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | One student |
| 4 | Morning talk | 2 min | One student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 min | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Short story | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 7 | Sayings about tolerance | 1 min | Three students |
| 8 | Assembly quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

## Introduction

> Good morning, and a morning full of kindness to everyone.
> Dear head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, we hope you are all well.
> Today we want to talk about a quality that turns a school into one home and a classroom into a small family: tolerance. On 16 November the world celebrates the International Day for Tolerance, and we can celebrate it every day, whenever we smile at a classmate, apologise for a mistake, or forgive someone who has hurt us.
> We begin our assembly with a recitation from the Holy Quran by our classmate …

## Quran verses about tolerance

Each of these verses carries a different side of tolerance: getting to know other peoples, answering bad with good, and pardoning when you're angry. The student recites one or more of them in Arabic, ideally reading from the mushaf. The meanings below are from the Saheeh International translation on quran.com:

> "O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Aware." (Surah Al-Hujurat, 49:13)

> "And not equal are the good deed and the bad. Repel [evil] by that [deed] which is better; and thereupon, the one whom between you and him is enmity [will become] as though he was a devoted friend." (Surah Fussilat, 41:34)

> "Who spend [in the cause of Allah] during ease and hardship and who restrain anger and who pardon the people - and Allah loves the doers of good." (Surah Al Imran, 3:134)

## Hadith about tolerance

> Jabir ibn Abdullah reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "May Allah have mercy on a man who is easy-going when he sells, when he buys and when he asks for what he is owed." (Sahih al-Bukhari 2076)

> Abu Hurairah reported that the Prophet (peace be upon him) said, in meaning: "Charity does not decrease wealth; Allah increases a servant who forgives only in honour; and no one humbles himself for Allah's sake but Allah raises him." (Sahih Muslim 2588)

A sentence or two of explanation helps here. The first hadith asks for mercy on people who are gentle and easy to deal with, whether they're buying, selling or collecting a debt. The second turns a common idea on its head: forgiving others never makes you weaker; it raises your honour.

## Morning talk on tolerance

> Dear friends,
> Tolerance is not weakness, as some people think. It is strength. Anyone can get angry and hit back. It takes real strength to stay calm and forgive.
> At school, tolerance looks like many small things: respecting a classmate whose opinion, accent or country is different from ours; never mocking anyone for their looks, clothes or marks; forgiving a classmate who bumped into us by accident in the line; and having the courage to say sorry when we are the ones who got it wrong.
> A class where tolerance rules is a calm class where everyone learns. A school full of respect is a school students look forward to every morning.
> So let our motto today be: "I may disagree with you, but I respect you."

## "Did you know?" facts about tolerance

Two students take turns, one fact each:

- Did you know that UNESCO's member states adopted the Declaration of Principles on Tolerance on 16 November 1995?
- Did you know that in 1996 the UN General Assembly invited countries to observe the International Day for Tolerance every year on 16 November?
- Did you know that the United Nations declared 1995 the United Nations Year for Tolerance?
- Did you know that UNESCO awards an international prize for promoting tolerance and non-violence, called the UNESCO-Madanjeet Singh Prize?
- Did you know that the Arabic word for tolerance, *tasamuh*, comes from the root s-m-h, which also gives *samaha*: generosity and gentleness?
- Did you know that a sincere apology, and accepting one, is among the quickest ways to end a quarrel between friends?

> **Did you know?** UNESCO's declaration says tolerance does not mean giving up your principles. It means respecting, accepting and appreciating the rich diversity of the world's cultures.

## A short story about tolerance

Students pay attention to stories that look like their own school day. This one was written for the assembly; it isn't a real event.

> The torn sketchbook
> Sami loved the sketchbook his grandmother had given him. In art class, his classmate Khaled tripped as he walked past, a cup of water spilled over the sketchbook, and one of its pages tore.
> Sami's face went red. He was about to shout at Khaled, but he saw how upset Khaled looked and heard him say, "I'm so sorry, Sami. I didn't mean it."
> Sami was quiet for a moment, then said, "It's all right, Khaled. Accidents happen to everyone."
> The next day Khaled came to school with a beautiful drawing. "I made this for you to stick in place of the torn page," he said. From that day on, Sami and Khaled were the best of friends.
> The lesson: saying "I forgive you" might cost you a page in a sketchbook, but it can win you a friend.

## Short sayings about tolerance

Each student reads one line, loudly and clearly, with a short pause before the next:

1. Tolerance is a bridge from disagreement to friendship.
2. A forgiving heart sleeps peacefully.
3. Say sorry when you are wrong, and forgive when you are wronged.
4. We can disagree and still be friends.
5. Our colours and languages differ, but we are equal in our humanity.

## Assembly quiz on tolerance

Most answers come straight from the assembly, and the last question is open so everyone can join in. The host asks, and students raise their hands:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| On what date is the International Day for Tolerance? | 16 November |
| Which organisation adopted the Declaration of Principles on Tolerance in 1995? | UNESCO |
| What do you say when you have wronged a classmate? | Sorry (an apology) |
| Finish the saying: "We can disagree and still …" | be friends |
| Name one act of tolerance at school | e.g. accepting a classmate's apology, or never mocking anyone |

## Closing

> To end our assembly, let's remember that tolerance is not a word we say in the morning line and then forget. It is how we behave in class, in the playground and at home.
> Let's start today: a smile for a classmate, an apology for a mistake, and forgiveness when we feel angry.
> Thank you all for listening, and have a wonderful day.

## What teachers ask before running this assembly

### When is the International Day for Tolerance?

It falls on **16 November** every year. UNESCO adopted its Declaration of Principles on Tolerance on that date in 1995, and a year later the UN General Assembly invited countries to mark it annually.

### How long should a school assembly be?

Usually 10 to 15 minutes. When time is tight, the introduction, the recitation, the morning talk, the "Did you know?" segment and the closing still carry the whole message.

### Is this assembly suitable for primary pupils?

Yes. The story, the short sayings and the quiz are well within reach of younger pupils; for the first grades, simplify the morning talk and cut it down.

### How can I make the assembly stand out?

Rehearsal matters most. Run through it with the students at least a day before, because a confident speaker is heard clearly and doesn't stumble in front of the whole school. Give parts to as many students as you can, and add a simple visual such as a large poster reading "I may disagree with you, but I respect you".
