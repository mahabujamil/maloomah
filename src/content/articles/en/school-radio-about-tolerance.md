Every year on **16 November** the world marks the **International Day for Tolerance**. It's a perfect week for a **school assembly on tolerance**, the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio", reminding students about respect, forgiveness and accepting people who are different. Below is a complete, ready-to-read script: an introduction, a morning talk, a "Did you know?" segment, a short story, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing the parts among students.

## Running order for a school assembly on tolerance

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Teachers can drop or add a segment to fit the time.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Morning talk | 2 min | One student |
| 4 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 min | Two students taking turns |
| 5 | Short story | 2 min | A confident reader |
| 6 | Sayings about tolerance | 1 min | Three students |
| 7 | Assembly quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 8 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

> **Teacher's note:** for the recitation segment, the student chooses suitable verses together with the Islamic education teacher and reads them directly from the mushaf.

## Introduction

> Good morning, and a morning full of kindness to everyone.
> Dear head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, we hope you are all well.
> Today we want to talk about a quality that turns a school into one home and a classroom into a small family: **tolerance**. On 16 November the world celebrates the International Day for Tolerance, and we can celebrate it every day, whenever we smile at a classmate, apologise for a mistake, or forgive someone who has hurt us.
> We begin our assembly with a recitation from the Holy Quran by our classmate …

## Morning talk on tolerance

> Dear friends,
> Tolerance is not weakness, as some people think. It is strength. Anyone can get angry and hit back. It takes real strength to stay calm and forgive.
> At school, tolerance looks like many small things: respecting a classmate whose opinion, accent or country is different from ours; never mocking anyone for their looks, clothes or marks; forgiving a classmate who bumped into us by accident in the line; and having the courage to say sorry when we are the ones who got it wrong.
> A class where tolerance rules is a calm class where everyone learns. A school full of respect is a school students look forward to every morning.
> So let our motto today be: "I may disagree with you, but I respect you."

## "Did you know?" facts about tolerance

Two students can read these in turn:

- **Did you know** that UNESCO's member states adopted the Declaration of Principles on Tolerance on 16 November 1995?
- **Did you know** that in 1996 the UN General Assembly invited countries to observe the International Day for Tolerance every year on 16 November?
- **Did you know** that the United Nations declared 1995 the United Nations Year for Tolerance?
- **Did you know** that UNESCO awards an international prize for promoting tolerance and non-violence, called the UNESCO-Madanjeet Singh Prize?
- **Did you know** that the Arabic word for tolerance, *tasamuh*, comes from the root s-m-h, which also gives *samaha*: generosity and gentleness?
- **Did you know** that a sincere apology, and accepting one, is among the quickest ways to end a quarrel between friends?

> **Did you know?** UNESCO's declaration says tolerance does not mean giving up your principles. It means respecting, accepting and appreciating the rich diversity of the world's cultures.

## A short story about tolerance

> **The torn sketchbook**
> Sami loved the sketchbook his grandmother had given him. In art class, his classmate Khaled tripped as he walked past, a cup of water spilled over the sketchbook, and one of its pages tore.
> Sami's face went red. He was about to shout at Khaled, but he saw how upset Khaled looked and heard him say, "I'm so sorry, Sami. I didn't mean it."
> Sami was quiet for a moment, then said, "It's all right, Khaled. Accidents happen to everyone."
> The next day Khaled came to school with a beautiful drawing. "I made this for you to stick in place of the torn page," he said. From that day on, Sami and Khaled were the best of friends.
> **The lesson:** saying "I forgive you" might cost you a page in a sketchbook, but it can win you a friend.

## Short sayings about tolerance

Each student reads one line, loudly and clearly:

1. Tolerance is a bridge from disagreement to friendship.
2. A forgiving heart sleeps peacefully.
3. Say sorry when you are wrong, and forgive when you are wronged.
4. We can disagree and still be friends.
5. Our colours and languages differ, but we are equal in our humanity.

## Assembly quiz on tolerance

The host asks the questions and students raise their hands to answer:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| On what date is the International Day for Tolerance? | 16 November |
| Which organisation adopted the Declaration of Principles on Tolerance in 1995? | UNESCO |
| What do you say when you have wronged a classmate? | Sorry (an apology) |
| Finish the saying: "We can disagree and still …" | be friends |
| Name one act of tolerance at school | e.g. accepting a classmate's apology, or never mocking anyone |

## Closing

> To close our assembly, let's remember that tolerance is not a word we say in the morning line and then forget. It is how we behave in class, in the playground and at home.
> Let's start today: a smile for a classmate, an apology for a mistake, and forgiveness when we feel angry.
> Thank you all for listening, and have a wonderful day.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the International Day for Tolerance?

It falls on **16 November** every year. UNESCO adopted its Declaration of Principles on Tolerance on that date in 1995, and in 1996 the UN General Assembly invited countries to mark it every year.

### How long should a school assembly be?

Usually 10 to 15 minutes. If time is short, keep the introduction, the recitation, the morning talk, the "Did you know?" segment and the closing.

### Is this assembly suitable for primary pupils?

Yes. The story, the short sayings and the quiz suit younger pupils, and the morning talk can be simplified and shortened for the first grades.

### How can I make the assembly stand out?

Rehearse with the students at least a day before, give parts to as many students as possible, and add a visual such as a large poster reading "I may disagree with you, but I respect you". Our guide to [effective study methods](/en/articles/effective-study-methods/) can help students memorise their parts quickly.

## Key takeaway

A **school assembly on tolerance** teaches students that respect, forgiveness and accepting differences are strengths, not weaknesses. Use the ready-made segments above as they are or adapt them for your school, and present them in the week of **16 November** to mark the International Day for Tolerance.
