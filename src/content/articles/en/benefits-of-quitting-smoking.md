Many smokers assume the damage is done and there's no point stopping now. The opposite is true. The **benefits of quitting smoking** start within minutes of your last cigarette and keep building for years, whatever your age and however long you've smoked. Here's what happens in your body step by step, what quitting does for your skin, mood and wallet, and how to get through withdrawal.

## Benefits of quitting smoking: the timeline

The **World Health Organization** and the UK's **NHS** publish similar timelines of what happens after you stop. Here's a summary:

| Time since your last cigarette | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **20 minutes** | Heart rate and blood pressure drop (WHO) |
| **8 hours** | Carbon monoxide and oxygen levels in the blood start returning to normal (NHS) |
| **12 hours** | Carbon monoxide in the blood drops to normal (WHO) |
| **48 hours** | Taste and smell improve (NHS) |
| **72 hours** | Breathing feels easier and energy may rise (NHS) |
| **2 to 12 weeks** | Circulation and lung function improve (WHO) |
| **1 to 9 months** | Coughing and shortness of breath decrease (WHO) |
| **1 year** | Risk of coronary heart disease is about half a smoker's (WHO) |
| **5 to 15 years** | Stroke risk falls toward that of a non-smoker (WHO) |
| **10 years** | Risk of dying from lung cancer is about half a smoker's (WHO) |
| **15 years** | Coronary heart disease risk is close to a non-smoker's (WHO) |

Details vary from person to person.

## After 1 week and after 1 month

**After a week,** the extra carbon monoxide is gone from your blood. Many people notice food tastes better, smells are sharper and stairs feel a little easier.

**After a month,** the hardest part of withdrawal has passed for most people, and circulation and lung function are starting to improve, so moving around takes less effort. It's a good moment to add some light activity: the [benefits of walking](/en/articles/benefits-of-walking/) every day help you swap an old habit for a healthy one.

## Benefits of quitting smoking for your body

- **Heart and blood vessels:** smoking narrows blood vessels and raises the risk of clots. Quitting gradually eases the load on your [heart](/en/articles/human-heart-facts/).
- **Lungs:** less coughing and phlegm, and better lung capacity over time.
- **Lifespan:** a large US study published in 2013 in the New England Journal of Medicine found that smokers lose about 10 years of life expectancy, and that quitting before 40 removes about 90% of the extra risk of death linked to continued smoking.
- **For men:** the NHS notes that smoking harms blood flow and can reduce fertility. Quitting improves circulation throughout the body.

## Benefits of quitting smoking on skin

Smoking cuts the supply of blood and oxygen to your skin, and its chemicals damage the collagen and elastin that keep skin firm. That's why smoking is linked to early wrinkles and a dull complexion. Once you quit, blood flow to the skin improves, and many people notice a healthier colour within weeks. The smell of smoke also leaves your hair, fingers and clothes. Wrinkles that have already formed won't all disappear, though: the main gain is slowing down premature ageing.

## Mental health benefits

Many smokers feel a cigarette calms them. The NHS explains that this feeling is mostly short-lived relief from nicotine withdrawal itself. Research suggests that, after a few weeks, people who quit feel less anxiety, stress and depression than those who keep smoking. Add the sense of achievement and the freedom of no longer planning around the next cigarette.

> **Did you know?** The WHO says tobacco kills more than 8 million people every year, including more than a million non-smokers exposed to second-hand smoke. Quitting protects your family too.

## Withdrawal symptoms and practical tips

According to the NHS, withdrawal can start within a few hours of stopping, the **first 3 days** are usually the hardest, and symptoms last **3 to 4 weeks** on average. Common ones include strong cravings, irritability, poor concentration, disturbed sleep and a bigger appetite.

What helps:

1. **Set a quit date** and get rid of cigarettes and lighters beforehand.
2. **Know your triggers**, such as coffee, finishing a meal or stress, and plan a replacement for each.
3. **Ride out cravings:** each one usually passes within minutes. Drink water, take a short walk or breathe slowly.
4. **Track the money you save** and put it toward something you want. It adds up quickly.
5. **Get support:** many countries have free quitlines and stop-smoking services. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist about the support that suits you.
6. Remember that a new routine takes time to stick: see [how long it takes to form a habit](/en/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-form-a-habit/).

## Quitting suddenly or gradually?

Both can work. A **Cochrane** review found similar success rates for stopping abruptly and for cutting down gradually toward a set quit date. What matters is choosing the approach you can stick with, and, if you cut down, setting a firm date to stop completely rather than just smoking less.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of quitting smoking after 1 week?

Carbon monoxide in your blood is back to normal, taste and smell improve, and withdrawal is usually at its worst in the first days before it starts to ease.

### When do your lungs start to heal after quitting?

According to the WHO, lung function improves within 2 to 12 weeks, and coughing and shortness of breath decrease within 1 to 9 months.

### Will I gain weight if I quit smoking?

Some people gain a little because appetite increases. Activity and balanced meals help, and the health gains far outweigh a few extra kilograms.

### Is it dangerous to quit smoking cold turkey?

No. Stopping suddenly is safe for most people; the first days are just uncomfortable. If you have a long-term condition, check with your doctor.

## Key takeaway

The benefits of quitting smoking begin after 20 minutes and grow every day: a stronger heart, clearer lungs, healthier skin, a calmer mood and more money in your pocket. The first few weeks are the hardest, but with a clear plan and support, quitting is possible at any age.
