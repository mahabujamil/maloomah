Do you know someone who is brilliant at school but loses their temper quickly and struggles with people? And someone else with average grades who is calm, well liked and does well at work? The difference may be **emotional intelligence**. So what is it, and can you learn it?

## What is emotional intelligence?

**Emotional intelligence (EI or EQ)** is the ability to **understand, manage and use emotions well**, both your own and other people's, in how you think, act and relate to others.

Psychologists **Peter Salovey and John Mayer** defined the concept scientifically in a **1990** paper. It became widely known after **Daniel Goleman**'s bestselling book **"Emotional Intelligence"** in **1995**.

Salovey and Mayer describe four abilities:

1. **Perceiving emotions:** noticing feelings in yourself and in other people's faces and voices
2. **Using emotions:** harnessing them to help you think and solve problems
3. **Understanding emotions:** knowing what causes them and how they change
4. **Managing emotions:** regulating your own and handling other people's wisely

## Goleman's five components of emotional intelligence

| Component | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-awareness | Knowing what you feel and why | "I'm tense because the exam is tomorrow" |
| Self-regulation | Controlling impulses instead of reacting rashly | Taking a deep breath instead of shouting |
| Motivation | Driving yourself towards goals despite setbacks | Keeping on learning after a failure |
| Empathy | Understanding others' feelings and seeing their side | Noticing a friend is sad and asking how they are |
| Social skills | Communicating well, resolving conflict, building relationships | Calming a heated argument between colleagues |

## Emotional intelligence vs IQ

- **IQ** measures abilities such as logical reasoning, maths, language and memory.
- **EQ** is about emotions, relationships and self-control.

Both matter. Many studies link emotional intelligence with better relationships, mental wellbeing and performance in some jobs, especially leadership and work with people. But **exaggerated claims** that circulate online, such as EQ being "responsible for 80% of success", **have no solid scientific basis**.

> **Did you know?** Most online "EQ tests" are just for fun. Researchers use validated tools such as the **Mayer–Salovey–Caruso Emotional Intelligence Test (MSCEIT)**, which measures emotional abilities with real tasks instead of only asking how you rate yourself.

## How to improve your emotional intelligence

The good news is that emotional intelligence is **a skill you can develop** with practice:

1. **Name your feelings.** Instead of "I'm upset", be precise: is it anger, disappointment or worry?
2. **Pause before you respond.** Take a few seconds or a deep breath in heated moments.
3. **Keep a journal.** Writing down what you felt today and why builds self-awareness.
4. **Listen to understand.** Focus on what the other person is saying, not on your reply.
5. **Ask for feedback.** Ask people you trust how you come across in difficult situations.
6. **Build small habits.** Change takes time and repetition. Read: [How long does it take to form a habit?](/en/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-form-a-habit/)

## Emotional intelligence in parenting

Children learn emotional intelligence from their parents first. Psychologist **John Gottman** recommends an approach he calls **"emotion coaching"**:

- Notice your child's emotions.
- Treat emotional moments as a chance to connect.
- Help your child name what they feel.
- Set clear limits on behaviour while you solve the problem together.

Children who are allowed to express feelings and learn to manage them cope better with life. The same idea helps with older children too: [How to deal with a stubborn teenager](/en/articles/dealing-with-stubborn-teenager/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is emotional intelligence in simple words?

The ability to understand and manage your own and other people's emotions, and to use them wisely in thinking and relationships.

### What's the difference between emotional and social intelligence?

Emotional intelligence is broader and includes understanding and controlling your inner feelings. Social intelligence focuses on reading social situations and dealing successfully with others. The two overlap a lot.

### Is emotional intelligence innate or learned?

Part of it comes from temperament and personality, but upbringing and experience shape it strongly, and it can be developed at any age.

### Can emotional intelligence be measured?

Yes, with validated scientific tools such as the MSCEIT. Quick online quizzes are usually not accurate.

## Key takeaway

**Emotional intelligence** is understanding and managing emotions in yourself and others. **Salovey and Mayer** defined it in 1990, and **Daniel Goleman** popularised it in 1995. Its five components are **self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills**, and it's a skill you can grow with daily practice.
