Count the trees in your school yard, then count the plastic bags that blow across it in a week. Those two numbers make a better opening for a **school radio about the environment** than any grand statement, because every student can check them on the way to class. There's also a timely hook: 5 December is **World Soil Day**, marked each year by the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). It's a chance to remind students that the environment means the ground that grows their food, not only air and water. Everything below is written out in full, so a teacher can hand it to students and cut sections to fit the time available.

## Sharing out the parts

A school broadcast goes well when each student gets their part a day or two early and practises reading it aloud. For a programme of roughly ten minutes, this split works:

| Segment | Students | Approx. time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 1–2 | 1 min |
| Quran | 1 | 1 min |
| Hadith | 1 | 30 sec |
| Morning speech | 1 | 2 min |
| Did you know? | 2–3 | 2 min |
| Quiz | 1 | 2 min |
| Closing | 1 | 30 sec |

With younger pupils, give the facts to three readers with two facts each. More children get a turn at the microphone, and nobody has a long passage to stumble through.

## Introduction

In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Good morning to our principal, our teachers and all my fellow students.

The ground we're standing on is the only home humans have ever had. We eat what grows from it, breathe its air and drink its water. Yet we often treat it as if it could never wear out. We throw things away, cut trees down and leave taps running, and then we're surprised when summers get hotter or a river turns grey. Today's broadcast is about that home, and about what one student can actually do to look after it. We begin, as always, with verses from the Holy Quran.

## Quran

The reader recites Surat Al-A'raf, verse 56:

﴿وَلَا تُفْسِدُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ بَعْدَ إِصْلَاحِهَا وَادْعُوهُ خَوْفًا وَطَمَعًا ۚ إِنَّ رَحْمَتَ اللَّهِ قَرِيبٌ مِّنَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ﴾

"And cause not corruption upon the earth after its reformation. And invoke Him in fear and aspiration. Indeed, the mercy of Allah is near to the doers of good." (Saheeh International)

And Surat Ar-Rum, verse 41:

﴿ظَهَرَ الْفَسَادُ فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتْ أَيْدِي النَّاسِ لِيُذِيقَهُم بَعْضَ الَّذِي عَمِلُوا لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ﴾

"Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned so He may let them taste part of [the consequence of] what they have done that perhaps they will return [to righteousness]." (Saheeh International)

## Hadith

Anas ibn Malik reported that the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, said: "There is none amongst the Muslims who plants a tree or sows seeds, and then a bird, or a person or an animal eats from it, but is regarded as a charitable gift for him." (Sahih al-Bukhari 2320; also in Sahih Muslim 1553)

The reader might add one line of their own: a tree planted today could shade another student twenty years from now, and feed a sparrow none of us will ever meet.

## Morning speech: looking after the environment

Dear classmates, when we hear the word "environment", many of us picture distant rainforests or wide oceans. That makes it sound like a job for scientists and governments. In practice the environment starts much closer: in this classroom, at the tap in the washroom, in the bag your sandwich came in.

Big environmental problems are built out of small habits repeated by millions of people. If every home in a large city like Cairo or Amman leaves one light on for no reason, the total is an enormous amount of electricity, much of it generated by burning fuel. If every student drops one juice carton in the yard, the yard is full by Thursday. The same arithmetic works the other way, though. Small good habits add up just as quickly.

Since World Soil Day is coming, I'd like to spend a moment on soil. We walk on it every day without a thought, yet the FAO says around 95% of our food is produced directly or indirectly on soil. Fertile soil also forms painfully slowly: a single centimetre of topsoil can take hundreds of years to build up. So when soil washes away after trees are cut, or gets poisoned by rubbish and chemicals, we lose something that won't come back in our lifetimes.

What can students do? We can put litter in the bin and use the recycling bins if our school has them. We can carry a refillable water bottle instead of buying a new one every day. We can switch off lights and fans when we leave a room, and look after the plants in the school garden instead of pulling their leaves off. None of this is heroic. Once it becomes habit, though, it changes how our school and our street look.

So here's my one request: don't just say you care about the environment. Change one habit, starting today. Thank you for listening.

## Did you know? Facts about the environment

> **Did you know?** About three quarters of Earth's surface is covered in water, but only a tiny share of it is fresh water we can drink. Most of the planet's water is salt water in the seas and oceans.

- Did you know that the oceans produce roughly half of Earth's oxygen, most of it from microscopic drifting plants called phytoplankton?
- Did you know that glass can be recycled over and over without losing quality?
- Did you know that recycling aluminium cans saves about 95% of the energy needed to make new cans from raw ore?
- Did you know that a plastic bottle may last hundreds of years in nature before it breaks down, according to scientists' estimates?
- Did you know that World Environment Day falls on 5 June each year, and World Soil Day on 5 December?

## Environment quiz for the assembly

The quiz reader puts these to the students in line. A small prize for the first correct answer keeps everyone listening:

1. Which gas do plants give off during photosynthesis? (Oxygen)
2. When is World Soil Day? (5 December)
3. Which layer of the atmosphere blocks most of the Sun's ultraviolet rays? (The ozone layer)
4. Name three materials that can be recycled. (Paper, plastic, glass, aluminium)
5. Why should we switch off the lights when we leave the classroom? (To save electricity and the fuel burned to make it)

## Closing

That brings our broadcast about the environment to an end. We've heard that the earth is a trust placed in our hands, that the soil under our feet feeds us, and that a planted tree keeps giving long after the person who planted it. Let's make our own school the example: a clean yard, classrooms with the lights off when empty, and a garden we look after together. Peace be upon you all.

## Questions teachers ask about this broadcast

### How do I tie it to World Soil Day?

Have the introduction mention the date, and keep the soil paragraph in the morning speech. If you have time that week, add a hands-on activity such as planting seedlings in small pots for each classroom.

### Does it work for primary pupils?

Yes. Shorten the morning speech to two paragraphs, one about litter in the yard and one about saving water and electricity. The percentages suit middle and secondary students better.

### How is it different from a radio about cleanliness?

A cleanliness broadcast focuses on personal hygiene and keeping classrooms and the yard tidy. An environment broadcast is wider, taking in water, air, soil, energy and recycling.
