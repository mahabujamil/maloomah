Empty your school bag onto the desk and look at what's there. Paper made in a factory, a pen designed so the ink flows evenly, a calculator with a tiny circuit inside, perhaps a pair of glasses. Every one of those things exists because someone learned something and then put it to use. That is a strong way into an **essay on science**, and it is how the model below begins. After the essay comes a short note on how it is built, then a version of about 100 words for younger students.

## Outline

Plan before you write. The model essay follows these points:

1. Introduction: science is a need, not a luxury.
2. Why knowledge is valued in our faith and culture.
3. Science in everyday life.
4. Science in Arab and Islamic civilisation.
5. Science and conscience: a blessing or a curse?
6. What students owe to learning.
7. Conclusion.

## Model essay on science

### Introduction

Science is the light that pushes back ignorance. People have been following it since the first time someone asked why fire burns, why rain falls and what the stars are. Today it is no longer something that belongs only to researchers in white coats. We rely on it every day to treat illness, talk to each other, grow food, build homes and travel. It has often been said that a nation's strength is measured by its knowledge before its wealth, and the modern world bears that out.

### Why knowledge is valued

Islam gives learning a very high place. The first word revealed in the Quran was a command to read, and the Quran asks plainly: "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" (Surah Az-Zumar, 39:9). It even teaches the Prophet to ask for more of one thing only: "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (Surah Taha, 20:114). Arab culture carried the same respect into the classroom. The poet Ahmed Shawqi wrote a famous line telling students to stand up for their teacher, who is "almost a messenger".

### Science in everyday life

The simplest way to see the value of science is to imagine a single day without it. No electric light, no clean water from the tap, no medicine for a fever, no phone to check on family. For centuries smallpox killed millions of people. Then came vaccination, and in 1980 the World Health Organization declared the disease eradicated. That is one example among thousands of science saving lives and making them easier. Even reading this essay on a screen depends on research that took people years to complete.

### Science in Arab and Islamic civilisation

Arab and Islamic civilisation had a golden age in which learning was central. In Baghdad the House of Wisdom flourished under the caliph al-Ma'mun. Books from other cultures were translated there, studied and built upon. [Al-Khwarizmi](/en/articles/who-is-al-khwarizmi/) laid the foundations of algebra, and the word "algorithm", used every day in computing, comes from his name. [Ibn al-Haytham](/en/articles/ibn-al-haytham/) wrote the *Book of Optics*, relying on experiment and observation, and Ibn Sina's *Canon of Medicine* was taught in European universities for centuries. These scholars were not necessarily cleverer than anyone else. What made the difference was a society that respected learning and rewarded those who pursued it.

### Science and conscience: a blessing or a curse?

Science is power, and power depends on how it is used. The chemistry that produces a medicine can also produce a poison. The technology that connects distant friends can be used to spy on people and steal their data. That is why countries pass laws to regulate research and ban experiments that harm people or damage the environment. But laws can't watch every laboratory. In the end, the real safeguard is the scientist's own conscience, the habit of asking before starting: will this help people or hurt them? Science without ethics is dangerous, and good intentions without knowledge achieve very little. The best people bring the two together.

### What students owe to learning

Students have the most to gain from all this, because school is where learning starts. We owe our teachers respect. We should study to understand rather than memorise blindly, and ask when something isn't clear, since a good question is half of knowing. We should read beyond the textbook, use the internet to research rather than to waste hours, and act on what we learn. Knowledge that never shows in behaviour is like a tree that gives no fruit.

### Conclusion

Knowledge is the most precious thing a person can own. Nobody can steal it, and it grows rather than shrinks when you share it. If we want our countries to move forward, the only road is knowledge put to work. So every school day is a step on that road, and every great scientist was once a student sitting at a desk much like ours.

> **Did you know?** The word "algorithm", used by programmers in every language on Earth, comes from the name of the Muslim mathematician Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, after it was turned into Latin in the Middle Ages.

## How the model is built

When a teacher marks an essay, three things usually count before style. Each paragraph opens with one clear idea, then explains it and gives an example. The evidence is varied: Quran verses, a line of poetry, an example from history and a modern one, the end of smallpox. And the paragraphs connect, each preparing for the next, while the conclusion returns to the opening idea in new words instead of repeating the introduction.

If your task is "science in everyday life", expand the third paragraph into two or three and trim the history. If it is "science: a boon or a curse", make the ethics paragraph the centre of the essay and give examples on both sides before reaching your verdict.

## Short essay on science (about 100 words)

> Science is one of the greatest gifts people have. It helps us tell right from wrong, and it makes nations strong. Doctors use science to treat the sick, engineers use it to build homes and bridges, and farmers use it to grow more food. Without science we would have no electricity, no telephones and no computers. Muslim scholars such as al-Khwarizmi in mathematics, Ibn Sina in medicine and Ibn al-Haytham in optics gave the world a great deal. My duty is to respect my teachers, study every day and read good books. Science is a treasure that never runs out.

## Questions students ask about this essay

### How long should it be?

That depends on your year and your teacher's instructions. Younger students usually need about five short paragraphs, like the 100-word version. Older students are expected to write something closer to the full model, with more points and varied evidence.

### Should I learn the model by heart?

It's better to understand the outline and write in your own words, borrowing ideas and examples from the model. Teachers can spot a memorised essay easily and give more credit to one that sounds like its writer. For practice on a related topic, try our [essay on reading](/en/articles/essay-about-reading/); reading is where all learning begins.

### What's a good first sentence?

Start with a picture or a question rather than a dry definition, for example: "Imagine one day with no electricity, no medicine and no books." Then show that science made all of these possible. An opening like that catches the reader from the first line.
