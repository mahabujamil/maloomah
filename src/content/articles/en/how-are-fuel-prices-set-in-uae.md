As each month draws to a close, the same question trends across the UAE: **what will fuel prices be next month?** Unlike many countries, the UAE doesn't keep petrol prices fixed; it reviews them every month. Here's how UAE fuel prices are set, who sets them, when they're announced, and what makes them go up or down.

## Since when have UAE fuel prices changed monthly?

Until mid-2015, fuel in the UAE was **subsidised** and prices stayed fixed for long periods. The government then **deregulated fuel prices** with effect from **1 August 2015**, linking them to global prices, and set up a **Fuel Price Committee** to set them each month.

The first change was striking: Special 95 petrol rose from AED 1.72 to AED 2.14 a litre, while diesel fell from AED 2.90 to AED 2.05, because diesel had been priced above its global level at the time.

## Who sets UAE fuel prices?

The **Fuel Price Committee** does. When it was created it included representatives of the Ministry of Energy and the Ministry of Finance, along with the chief executives of the major fuel distributors such as **ADNOC Distribution** and **ENOC**. New prices apply at every station in the country from the first day of each month.

## How are prices calculated?

The committee uses a market-linked formula based mainly on:

1. **The average global price of petrol and diesel** during the previous month.
2. **Transport, distribution and operating costs** borne by the fuel companies.
3. **Value-added tax** of 5%, which the UAE has applied since 2018.

So if global oil and fuel prices rise during one month, petrol prices are likely to rise the next month, and vice versa. Because the dirham is pegged to the US dollar at a fixed rate (about AED 3.67 per dollar), global price moves pass through directly, with little effect from currency swings. To learn more about how currencies affect prices, see [how exchange rates are determined](/en/articles/how-are-exchange-rates-determined/).

> **Did you know?** Petrol prices don't just track the price of crude oil. They also depend on the cost of refining crude into petrol, which is why pump prices can rise even when crude oil prices stay flat.

## UAE fuel grades and how they differ

| Grade | Octane rating | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Super 98 | 98 | Sports cars and high-performance engines whose maker recommends it |
| Special 95 | 95 | Most modern cars |
| E-Plus 91 | 91 | Cars whose manual allows 91-octane fuel |
| Diesel | — | Trucks, buses and diesel cars |

The **octane rating** measures how well fuel resists igniting too early inside the engine (known as "knocking"). It is not a measure of how "strong" or clean the fuel is. The golden rule: use the grade your **owner's manual** recommends. Putting higher-octane fuel in a car that doesn't need it brings little benefit but costs you more.

## When are next month's fuel prices announced?

New prices are usually announced in **the last days of each month**, often on the final day, through official media and the fuel companies' accounts, and stations apply them from midnight on the first of the new month. Rely on the official announcement rather than the predictions that circulate beforehand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are fuel prices the same across all emirates?
Yes. The prices announced by the committee apply at stations across the country, whichever emirate or fuel company.

### Why did petrol go up this month when oil prices were falling?
Because the calculation is based on the **previous month's** average, and refined fuel prices can move differently from crude oil.

### Do fuel prices affect other prices?
They can partly affect transport and shipping costs, which is one of the factors behind [inflation](/en/articles/what-is-inflation/).

### Can I know the price before the official announcement?
Some media publish forecasts based on global price movements, but they remain estimates. The official announcement is the only reliable reference.

## Key takeaway

UAE fuel prices have been deregulated since August 2015. The Fuel Price Committee sets them monthly, based on the previous month's average global prices plus operating, distribution costs and VAT. Prices are announced at the end of the month and apply from the first. The best thing you can do is use the fuel grade your car actually needs and drive in a way that saves fuel.
