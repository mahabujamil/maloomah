Sooner or later, almost every student meets this assignment: write an **essay about reading**. It turns up from upper primary all the way through middle school, and it's easy to see why teachers like it. Reading is the skill every other subject depends on, so a student who writes well about it usually reads well too. Below is a complete model essay with an outline, an introduction and a conclusion, then a short version for younger pupils, a handful of quotes worth borrowing, and advice on turning all of it into an essay that sounds like you.

## Outline for an essay about reading

Students who start writing straight away often repeat one idea in two paragraphs and forget another altogether. A few minutes spent on an outline prevents that. This one works for most grades:

| Section | Main idea |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Reading is food for the mind and a window on the world |
| Point 1 | The place of reading, and the first word revealed in the Quran |
| Point 2 | Benefits of reading for the individual |
| Point 3 | Benefits of reading for society |
| Point 4 | Types of reading and how its tools have changed |
| Point 5 | How to make reading a daily habit |
| Conclusion | An invitation to make books lifelong friends |

## Introduction to the essay about reading

Reading is food for the mind, just as food nourishes the body. A body without food grows weak, and so does a mind without knowledge. Reading is also a window on the world that we can open without leaving our seats: we travel to other countries, live in past centuries and meet the ideas of scientists and writers, all from our own rooms. That is why the old Arabic saying goes: "The best companion at any time is a book."

## Body: the value and benefits of reading

### The place of reading in our lives

Reading holds a special place in Arab and Islamic culture. The very first word revealed in the Quran was a command to read: «اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ», "Recite in the name of your Lord who created" (Surah Al-Alaq, 96:1), followed soon after by «الَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ», "Who taught by the pen" (96:4). Reading and the pen arrive together at the very start, a clear sign that learning begins with reading and writing, and that a nation that reads is a nation that moves forward.

### Benefits of reading for the individual

The first gift reading gives is a sharper mind. Books put different opinions in front of us, and readers learn to compare those ideas and judge them rather than accept whatever they hear. Each book also adds to a reader's vocabulary and sense of style. New words and well-made sentences start showing up in how they speak and write, which is exactly why keen readers tend to do well in essay writing.

Reading is a workout for memory and concentration as well. Following a long story means keeping track of what happened and who did it. All of this carries over into school: students who read understand their lessons faster and answer questions with more confidence. And it's a good way to spend free time, far better than hours drifting in front of a screen.

### Benefits of reading for society

The benefits don't stop with one person. A reading society is an aware society. Its people know their rights and duties, can hold a real discussion and are not easily fooled by rumours. The nations that advanced in science and industry have always respected books and built libraries. Arab and Islamic civilisation knew this long ago: it had great libraries such as the "House of Wisdom" (Bayt al-Hikma) in Baghdad, a famous centre of translation and learning.

### Types of reading and how its tools have changed

Most students already know the two main types from class. In **silent reading** we read with our eyes only, which is faster and helps understanding. In **reading aloud** we use our voice, which trains correct pronunciation and confident speaking. The tools have changed a great deal over time, from clay tablets and papyrus to printed books, and now to e-books and audiobooks on phones and computers. The format keeps changing, but the goal has always been the same: knowledge.

### How to make reading a daily habit

Habits grow from small steps repeated often, not from one big effort. Here are some any student could start tomorrow:

- Set aside twenty minutes a day for reading, even if it's just before bed.
- Start with books you enjoy: stories, simple science books or the lives of famous scientists.
- Visit the school library or a public library, and join reading competitions.
- Read with your family and swap books with friends.
- Keep a small notebook of the best lines you read; it will help with your essays.

> **Did you know?** The House of Wisdom, which flourished in Baghdad in the Abbasid era, is one of the most famous centres of learning in history. Many books on medicine, astronomy and mathematics were translated into Arabic there. For more about the Arabic language, browse our [Arabic language facts page](/en/did-you-know/arabic/).

## Conclusion of the essay about reading

In the end, reading is not a school task to finish and forget. It is a lifelong friend. Whoever reads widens their mind, improves their speech and finds their way to success. Let us make a book our companion every day, and remember that a nation that reads never falls behind.

## A short essay about reading for primary students

Younger pupils aren't usually asked for a seven-part essay. A few short paragraphs in simple language are enough, like this:

> Reading is food for the mind and one of the best hobbies. When I read a story, I feel as if I am travelling to a new place and meeting new friends. The first word revealed in the Holy Quran was "Read", which shows how important reading is.
> Reading helps me learn new words, makes my essays easier to write and strengthens my memory. That's why I read every night before bed and borrow books from the school library.
> I love reading very much, and I advise all my friends to make books their best friends.

## Quotes about reading to decorate your essay

One well-placed quote in the introduction or conclusion does more than five scattered through the essay. Pick the one that suits your point:

1. "The best companion at any time is a book." This is half of a line by the classical Arab poet **Abu al-Tayyib al-Mutanabbi**, and it's often quoted when people talk about the value of books.
2. Reading is food for the mind, as food is for the body.
3. A book is a window on the world.
4. A nation that reads is a nation that rises.
5. Read a lot and you will know a lot.

## How to write an excellent essay about reading

Keep the introduction to three or four sentences. Compare reading to food or to a window, say that it carries you to new worlds, and finish with a well-known line such as "The best companion at any time is a book." A long opening tires the reader before your ideas even arrive.

After that, give each point in the outline its own paragraph, and open each paragraph with a sentence that sums up its idea, so whoever marks it knows straight away what you're discussing. If you quote a verse or a line of poetry, copy it exactly; one slip in a quotation spoils it. Use a range of linking words, such as "in addition", "on the other hand" and "in the end", so the essay reads as connected thought rather than a pile of sentences. Then read it through once more to catch spelling and punctuation mistakes.

How long should it be? That depends on the grade and the teacher. In primary school, one to three short paragraphs are usually enough. In middle school, an essay of five or more paragraphs following the full outline is better.

Most important of all, use the model above as a guide, not a script. Put the ideas in your own words and add something of your own experience with books. Teachers value honest ideas far more than memorised text.
