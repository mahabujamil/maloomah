How long does a plastic water bottle last if someone leaves it on a beach? Ask a class and the guesses usually land somewhere between one and ten years. Then the room goes quiet when students hear that estimates run to hundreds of years. One question like that teaches more about the environment than a page of advice. So here are 40 **environment quiz questions and answers**, grouped into six themes, many with a short line explaining why. A student who understands *why* air gets polluted remembers the answer far longer than one who simply memorised it.

## Before you start the quiz

In class, split students into two teams and give each a nature name, like "Team Forest" and "Team Ocean". Award two points for a correct answer with a reason and one point for the answer alone. That small rule nudges students to think instead of guess.

For a school radio slot, five questions from one theme are plenty. Match the theme to the occasion: water questions for World Water Day, pollution questions for World Environment Day.

## Questions about the environment and ecosystems

1. What does "the environment" mean? Answer: everything around a living thing, including other living things as well as water, air, soil, light and temperature.
2. What is an ecosystem? Answer: the living things in a place, the non-living things around them, and the relationships between them. A pond is an ecosystem, so is a forest, and even a rotting log is a tiny one.
3. What are producers? Answer: plants and algae, because they make their own food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide through photosynthesis.
4. What does every food chain start with? Answer: a producer, meaning a plant or alga. There's more in [what is a food chain](/en/articles/what-is-a-food-chain/).
5. What do decomposers such as fungi and some bacteria do? Answer: they break down dead plants and animals and return their nutrients to the soil, where plants can use them again.
6. What is biodiversity? Answer: the variety of living things in a place, or on Earth as a whole: plants, animals and microorganisms.
7. Why are bees important to the environment? Answer: they carry pollen from flower to flower, helping many plants produce fruit and seeds.

## Pollution quiz questions and answers

8. What is pollution? Answer: harmful substances or energy getting into water, air or soil in amounts that damage living things.
9. Name the main types of pollution. Answer: air, water, soil, noise and light pollution.
10. What are the biggest sources of air pollution in cities? Answer: vehicle exhaust, factory smoke, and burning fuel and rubbish.
11. What causes acid rain? Answer: gases such as sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, released when fuels burn, react with water vapour in the air.
12. What unit is used to measure how loud a sound is? Answer: the decibel.
13. What is light pollution? Answer: too much artificial light at night. It hides the stars and confuses migrating birds and some insects.
14. How long does a plastic water bottle last in nature? Answer: a commonly quoted estimate is about 450 years. More accurately, it never really disappears. It breaks into tiny pieces called **microplastics**, which end up in the sea and inside fish.
15. What is smog? Answer: a mix of fog, smoke and pollutants that hangs over some busy cities and makes breathing harder.

## Water, air and the ozone layer

16. What share of Earth's water is fresh water? Answer: only about 2.5%. Almost all the rest is salt water in the seas and oceans.
17. Where is most fresh water found? Answer: frozen in ice caps and glaciers, with much of the rest underground. Rivers and lakes hold only a tiny share.
18. Which gas do plants take in from the air? Answer: carbon dioxide. They give out oxygen.
19. What does the ozone layer do? Answer: it absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet rays.
20. Which chemicals thinned the ozone layer? Answer: chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), once used in fridges and aerosol cans.
21. Which international agreement limited them? Answer: the Montreal Protocol of 1987. The full story is in [what is the ozone layer](/en/articles/ozone-layer/).
22. What is global warming? Answer: certain gases in the atmosphere trap some of Earth's heat, so the planet warms up. The most important one released by human activity is carbon dioxide.

> **Did you know?** The Arabian oryx died out in the wild in the 1970s, surviving only in small captive herds. Breeding programmes returned it to the desert until the wild population reached around a thousand, and in 2011 the IUCN moved it from Endangered to Vulnerable. It was the first species once extinct in the wild to improve by three full categories on the Red List.

## Energy and recycling questions

23. What is renewable energy? Answer: energy from sources that don't run out as we use them, such as sunlight, wind, flowing water and heat from inside the Earth.
24. How is it different from non-renewable energy? Answer: oil, coal and natural gas formed over millions of years and won't come back in a human lifetime. Burning them also releases carbon dioxide.
25. What do we call panels that turn sunlight into electricity? Answer: solar panels, made of photovoltaic cells.
26. What are the "three Rs" of waste? Answer: reduce, reuse, recycle. The order matters, because reducing is the best of the three.
27. What is compost? Answer: fertiliser made from food scraps and leaves once they've rotted down, so the goodness goes back into the soil instead of a landfill.
28. Name a material that can be recycled again and again without losing quality. Answer: aluminium, and glass too.
29. What's the simplest way for a student to save electricity? Answer: switch off lights and devices when leaving a room. More ideas are in [how to save electricity at home](/en/articles/how-to-save-electricity-at-home/).

## Forests, deserts and endangered animals

30. What is the largest rainforest in the world? Answer: the Amazon, in South America.
31. What is the largest hot desert in the world? Answer: the Sahara, in North Africa.
32. What is desertification? Answer: fertile land turning into near-desert because of drought, cutting down trees, overgrazing and poor use of soil.
33. What does "endangered" mean for an animal? Answer: its numbers are very low or falling fast, so it could disappear without protection.
34. Name a bird that humans drove to extinction. Answer: the dodo, which lived on the island of Mauritius and died out in the 17th century.
35. Why does cutting down forests harm the environment? Answer: it destroys homes for thousands of species, means less carbon dioxide is absorbed, and leaves soil open to erosion.

## Easy environment quiz for kids

These are for the youngest classes. Each one works best with a picture or a hand gesture:

36. Where does a sweet wrapper go in the playground? Answer: in the bin.
37. Should the tap stay on while you brush your teeth? Answer: no, turn it off so water isn't wasted.
38. What do trees give us? Answer: oxygen, shade, fruit, and homes for birds.
39. On what date is World Environment Day? Answer: 5 June every year.
40. And Earth Day? Answer: 22 April.

To add short nature facts to your quiz, there are plenty on the [environment "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/environment/).
