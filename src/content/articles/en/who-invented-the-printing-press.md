**Johannes Gutenberg, a goldsmith from Mainz in Germany, invented the movable-type printing press around 1440.** That's the answer most textbooks give to the question of who invented the printing press, and it's correct as far as it goes. It leaves out a surprising amount, though. Printers in China were pressing ink onto paper six centuries before Gutenberg, and a Korean book made with metal type is older than his famous Bible. So the real question isn't who printed first. It's who made printing fast and cheap enough to change the world, and that's where Gutenberg earns his place.

## Who invented the printing press: two answers for two questions

If an exam asks "Who invented the modern printing press?", write Gutenberg, around 1440. If it asks "Who invented movable type?", the more accurate answer is Bi Sheng, a Chinese craftsman who made characters out of baked clay around 1040.

The difference matters. Bi Sheng came up with the core idea: make a separate small block for each character, arrange the blocks into a page, print it, then break the page apart and reuse the blocks for the next one. Gutenberg's achievement was a complete working system that made hundreds of identical copies routine. His version is the one that spread across Europe and, eventually, the world.

## Printing before Gutenberg

The oldest method was **woodblock printing**. A craftsman carved an entire page, text and pictures, into a block of wood so that the design stood out in relief. The block was inked and paper pressed onto it. The oldest printed book that carries a date is the Diamond Sutra, a Buddhist text printed in China in 868 CE. It was found in the caves at Dunhuang and is now in the British Library.

Woodblocks had an obvious drawback: every page needed its own carved block, useless for anything else. Bi Sheng's clay type, described by the eleventh-century scholar Shen Kuo, solved that. But written Chinese uses thousands of different characters, so setting type was slow, and movable type never displaced woodblocks there the way it later took over in Europe.

Korea went a step further and cast type in metal. The book known as Jikji, printed in 1377, is the oldest surviving book printed with movable metal type, about eighty years earlier than Gutenberg's Bible. UNESCO added it to its Memory of the World register in 2001. Its only known copy is kept in the national library in Paris.

## What Gutenberg actually invented

Gutenberg was born in Mainz around 1400 and trained in metalwork, which turned out to be exactly the right background. He didn't invent a single gadget. He solved four problems at once:

1. A hand mould for casting type. It let him produce thousands of letters that were all the same height, so lines of text sat perfectly level.
2. A suitable metal. His alloy was mostly lead, with tin and antimony, so it melted easily, set quickly and stood up to repeated pressing.
3. Oil-based ink. Water-based writing ink beaded up on metal, so he developed a sticky, oil-based ink that clung to the type and transferred cleanly.
4. The press. He adapted the screw presses already used for squeezing grapes and olives, which pressed paper evenly onto the inked type.

Historians often point out that his genius lay in combining and refining these parts. None of them would have done much alone. Together they produced a machine that could print hundreds of pages a day.

> **Did you know?** Gutenberg didn't get rich from his invention. He borrowed heavily from a business partner, Johann Fust, and in 1455 Fust took him to court over the debt. Gutenberg lost, and much of his printing equipment passed to Fust. He died in 1468.

## The Gutenberg Bible

The most famous product of Gutenberg's workshop is a Latin Bible, often called the 42-line Bible because most of its pages have 42 lines per column. It was finished around 1455. Historians estimate about 180 copies were printed, some on paper and some on vellum made from animal skin. Around 49 survive today in libraries and museums, many of them incomplete.

One hundred and eighty copies sounds modest now. At the time, a single scribe could spend years copying one book of that size by hand.

## How printing changed the world

Before printing, books were so expensive that only the wealthy and large institutions owned them. As printed books grew cheaper, many more people learned to read. Within about fifty years of Gutenberg's work, presses were running in cities across Europe and millions of copies had been printed.

Science felt the effect directly. A scholar could now publish identical copies of a discovery, and readers in other countries could check it and build on it. Hand copying had always introduced small errors that multiplied with each new copy, and print largely put an end to that. Historians also credit printed arithmetic books with helping [Arabic numerals](/en/articles/origin-of-arabic-numerals/) spread among European merchants and students.

The machine itself kept improving. In 1814 The Times of London printed an edition on a steam-powered press designed by the German engineer Friedrich Koenig, which was far faster than any hand press. Rotary presses followed, and eventually computer-controlled printing. Today we also hear about 3D printing, which builds solid objects layer by layer. It shares the name but is a completely different technology.

## Printing in the Arab world

Printing reached Arab countries relatively late, even though European presses had been printing books in Arabic script long before. The French expedition brought a press to Egypt in 1798, with limited impact. Then in 1820 Muhammad Ali founded the **Bulaq Press** in Cairo. Its first books appeared shortly after, and it became one of the most important publishers of Arabic books in the nineteenth century. It still operates today as Egypt's government printing house.

## Questions students ask about the printing press

### Was the printing press invented in 1440 or 1450?

Sources give different years because Gutenberg worked on his invention for a long time, starting in Strasbourg and finishing in Mainz, and there's no single recorded date of invention. Most references say "around 1440". Some say about 1450, when his press began serious production.

### Who invented printing in the first place?

Woodblock printing appeared in China before the ninth century, and the name of its inventor isn't known. Movable type is credited to Bi Sheng.

### Why do textbooks mention only Gutenberg?

Because his system is the one that spread and directly changed the world, remaining the basis of printing for more than four centuries. It's a bit like the story of [who invented the light bulb](/en/articles/who-invented-the-light-bulb/): plenty of inventors came before Edison, but his name stuck to the practical bulb that reached ordinary homes.
