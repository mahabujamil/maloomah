Many of us learned at school that the solar system has **nine planets**, then heard that the number had dropped to eight. So **how many planets are in the solar system** today, and why did Pluto lose its title? Here's the answer, with the planets in order and what makes each one special.

## How many planets are there?

Our solar system has **8 planets**, according to the definition used by the **International Astronomical Union (IAU)**. From closest to the Sun to farthest:

1. **Mercury**
2. **Venus**
3. **Earth**
4. **Mars**
5. **Jupiter**
6. **Saturn**
7. **Uranus**
8. **Neptune**

## Why isn't Pluto a planet anymore?

**Pluto** was discovered in **1930** and counted as the ninth planet for decades. Then astronomers began finding other bodies in the outer solar system of similar size, some very close to Pluto's size, such as **Eris**.

So on **24 August 2006**, the **IAU** adopted a scientific definition of a planet. A planet must:

1. **Orbit the Sun.**
2. **Have enough mass** to pull itself into a nearly round shape.
3. **Have cleared its orbit** of other similar objects.

Pluto meets the first two conditions but **not the third**, because it shares its orbital zone with many objects in the **Kuiper Belt**. That's why it was reclassified as a **dwarf planet**.

The IAU currently recognises **five dwarf planets**: **Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris**. You may see other planet counts in old or unreliable sources, but the scientific number is **8**.

## Inner and outer planets

- **Inner (rocky) planets:** Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars. They're relatively small and have solid surfaces.
- **Outer (giant) planets:** Jupiter and Saturn are **gas giants**, and Uranus and Neptune are **ice giants**. They're huge and have no solid surface to stand on.

The **asteroid belt** between Mars and Jupiter separates the two groups.

> **Did you know?** A **day** on Venus is longer than its **year**! Venus takes about **243 Earth days** to spin once on its axis but only about **225 days** to orbit the Sun. It also spins in the opposite direction to most planets.

## Is there a Planet Nine?

Some scientists suspect there may be a very distant, undiscovered planet, nicknamed **"Planet Nine"**. Their evidence is the unusual clustering of orbits of some objects at the edge of the solar system. But **no such planet has been observed so far**, so it remains a hypothesis.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are there 7, 8 or 9 planets?

There are **8 planets**. The count was 9 until 2006, when Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet.

### What is the order of the planets from the Sun?

Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

### What is the largest planet in the solar system?

Jupiter. It's so big that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.

### Which planet is closest to Earth?

Venus comes closest to Earth's orbit. Averaged over time, though, Mercury is our closest neighbour, because it always stays near the Sun.

## Key takeaway

The solar system has **8 planets**: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. **Pluto has been a dwarf planet since 2006** because it hasn't cleared its orbit. **Jupiter** is the largest, **Mercury** the smallest and **Venus** the hottest.
