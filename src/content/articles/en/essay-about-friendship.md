Almost every student has one friend they could fill a whole page about: the person who sat next to them on the first day of school, who explained a maths problem the night before the test, or who stayed beside them when others were laughing. That is why an **essay about friendship** looks easy on paper. Then the writing starts, and it turns out that having plenty of feelings is not the same as putting them in order. What follows is a full model essay built from an outline, a shorter version for younger pupils, some lines worth quoting, and advice on turning a model into something that sounds like you.

## An outline for your essay about friendship

Plan before you write. A simple outline stops you repeating the same point, and it shows whoever is marking that your essay has a structure.

1. Introduction: people are not meant to live alone, and friendship is one of the few relationships we choose ourselves.
2. What friendship actually means.
3. The qualities of a true friend.
4. What friendship gives us and the people around us.
5. True friends versus friends of convenience.
6. How to keep a friendship healthy.
7. Conclusion: a loyal friend is a treasure, and we should be that friend to others.

## Introduction

Human beings are social by nature. We are born into families we did not choose and placed in classes with people we did not pick, yet out of all of them we choose a few to be close to, and those people become our friends. Friendship is not imposed by blood, money or duty. It rests on affection and trust, and that is exactly what makes it so valuable. The old saying "a friend in need is a friend indeed" sums it up neatly: the real test of a friendship comes when life gets hard.

## Body: what friendship means and what a true friend is like

### What friendship means

In Arabic, the word for friend, *sadeeq*, comes from the same root as *sidq*, honesty, as if a friend is simply someone who is honest with you in both affection and advice. That is a useful definition in any language. Friendship is more than spending time with someone pleasant; it is a relationship in which each person is truthful with the other, in good times and bad, face to face and behind each other's backs.

### The qualities of a true friend

A true friend keeps your secrets, even after an argument. They give honest advice, pointing out a mistake gently instead of flattering you and watching you make it. They are loyal: they check on you when you are off school, they are glad when you succeed instead of envious, and they push you towards good choices. If they see you slacking before an exam, they encourage you; if they see you drifting towards a bad habit, they say so.

### What friendship gives us

For a student, a good friend makes worries lighter simply by listening, makes revision easier when you study together, and makes school and the neighbourhood feel safer. Friendship also teaches things that are hard to learn alone, such as patience, generosity and respecting an opinion that differs from yours. And when a community is full of good friendships, there is less hostility and more cooperation.

### True friends and friends of convenience

Not everyone you walk around with is a friend. Some people stay close only while you are useful to them, lending homework or sharing what you have, and disappear the moment you need something back. A true friend shows up when things are difficult, when you are ill, have lost something or are upset. Hard times reveal friends the way fire reveals real gold.

### Keeping a friendship healthy

A friendship is like a plant that needs regular care. We look after it by apologising when we are wrong, forgiving when our friends are, keeping their confidences, and not believing every story we hear about them until we have checked. It also matters to choose friends carefully from the start, because we absorb the habits, the way of speaking and even the interests of the people we spend time with.

> **Did you know?** The Arabic word for friend, *sadeeq*, shares its three-letter root (ṣ-d-q) with the word for honesty, *sidq*. Many Arab writers have used that link to argue that real friendship is built on honest affection and honest advice.

## Conclusion

Friendship is one of life's great gifts, and a loyal friend is a treasure no amount of money can buy. But friendship runs in both directions. If we want friends who are honest, trustworthy and loyal, we have to be that kind of friend ourselves. So let us choose our friends wisely and keep them with loyalty and forgiveness, because years from now the people we remember will be the ones who stood by us when we needed them.

## A short essay about friendship (about 150 words)

> Friendship is one of the most beautiful relationships between people. A friend is someone who cares about me and whom I care about, and who stays by my side when I am happy and when I am sad. My true friend is honest with me, keeps my secrets and helps me when a lesson is hard, and I help them too.
> At school we play together at break, work together on homework, and if one of us is absent, the other asks after them. When we disagree, we say sorry and make up quickly, because our friendship matters more than any argument.
> I try to choose friends who are kind and hard-working, because friends influence each other. I hope I will always be a loyal friend, because, as the saying goes, a friend in need is a friend indeed.

This version suits pupils of around eight to eleven. Older students can use the long essay and cut each body paragraph down to two or three sentences when they need something shorter.

## Lines about friendship you can quote

Use one to open your introduction, finish a paragraph, or write in a card:

- A good friend is a mirror who shows you your faults kindly so you can fix them.
- A friend is not only someone who laughs with you, but someone who stays when you cry.
- Friendship is measured by honest moments, not by the number of years.
- One loyal friend is worth more than a hundred companions who only call when they need something.

## Making the essay yours, not ours

Teachers read dozens of near-identical essays, and the ones that stand out carry something from the writer's own life. Once you understand this model, add one real moment you shared with a friend, in two or three sentences, inside the paragraph on qualities or on what friendship gives us. Join your sentences with linking words such as "however", "because of this" and "as well as", which make writing flow. ## Questions students ask about friendship essays

### How long should an essay about friendship be?

That depends on your year group and your teacher. Younger pupils usually write somewhere between 80 and 150 words, while older students are expected to produce a longer, structured essay that may run to a page or more.

### Should I name my friend in the essay?

If the title is "My best friend", yes. If it is about friendship in general, keep it general and add a short example from your own life without making the whole essay about one person.

### What is the difference between a friend and a classmate?

A classmate shares a place with you, such as a class or a team. A friend is someone you choose and trust. Every friend in your class is a classmate, but not every classmate is a friend.
