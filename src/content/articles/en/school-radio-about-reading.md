The first word of the Quran to be revealed was "Read". That single word is a good place to start any **school radio programme about reading**, because it tells students the whole idea in one breath: reading isn't homework you finish and shelve, it's the door to every other subject. Below is a complete, ready-to-use script for a morning assembly of about ten minutes. A teacher can hand the parts to students as they are, or trim it for younger classes.

## Running order and who reads what

A school radio programme works best when the parts are handed out a day or two early, so each student can practise out loud. Here is a suggested running order:

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presenter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Opening | 1 min | Host |
| 2 | Quran recitation | 2 min | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 min | A student |
| 4 | Morning talk on reading | 2 min | A confident speaker |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 2 min | Two students, taking turns |
| 6 | Poetry and sayings about books | 1 min | Two students |
| 7 | Quiz | 2 min | Host |
| 8 | Closing | 1 min | Host |

Short on time? Drop the poetry or cut the quiz to three questions. The verses, the hadith and the morning talk carry the programme, so keep those.

## Opening for the school radio on reading

The host reads this slowly, looking up at the audience rather than only at the paper, and fills in the reciter's name on the day:

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful, and peace and blessings on the one to whom the first revealed word was "Read".
> Our respected principal, dear teachers, fellow students, good morning: may your day be an open book.
> Today we're talking about a friend who never gets bored of you, never gets angry, and shares everything it knows: the book. We'll talk about why we read, how to read well, and how to make reading a habit that stays with us.
> We begin with the best of words, verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran recitation

These verses were chosen because they are the first of the Quran to be revealed, and they mention both reading and the pen that books are written with. The Arabic text is from quran.com and the English is the Saheeh International translation. The student should recite from a printed Mushaf:

> "Recite in the name of your Lord who created. Created man from a clinging substance. Recite, and your Lord is the most Generous, Who taught by the pen, Taught man that which he knew not." [Surah Al-Alaq, 96:1-5]

> "...and say, 'My Lord, increase me in knowledge.'" [Surah Ta-Ha, part of 20:114]

The host then says: "God the Almighty has spoken the truth," and introduces the hadith reader.

## Hadith

> Abu Hurayrah reported that the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said: "...Whoever travels a path seeking knowledge, God will make easy for him a path to Paradise..." [Part of a hadith narrated by Muslim, 2699]

The reader can add one sentence so younger students follow: the "path" can be the walk to school or the library, and it can also be the pages you read and understand. Every useful book you open is a step along it.

## Morning talk: reading feeds the mind

This is the heart of the programme. The speaker should take it slowly and lift their voice on the key lines:

> Dear friends,
> Your body needs food every day to grow and stay strong. Your mind needs food too, and its food is reading. A mind that stops reading doesn't switch off, but it keeps circling the same few thoughts, a bit like eating the same meal every single day.
> Books give us what we could never live through ourselves. None of us travelled with Ibn Battuta or sat with Ibn al-Haytham in his darkened room studying light, yet books carried what they saw and understood all the way to us. Reading also sharpens our language. Students who read a lot write richer essays and understand exam questions the first time they read them.
> Some of us say, "I don't have time." But ten minutes before bed each night is enough to finish a short book in two weeks. Start with something you enjoy: a story, a book about space, the life of a scientist. What matters is starting, and then keeping going.
> So here's our motto for today: "A book in your bag, and a few pages every day."

## Did you know? Facts about reading and books

Two students take turns, one fact each, to keep the pace up:

- Did you know that the first verses of the Quran to be revealed are the opening verses of Surah Al-Alaq, which begin with the word "Read"?
- Did you know that UNESCO chose 23 April as World Book and Copyright Day in 1995, because the date marks the deaths of several great writers, including Cervantes and Shakespeare?
- Did you know that the House of Wisdom in Abbasid Baghdad was one of the world's great centres of books and translation, where many Greek and Persian works were translated into Arabic?
- Did you know that Johannes Gutenberg developed printing with movable metal type in the 15th century, which made copying books far faster and cheaper?
- Did you know that blind people read with their fingertips using Braille, a system of raised dots invented by Louis Braille in 19th-century France?

> **Did you know?** The famous Arabic line "and the best companion in any age is a book" isn't an anonymous proverb. It comes from a poem by the 10th-century poet al-Mutanabbi.

For more short facts about the Arabic language that work well in assembly, see the [Arabic language "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/arabic/).

## Poetry and sayings about books

The first student reads al-Mutanabbi's line, ideally in Arabic and then in English:

> "The dearest place in this world is the saddle of a swift horse, and the best companion in any age is a book."

The second student reads a few short sayings, which can also go on the classroom wall:

- Reading turns words into friends who won't let you down when you write.
- Ten pages a day adds up to more than three thousand pages a year.
- A book you return to the library in good shape is the next student's book.

For the next assembly, the teacher could ask every student to bring one sentence they loved from a book they've read. Then this segment belongs to the students themselves.

## Quiz

The host asks, students raise their hands, and the winner gets a small book or story if possible, which is the perfect prize for an assembly about reading:

1. What was the first word of the Quran to be revealed? Answer: "Read" (Iqra).
2. On what date is World Book Day marked? Answer: 23 April.
3. What is the name of the raised-dot writing that blind people read by touch? Answer: Braille.
4. Which poet wrote "the best companion in any age is a book"? Answer: al-Mutanabbi.
5. What was the famous library and translation centre in Abbasid Baghdad called? Answer: the House of Wisdom.

If you'd like to turn the same theme into a writing task, there's a full model in our [essay about reading](/en/articles/essay-about-reading/).

## Closing

> That brings our programme to an end. We hope each of you leaves this morning with one book in mind that you'd like to read.
> Visit the school library this week, borrow just one book, and read a little of it every night.
> Thank you to our principal and teachers for listening, and to my classmates who took part.
> Peace be upon you, and God's mercy and blessings.

### How can I shorten it for Grade 1 and 2?

Keep the opening, one verse from Surah Al-Alaq, three "Did you know?" facts and two quiz questions. Cut the morning talk down to three sentences: reading feeds the mind, a book is a friend, and read a little every day.

### Can we use this script on World Book Day?

Yes, it fits the week of 23 April perfectly. The host just adds a line at the start about the occasion, and students can bring books from home to swap in the schoolyard.
