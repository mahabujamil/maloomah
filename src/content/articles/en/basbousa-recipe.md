Basbousa is a semolina cake soaked in syrup, and it is one of the easiest Middle Eastern desserts to get right at home. In the Levant it often goes by harissa, and in Lebanon by namoura. The ingredients are simple and probably already in your kitchen. When basbousa does go wrong, coming out dry or hard, the cause is almost always one small detail, so this basbousa recipe with semolina and yogurt explains the reason behind each step as well as the step itself.

## Basbousa recipe ingredients

These amounts fit a medium tray (about 30 × 22 cm) and make around 20 pieces.

### For the cake

- 2 cups coarse semolina (about 320 g)
- 1 cup sugar
- 1 cup plain yogurt
- ½ cup melted ghee or butter (oil also works)
- ½ cup desiccated coconut (optional)
- 1 teaspoon baking powder
- ½ teaspoon vanilla
- 1 tablespoon tahini to grease the tray
- Blanched almonds or peanuts to decorate

### For the syrup

- 1½ cups sugar
- 1 cup water
- 1 teaspoon lemon juice
- 1 tablespoon rose water or orange blossom water (optional)

There are no eggs in this recipe at all. The yogurt and baking powder are all it needs. You can swap the ghee for oil, though ghee or butter gives a richer flavour, and a mix of the two works fine.

## How to make basbousa syrup

Make the syrup first. It has to be completely cold by the time the basbousa leaves the oven, and that takes a while.

1. Put the sugar and water in a pan over medium heat and stir until the sugar dissolves.
2. When it starts to boil, add the lemon juice and stop stirring. Stirring a boiling syrup can make the sugar crystallise again.
3. Let it simmer gently for 8–10 minutes until slightly thick and syrupy.
4. Turn off the heat, add the rose or orange blossom water, and leave it to cool completely.

> **Did you know?** The golden rule of Middle Eastern syrup desserts is **"hot on cold"**. If the basbousa is hot, the syrup must be cold, and vice versa. Pouring hot syrup on hot basbousa makes it too soggy.

## How to make basbousa, step by step

1. Preheat the oven to 180 °C (350 °F).
2. Grease the tray with tahini. It stops sticking and gives a golden base with a lovely flavour.
3. In a large bowl, mix the semolina, sugar, coconut and baking powder.
4. Add the melted ghee and rub it into the semolina with your fingertips until absorbed.
5. Add the yogurt and vanilla and stir with a spoon just until combined, without overmixing.
6. Let the mixture rest for 10–15 minutes.
7. Spread it in the tray and smooth the top with the back of a wet spoon.
8. Cut into squares or diamonds before baking, and press an almond into the centre of each piece.
9. Bake on the middle rack for 25–35 minutes until golden. You can switch on the top grill for the last two minutes to brown the surface.
10. As soon as it comes out of the oven, pour the cold syrup over the hot basbousa, then run a knife along the cuts.
11. Leave it for at least 30 minutes to soak up the syrup before serving.

## Why basbousa turns out dry or hard

Most failed trays come down to four things.

The first is the semolina. **Coarse semolina** gives the crumbly yet firm texture people expect from a sweet shop. Fine semolina makes something closer to an ordinary cake.

The second is mixing. Hard basbousa nearly always means it was overmixed after the yogurt went in, or that there wasn't enough fat. Stir gently, stop as soon as everything is combined, and the texture stays tender.

The third is the syrup. Dry basbousa usually had too little of it, was baked too long, or got its syrup after it had already cooled. Pour plenty on the moment the tray comes out, and if a piece still seems dry once the syrup has soaked in, spoon over a little more.

The fourth is cutting. Cut the cake after baking and the pieces tend to crumble. Cut it before, as in step 8, and they come out neat.

## Variations and storage

Once the basic version works for you, it is easy to change. For basbousa with cream, spread half the mixture in the tray, add a layer of clotted cream (ashta), then cover it with the other half. Coconut lovers can increase the coconut to a full cup. For a nutty finish, scatter crushed pistachios on top after the syrup goes on.

Keep basbousa covered at room temperature for two to three days, or in the fridge for up to about a week. If it has been chilled, warm it slightly before serving. It is rich in sugar, so a little goes a long way: enjoy it in moderation.
