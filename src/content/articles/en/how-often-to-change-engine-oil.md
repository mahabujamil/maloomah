Ask three people how often to change engine oil and you may get three answers. The figure that counts is the one your manufacturer sets, but as a rule of thumb **conventional oil is changed about every 5,000 km, synthetic about every 10,000–15,000 km, and either kind at least once a year**.

## What engine oil does, and why it wears out

Engine oil is a car's lifeblood. It reduces friction between metal parts, helps carry heat away and keeps the engine clean. Over time it wears out and protects less well, which is why it has to be replaced on schedule.

The most reliable schedule is in your **owner's manual**, because the manufacturer set it for your specific engine. Many modern cars also show a service reminder on the dashboard when an oil change is due.

## How often to change engine oil by oil type

Without the manual to hand, the type of oil in the engine is the best guide to a rough interval:

| Oil type | Typical interval | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional (mineral) | About 5,000 km | Cheapest; often used in older engines |
| Semi-synthetic (blend) | About 7,000–8,000 km | A middle ground on price and performance |
| Full synthetic | About 10,000–15,000 km | Handles high heat and protects for longer |

Synthetic oil copes better with high heat and protects for longer; conventional oil is cheaper but needs changing more often. Treat these as rules of thumb. Your manufacturer may specify something different, and when it does, its figure wins.

## Time matters as much as distance

A car that sits in the driveway most of the week still needs fresh oil. Oil ages over time even when the engine barely runs, because moisture and heat still get to it. That's why many manufacturers say to change it at least once a year, or at whatever period the manual gives, whichever comes first: the distance or the time.

## When to change your oil sooner

Carmakers class certain driving as "severe service" and recommend shorter intervals for it. These conditions wear the oil out faster than usual. They include:

- Driving in extreme heat, which is normal in many parts of the Middle East and beyond
- Dusty or desert roads
- Frequent short trips in which the engine never fully warms up, like a short school run
- Heavy stop-and-go traffic
- Towing or carrying heavy loads

> **Did you know?** The code on an oil bottle, such as 5W-30, describes its viscosity. The W stands for winter. The number before it shows how easily the oil flows when cold, and the number after it shows its thickness at normal engine operating temperature.

## How to check your oil level

Checking the oil takes a couple of minutes. Do it about once a month and before a long trip:

1. Park on level ground, switch the engine off and wait a few minutes.
2. Pull out the **dipstick**, wipe it with a clean cloth and push it fully back in.
3. Pull it out again and look at the oil mark. It should sit between the minimum and maximum marks.
4. If it's low, top up gradually with the oil grade recommended for your car.

Warning signs that call for a check include the oil warning light on the dashboard, unusual engine noises, or a smell of burning oil. If the red oil-pressure light comes on, stop safely and have the car checked.

## Questions drivers ask about oil changes

### Should I change the oil filter every time?

That's the usual advice. An old filter holds dirt, and replacing it keeps the new oil clean. Check your manual to be sure.

### How often should transmission fluid be changed?

Transmission fluid is a different product from engine oil. Its intervals are much longer and vary widely from car to car: some manufacturers set long intervals, while others treat it as fluid that doesn't need routine changes under normal use. Your manual or your dealer will have the answer for your car.

### Can I mix conventional and synthetic oil?

Modern oils are generally compatible in a pinch, but it's best to use the type and viscosity your manufacturer recommends.

### What happens if I'm late with an oil change?

The oil loses its ability to lubricate and clean, so friction, heat and deposits build up inside the engine. Over time this can wear the engine and lead to costly repairs. So the manual comes first, and in heat, dust or heavy traffic it pays to change the oil early.
