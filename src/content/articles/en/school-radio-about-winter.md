As the mornings turn cold and the first rain falls, teachers and students start looking for a **school radio about winter** that welcomes the season with something worth hearing. Here is a complete, ready-to-read script: an introduction, verses from the Quran, an authentic hadith, a morning speech, a "Did you know?" segment with reliable science facts, winter health and safety tips, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing out the roles.

## Running order for a school radio about winter

This running order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. If you have less time, drop the short sayings or the quiz.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 minute | Host |
| 2 | Quran | 1 minute | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 minute | A student |
| 4 | Morning speech | 2 minutes | A student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 minutes | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Winter tips | 1.5 minutes | Three students |
| 7 | Short sayings | 1 minute | A student |
| 8 | Quiz | 2 minutes | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 minute | Host |

## Introduction to a school radio about winter

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who gave every season its own beauty and wisdom.
> Good morning on this day of clouds and cool, fresh air.
> Dear head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning to you all.
> Today's radio is about a guest who visits us every year. It washes the earth with rain, fills wells and reservoirs, and dresses the mountains in white snow. It is **winter**: the season of warm homes, of helping one another, and of being well prepared.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by our classmate ……

## Quran segment

The student recites verses 9 to 11 of Surah Qaf. The Arabic text is taken word for word from quran.com, and the student should read it from the Mushaf with proper recitation:

> ﴿وَنَزَّلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً مُّبَارَكًا فَأَنبَتْنَا بِهِ جَنَّاتٍ وَحَبَّ الْحَصِيدِ ۝ وَالنَّخْلَ بَاسِقَاتٍ لَّهَا طَلْعٌ نَّضِيدٌ ۝ رِّزْقًا لِّلْعِبَادِ ۖ وَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ بَلْدَةً مَّيْتًا ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ الْخُرُوجُ﴾ [Surah Qaf, 50:9–11]

**Meaning (Saheeh International):** "And We have sent down blessed rain from the sky and made grow thereby gardens and grain from the harvest, and lofty palm trees having fruit arranged in layers, as provision for the servants, and We have given life thereby to a dead land. Thus is the emergence."

**Host after the recitation:** God Almighty has spoken the truth. Now for the hadith, presented by ……

## Hadith segment

> Anas ibn Malik (may God be pleased with him) said: "Rain fell upon us while we were with the Messenger of God ﷺ. He uncovered part of his garment so that the rain would fall on him. We said: 'O Messenger of God, why did you do that?' He said: 'Because it has just come from its Lord, the Exalted.'" [Narrated by Muslim, 898]

**In short:** the Prophet ﷺ was happy when it rained and let the rain touch him, because it had only just been created by God and is a mercy and a blessing. So we learn to welcome rain with joy and thanks rather than complaints.

## Morning speech about winter

> Dear friends,
> Some of us think winter is only about cold weather and staying indoors. In fact, it is a season of goodness. It brings the rain that waters the land and refills the groundwater we drink all year, and it helps grow the crops we eat.
> Winter is also a small school that teaches us to plan ahead: to wear the right clothes, to look after our health, and to leave home early, because roads are slower on rainy days.
> And it is a season of kindness. Somewhere in our school and our neighbourhood there is someone who needs a coat or a blanket. How wonderful it would be to work together and bring warmth to their homes and hearts.
> So let this winter's motto be: **"Warm hearts before warm homes."** Let us welcome the rain with thanks, the cold with good preparation, and the long nights with reading and study.

## "Did you know?" facts about winter

Two students read these facts in turn:

- Did you know that astronomical winter in the Northern Hemisphere begins at the **winter solstice** on 21 or 22 December, and that in 2026 it falls on **21 December**?
- Did you know that the winter solstice is the **shortest day and longest night** of the year in the Northern Hemisphere?
- Did you know that the seasons are caused by the **tilt of Earth's axis**, about 23.4 degrees, and not by how near or far Earth is from the Sun?
- Did you know that Earth is at its **closest point to the Sun** in early January, right in the middle of our winter?
- Did you know that **every snowflake** is built from ice crystals with six sides or six arms?
- Did you know that water is densest at about **4°C**? That is why ice floats and lakes freeze from the top down, so fish survive in the water underneath.
- Did you know that some animals, such as **hedgehogs and bats**, spend the winter in **hibernation**, slowing their heartbeat and breathing to save energy?

> **Did you know?** The winter solstice happens at a single moment for everyone on Earth, but the date depends on local time. In 2026 it falls on 21 December in the Arab world, while in East Asia and Australia it is already 22 December. At that same moment, **summer** begins in the Southern Hemisphere.

## Winter health and safety tips

Three students read these tips, two each:

| Tip | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds | It cuts the spread of cold and flu germs |
| Cover your mouth and nose with your elbow or a tissue when you cough or sneeze | It protects your classmates |
| Dress in warm layers and cover your head and hands | Layers trap warm air around your body |
| Open classroom and bedroom windows a little from time to time | Fresh air helps clear germs |
| Never sleep in a closed room with a charcoal or gas heater running | Carbon monoxide can build up, a poisonous gas with no colour or smell |
| Keep away from floodwater, streams and power lines on rainy days | For your safety and your family's |

**Note:** these are general safety tips. A student who feels ill should tell their parents so they can see a doctor.

## Closing for a school radio about winter

> To end our radio, let us remember that winter is one of God's blessings. It brings rain and goodness, and it teaches us to prepare, to cooperate and to be kind.
> Let us look after our health and cleanliness, remember those who need warmth, and make the long winter nights a time for learning and reading.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does winter start?

Astronomical winter in the Northern Hemisphere starts at the winter solstice on 21 or 22 December and ends at the spring equinox around 20 or 21 March. Meteorologists usually count winter as December, January and February.

### How long is this school radio about winter?

About 10 to 12 minutes. If time is short, keep the introduction, the Quran, the morning speech, the "Did you know?" segment and the closing.

### Is the hadith in this script authentic?

Yes. Imam Muslim recorded it in his Sahih (no. 898) from Anas ibn Malik. We left out the popular saying "winter is the believer's spring" because hadith scholars have questioned its authenticity.

### Does this script work for primary school?

Yes. The short sayings, the quiz and the safety tips suit younger pupils, and the teacher can choose just three easy facts from the "Did you know?" segment.

## Key takeaway

A **school radio about winter** is a chance to teach students that rain is a mercy, that the seasons are a piece of science caused by Earth's tilted axis, and that preparation, hygiene and kindness are the best way to meet the cold. Present it in the weeks before the winter solstice on **21 December**, and close with a small school-supervised drive to collect warm clothes for people who need them.
