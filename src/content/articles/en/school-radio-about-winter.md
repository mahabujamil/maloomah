Cold hands in pockets, puddles still in the playground from last night's rain: that's the morning a **school radio about winter** was made for. This one is complete and ready to read aloud at assembly. It has an introduction, verses from the Quran, an authentic hadith, a morning speech, a "Did you know?" segment with reliable science facts, winter health and safety tips, a quiz and a closing, along with a table that makes it easy to hand out the parts.

## Running order for a school radio about winter

The order below fills a morning assembly of 10 to 12 minutes. With less time, the short sayings or the quiz are the first to go, since the other segments carry the main message.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 minute | Host |
| 2 | Quran | 1 minute | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 minute | A student |
| 4 | Morning speech | 2 minutes | A student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 minutes | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Winter tips | 1.5 minutes | Three students |
| 7 | Short sayings | 1 minute | A student |
| 8 | Quiz | 2 minutes | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 minute | Host |

## Introduction to a school radio about winter

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who gave every season its own beauty and wisdom.
> Good morning on this day of clouds and cool, fresh air.
> Dear head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning to you all.
> Our guest today visits every year. It washes the earth with rain, fills wells and reservoirs, and dresses the mountains in white snow. It's winter: the season of warm homes, of helping one another, and of being well prepared.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by our classmate ……

## Quran segment

The verses chosen are 9 to 11 of Surah Qaf, which describe rain as blessed water that brings dead land back to life. The Arabic text is taken word for word from quran.com, and the student should read it from the Mushaf with proper recitation:

> ﴿وَنَزَّلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً مُّبَارَكًا فَأَنبَتْنَا بِهِ جَنَّاتٍ وَحَبَّ الْحَصِيدِ ۝ وَالنَّخْلَ بَاسِقَاتٍ لَّهَا طَلْعٌ نَّضِيدٌ ۝ رِّزْقًا لِّلْعِبَادِ ۖ وَأَحْيَيْنَا بِهِ بَلْدَةً مَّيْتًا ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ الْخُرُوجُ﴾ [Surah Qaf, 50:9–11]

The meaning, in the Saheeh International translation: "And We have sent down blessed rain from the sky and made grow thereby gardens and grain from the harvest, and lofty palm trees having fruit arranged in layers, as provision for the servants, and We have given life thereby to a dead land. Thus is the emergence."

After the recitation, the host says: God Almighty has spoken the truth. Now for the hadith, presented by ……

## Hadith segment

> Anas ibn Malik (may God be pleased with him) said: "Rain fell upon us while we were with the Messenger of God ﷺ. He uncovered part of his garment so that the rain would fall on him. We said: 'O Messenger of God, why did you do that?' He said: 'Because it has just come from its Lord, the Exalted.'" [Narrated by Muslim, 898]

The Prophet ﷺ was glad when it rained and let the rain touch him, explaining that it had only just come from God, a fresh mercy and blessing. Students can take a simple lesson from it: rain deserves thanks, not grumbling about mud and wet shoes.

## Morning speech about winter

> Dear friends,
> Some of us think winter is just cold weather and staying indoors. It's actually a season of goodness. It brings the rain that waters the land and refills the groundwater we drink all year, and it helps grow the crops we eat.
> Winter also teaches us to plan ahead. Wear the right clothes and you stay healthy; leave home a little early and you arrive on time, because roads are slower on rainy days.
> And it's a season of kindness. Somewhere in our school and our neighbourhood there is someone who needs a coat or a blanket. Imagine what we could do if we worked together to bring warmth into their homes and hearts.
> So let this winter's motto be: "Warm hearts before warm homes." Let's welcome the rain with thanks, the cold with good preparation, and the long nights with reading and study.

## "Did you know?" facts about winter

Two students read these facts in turn:

- Did you know that astronomical winter in the Northern Hemisphere begins at the winter solstice on 21 or 22 December, and that in 2026 it falls on 21 December?
- Did you know that the winter solstice is the shortest day and longest night of the year in the Northern Hemisphere?
- Did you know that the seasons are caused by the **tilt of Earth's axis**, about 23.4 degrees, and not by how near or far Earth is from the Sun?
- Did you know that Earth is at its closest point to the Sun in early January, right in the middle of our winter?
- Did you know that every snowflake is built from ice crystals with six sides or six arms?
- Did you know that water is densest at about 4°C? That is why ice floats and lakes freeze from the top down, so fish survive in the water underneath.
- Did you know that some animals, such as hedgehogs and bats, spend the winter in **hibernation**, slowing their heartbeat and breathing to save energy?

> **Did you know?** The winter solstice happens at a single moment for everyone on Earth, but the date depends on local time. In 2026 it falls on 21 December in the Arab world, while in East Asia and Australia it is already 22 December. At that same moment, summer begins in the Southern Hemisphere.

Someone will usually ask the obvious question: if we're closest to the Sun in January, why is it cold? Because the tilt matters far more than the distance. In winter our half of the planet leans away, so sunlight arrives at a low angle, spreads over a bigger area, and the days are shorter. For more short science facts for assembly, browse the [science "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/science/), and read a simple explanation of [how rain forms](/en/articles/how-does-rain-form/).

## Winter health and safety tips

Three students read these tips, two each. It helps if each one reads the reason too, because children follow advice they understand:

| Tip | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Wash your hands with soap and water for at least 20 seconds | It cuts the spread of cold and flu germs |
| Cover your mouth and nose with your elbow or a tissue when you cough or sneeze | It protects your classmates |
| Dress in warm layers and cover your head and hands | Layers trap warm air around your body |
| Open classroom and bedroom windows a little from time to time | Fresh air helps clear germs |
| Never sleep in a closed room with a charcoal or gas heater running | **Carbon monoxide** can build up, a poisonous gas with no colour or smell |
| Keep away from floodwater, streams and power lines on rainy days | For your safety and your family's |

These are general safety tips, not medical advice. A student who feels ill should tell their parents so they can see a doctor.

## Short sayings about winter

- Winter is a season of goodness: it waters the land and fills the wells.
- Rain is a mercy, so greet it with thanks, not complaints.
- Long winter nights are a chance for a good book and a well-learned lesson.
- The best thing about winter is sharing warmth with those who need it.
- The cold doesn't stop a hard worker; it wakes them up early.

## School radio quiz about winter

The host asks the questions and students raise their hands. Most answers came up earlier in the assembly, so it doubles as a check on who was listening:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What causes the four seasons? | The tilt of Earth's axis |
| Which is the shortest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere? | The winter solstice (21 or 22 December) |
| How many sides does a snowflake crystal have? | Six |
| What do we call the long winter sleep of some animals? | Hibernation |
| Complete the verse: "And We have sent down … rain from the sky" | Blessed |
| Which poisonous gas can leak from heaters in closed rooms? | Carbon monoxide |

## Closing for a school radio about winter

> To end our radio, let's remember that winter is one of God's blessings. It brings rain and goodness, and it teaches us to prepare, to cooperate and to be kind.
> Let's look after our health and cleanliness, and remember those who need warmth, perhaps with a small school-supervised drive to collect warm clothes. And let's make the long winter nights a time for learning and reading.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## What teachers ask before a winter assembly

### When does winter start?

Astronomical winter in the Northern Hemisphere starts at **the winter solstice on 21 or 22 December** and ends at the spring equinox around 20 or 21 March. Meteorologists usually count winter as December, January and February. So the weeks before 21 December are a natural time for this assembly, though it works all through the cold months.

### How long does it take?

About 10 to 12 minutes. If time is short, keep the introduction, the Quran, the morning speech, the "Did you know?" segment and the closing.

### Is the hadith authentic?

Yes. Imam Muslim recorded it in his Sahih (no. 898) from Anas ibn Malik. We left out the popular saying "winter is the believer's spring" because hadith scholars have questioned its authenticity.

### Does it work for primary school?

Yes. The short sayings, the quiz and the safety tips suit younger pupils, and the teacher can pick just three easy facts from the "Did you know?" list.
