Ask a class who cracked Egyptian hieroglyphs, or where the oldest working university is, and hands go up before you finish the sentence. That is what makes **history quiz questions and answers** such a good teaching tool: a list of names and dates becomes a race between teams, a lively segment in the school assembly, or a family quiz night. Below are 40 questions in five rounds: ancient civilisations, Arab and Islamic civilisation, explorers and discoveries, inventions, and a set of easy questions for younger children. Many answers carry a short extra fact, because a student who knows why something happened remembers it far longer than one who only learned the name.

## Getting the most out of these questions

The classic classroom format still works best. Split the class into two teams, ask each team a question in turn, and award a point for every correct answer. For a school assembly, five or six questions from a single round are plenty; a short, connected segment holds attention better than a long mixed list. Students revising on their own can cover the answer column with a sheet of paper, have a go, and then check.

You will see the word "about" in front of some dates. Many events in the ancient world were never recorded on a precise calendar, so historians estimate them from ruins, inscriptions and analysis of materials. The result is an approximate date rather than an exact day.

## History quiz questions about ancient civilisations

The first round starts along the Nile and in Mesopotamia, where writing and written law first appeared, then moves west to the Mediterranean and Greece.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Which is the largest of the pyramids of Giza? | The Pyramid of Khufu |
| 2 | Which is the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still standing? | The Great Pyramid of Giza |
| 3 | Which stone helped scholars decipher Egyptian hieroglyphs? | The **Rosetta Stone**, found in 1799 |
| 4 | Who deciphered the Rosetta Stone? | Frenchman Jean-François Champollion, in 1822 |
| 5 | Who discovered the tomb of Tutankhamun? | British archaeologist Howard Carter, in 1922, in the Valley of the Kings |
| 6 | Which people invented cuneiform writing? | The Sumerians of Mesopotamia, about 5,000 years ago |
| 7 | Why is Mesopotamia called "the land between the rivers"? | It lies between the Tigris and Euphrates |
| 8 | Which Babylonian king is famous for a written code of laws named after him? | Hammurabi |
| 9 | Who built the rock-carved city of Petra? | The Nabataeans |
| 10 | Which seafaring trading people spread the alphabet around the Mediterranean? | The Phoenicians |
| 11 | Who founded the city of Alexandria in Egypt? | Alexander the Great, in about 331 BC |
| 12 | Where were the ancient Olympic Games first held? | At Olympia in Greece, traditionally dated to 776 BC |

Questions 3 and 4 make a neat pair: the stone turned up in 1799, yet it took another 23 years before anyone could read it. For the full story of how the Great Pyramid went up, read [how the pyramids were built](/en/articles/how-were-the-pyramids-built/).

This round covers a capital city planned from scratch, a library where the books of other nations were translated, and scholars whose names still appear in maths and physics textbooks.

| 18 | In which city is al-Azhar Mosque? | Cairo; it was built under the Fatimids in about 970–972 |
| 19 | Who is known as the "father of algebra"? | Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi |
| 20 | Who wrote the *Muqaddimah*, one of the earliest works of sociology? | Ibn Khaldun |
| 21 | Which Moroccan traveller from Tangier travelled across the Islamic world and beyond? | Ibn Battuta |
| 22 | Which Arab scholar is seen as a pioneer of the science of optics? | Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen) |

Question 17 tends to catch people out. Some students assume the oldest university must be in Europe, and are surprised to learn it was founded by a woman in Fez. If question 19 sparks curiosity, read [who was al-Khwarizmi](/en/articles/who-is-al-khwarizmi/).

## Quiz questions about explorers and great discoveries

From wooden ships crossing oceans to a spacecraft landing on the Moon, with a canal in between that reshaped how ships moved between continents.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 23 | In which year did Christopher Columbus reach the Americas? | 1492 |
| 24 | Who was the first European to reach India by sea, sailing around the Cape of Good Hope? | Portuguese navigator Vasco da Gama, in 1498 |
| 25 | Which voyage was the first to sail all the way around the world? | Magellan's expedition (1519–1522), completed by Elcano after Magellan died on the way |
| 26 | Who was the first person to walk on the Moon? | American astronaut Neil Armstrong, on 20 July 1969 |
| 27 | What was the name of the first mission to land people on the Moon? | Apollo 11 |
| 28 | In which year did the Suez Canal open to shipping? | 1869 |

Question 25 has a small trap worth pointing out once the answer is given. The voyage carries Magellan's name, but he never finished it: he died partway through, and Elcano brought the expedition home.

## History quiz questions about inventions

Each invention in this round changed something students handle every day: the exercise book, the printed textbook, the phone, the light over the desk.

| # | Question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 29 | Where was paper first invented? | In China; Cai Lun improved it in about 105 CE |
| 30 | Who developed the movable-type printing press in Europe? | German Johannes Gutenberg, in the mid-1400s |
| 31 | Who received the patent for the telephone in 1876? | Alexander Graham Bell |
| 32 | Who made the first powered, piloted aeroplane flight in 1903? | The Wright brothers |
| 33 | Who developed a practical electric light bulb in 1879? | Thomas Edison |
| 34 | Which invention allowed blind people to read by touch? | Braille, published by Louis Braille in 1829 |

Before asking these, try putting the six inventions on a timeline. Students will notice that paper came more than thirteen centuries before Gutenberg's press, while the last four arrived within less than eighty years of each other.

## Easy history quiz questions for kids

These six are short with clear answers, which makes them ideal for younger children and a good warm-up so that everyone scores early in any quiz.

1. In which country are the famous pyramids? Answer: Egypt.
2. Which animal did Arab traders use for caravans across the desert? Answer: the camel, nicknamed the "ship of the desert".
3. What is the name of the long wall built in China to protect its northern borders? Answer: the Great Wall of China.
4. What did the ancient Egyptians write on? Answer: papyrus, made from a plant that grows along the Nile.
5. What were the kings of ancient Egypt called? Answer: Pharaohs.
6. What is the picture-writing of the ancient Egyptians called? Answer: hieroglyphs.

> **Did you know?** The Rosetta Stone is in the British Museum in London. It carries the same text in three scripts: hieroglyphic, demotic and ancient Greek. Because scholars could read Greek, they could compare the three versions and crack the hieroglyphs. For more facts, visit the [history "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/history/).

## How to run a history quiz that works

Open with easy questions so that every team gets something right, then raise the difficulty bit by bit. A student who scored early stays keen when the questions get harder. A time limit of 15 to 30 seconds per answer keeps the pace up and stops teams from quietly asking the people next to them.

After each answer, add one sentence about why the event mattered. Explaining why the Suez Canal was so important for shipping ties the number 1869 to an idea, and ideas stay in the memory longer than bare numbers. When a question is about a place, such as Petra or Tangier, ask the student to point to it on a map. A bonus point for anyone who adds a correct extra fact rewards the students who read beyond the textbook.

## Questions teachers ask about history quizzes

### Which history questions work best for a school quiz?

Questions with one clear, agreed answer, such as the names of explorers, inventors and famous cities. Be careful with very ancient dates: historians' estimates differ, and a student who quotes a date from another reference book may be right too.

### How can students remember historical dates more easily?

Link each date to a story or a picture, and draw the events on a big **timeline**. Seeing which event came first helps far more than memorising numbers one by one.

### Are these questions suitable for every age?

The easy round suits primary school, and the rest suit middle and high school. Teachers can pick the questions that match their own curriculum.
