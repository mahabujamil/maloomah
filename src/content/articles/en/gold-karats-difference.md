Walk into a gold shop and the price board rarely shows one price per gram. There's one for 24K, a lower one for 21K, a lower one again for 18K. With gold karats explained in a sentence, the reason is purity: **a karat tells you how many parts out of 24 are pure gold**. The price, the colour, the hardness and what each karat is used for all follow from that one idea.

## What does karat mean?

Picture a piece of gold divided into 24 equal parts. In 24K gold all 24 parts are gold, so it's practically pure. In 21K, 21 of those parts are gold and the other three are different metals. In 18K, gold makes up only 18 of the 24.

So why would a goldsmith mix anything into gold at all? Because pure gold is very soft. It scratches and bends so easily that a pure gold ring wouldn't last long on a hand that writes, cooks and carries bags every day. Adding metals such as copper and silver turns it into an **alloy** that is harder and better suited to jewellery, and those metals also shift the colour slightly.

One quick warning about spelling: *karat* measures gold purity, while *carat* is the unit of weight used for gemstones such as diamonds. They sound the same but measure completely different things.

## Gold karat chart

| Karat | Pure gold content | Common hallmark | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24K | about 99.9% | 999 | Bars and bullion coins |
| 22K | about 91.6% | 916 | Some gold coins, much jewellery in South Asia |
| 21K | 87.5% | 875 | Jewellery across most of the Arab world |
| 18K | 75% | 750 | Fine jewellery in Europe, gem-set pieces |
| 14K | about 58.5% | 585 | Everyday jewellery in Europe and the US |

The third column is the **hallmark**, the number stamped on the piece. It measures purity out of 1,000 instead of out of 24. A bracelet stamped 875 has 875 parts gold in every 1,000, which is 87.5%, which is simply 21K written a different way.

> **Did you know?** Truly 100% pure gold is practically impossible to produce, so bars are usually stamped 999 or 999.9, meaning impurities make up no more than one part per thousand, or less.

## 24K vs 21K gold

Everything here comes back to purity. 24K is practically pure, while 21K is 87.5% gold, and that gap explains the rest. 24K is soft and not ideal for daily wear; 21K is harder and stands up to use. 24K has a deep orange-yellow colour, and 21K is a little lighter because of the metals mixed into it.

That's why 24K mostly ends up as bars and savings, while 21K is the standard for jewellery in Arab markets. And a gram of 24K costs more for the most obvious reason of all: it contains more gold.

## 18K vs 21K gold

18K is only 75% gold, which makes it both cheaper and tougher than 21K, since a bigger share of it is harder metal. That toughness is why jewellers like it for pieces set with gemstones: it holds a stone more securely. Changing the added metals also lets 18K come in white or rose tones.

21K, on the other hand, contains more gold, has a richer yellow colour and keeps more of its value when you sell it back. Choosing between them really depends on what you want from the piece: more value, or a lower price and a finer setting.

## How to work out the price of any karat

Because price follows pure gold content, you can estimate the price of any karat with one formula:

Price per gram of a karat = price per gram of 24K × (karat ÷ 24)

Say 24K costs 100 per gram. Then 22K comes to about 100 × 22 ÷ 24 ≈ 91.7, 21K to 100 × 21 ÷ 24 = 87.5, and 18K to 100 × 18 ÷ 24 = 75. Notice that these are the same percentages as in the chart. That's no coincidence.

That figure is only the raw gold value, though. A finished piece usually carries a **making charge** for the craftsmanship, and sometimes taxes too, which is why the same weight can cost different amounts in different shops. Gold prices also move every day with world markets and [exchange rates](/en/articles/how-are-exchange-rates-determined/), so check the quoted price on the day you buy.

## Tips before buying gold

1. Check the hallmark, and ask for a receipt that states the karat and weight.
2. Compare making charges, which often vary more between shops than the gold price itself.
3. Buy from reputable, licensed dealers, and be wary of deals that look far below the market price.

For saving, many people prefer bars and coins, because their making charges are lower than jewellery's. That's general information, not investment advice.

## Questions buyers ask about gold karats

### Is 21K or 18K better?

It depends on what you want. 21K has more gold and holds more value; 18K is cheaper, stronger and better for delicate or stone-set pieces.

### Can 24K gold be used for rings and bracelets?

It can, but it's so soft that it bends and scratches easily, which is why it's mostly used for bars and coins.

### Why does the gold price differ between countries?

The world price is set in US dollars. Each country's local price then depends on its exchange rate, taxes, duties and making charges.

### Does 18K gold change colour?

Its colour is stable, though lighter than 21K, and it can be yellow, white or rose depending on the metals added.
