Hundreds of millions of people drink it every morning, and in Arab homes it's served as a symbol of hospitality. But **where does coffee come from**, and how did it conquer the world? The story starts in the mountains of Africa, passes through Yemen and ends in almost every country on Earth.

## Original home: the Ethiopian highlands

The **Arabica coffee plant (Coffea arabica)**, the best-known species, is native to the **highlands of Ethiopia** in East Africa. Coffee shrubs grew wild in the forests there long before the rest of the world knew them.

A **famous legend** tells of an Ethiopian goat herder named **Kaldi**. He noticed his goats became unusually lively after eating the berries of a certain shrub, so he tried them himself. The story only appears in writing centuries later, though, and there's no historical evidence for it.

## Yemen: where the drink was born

Historians agree that the earliest reliable evidence of coffee **as a drink** comes from **Yemen in the 15th century**. People there drank it to stay awake through long nights.

From Yemen the coffee trade reached the world through the Red Sea port of **Mocha (al-Makha)**. The port became so famous that its name, **mocha**, is still attached to coffees and drinks today.

## Coffee's journey around the world

| Stage | Place | Approximate period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wild plant | Ethiopian highlands | Before the 15th century |
| First documented drink | Yemen | 15th century |
| Spread to the Hejaz and Egypt | Mecca and Cairo | Early 16th century |
| Famous early coffeehouses | Istanbul | Mid-16th century |
| Arrival in Europe | Venice, London and others | 17th century |
| Grown outside Africa and Arabia | Java, then Latin America | Late 17th and 18th centuries |

In **Istanbul**, coffeehouses became places for conversation, literature and games. The drink's name in many languages travelled the same route. The English word **coffee** comes from the Dutch **koffie**, from the Turkish **kahve**, which in turn comes from the Arabic **qahwa**.

> **Did you know?** **Brazil** is the world's largest coffee producer today, yet coffee only arrived there in the 18th century. The country that grows the most coffee isn't its original home.

## Arabic coffee and Turkish coffee

- **Arabic coffee:** the beans are usually lightly roasted and brewed with **cardamom**, sometimes with saffron or cloves. It's served in small cups, usually without sugar, as part of traditions of hospitality. It was added to **UNESCO's list of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2015**.
- **Turkish coffee:** the beans are ground very fine and boiled with water, and sometimes sugar, in a small pot called a **cezve**. It's served unfiltered, so the foam and grounds stay in the cup. Turkish coffee culture and tradition were added to the UNESCO list **in 2013**.

## Arabica vs Robusta

Most of the world's coffee comes from two species:

- **Arabica:** the most widely produced, with a smoother, more varied flavour. It grows at higher altitudes.
- **Robusta:** hardier against heat and pests, with a stronger, more bitter taste and more caffeine.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where did coffee originate?

The coffee plant is native to the Ethiopian highlands. The drink as we know it is first reliably documented in Yemen in the 15th century.

### Why is it called mocha?

After the port of Mocha in Yemen, which was the world's most famous coffee-exporting port for centuries.

### Is Turkish coffee originally Turkish?

Its brewing style and coffeehouse culture flourished in Ottoman Istanbul, but the coffee itself reached Istanbul from Yemen.

### Which country produces the most coffee today?

Brazil, followed by countries such as Vietnam, Colombia and Ethiopia.

## Key takeaway

The coffee plant comes from **Ethiopia**, and the drink was born in **Yemen** in the 15th century and shipped to the world from the port of **Mocha**. It spread through Mecca, Cairo and Istanbul to Europe, and most languages took its name from the Arabic word **qahwa**.
