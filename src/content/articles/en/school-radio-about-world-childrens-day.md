Every year on **20 November** the world marks **World Children's Day**. It's an ideal week for a **World Children's Day school assembly**, the morning programme that Arab schools call "school radio", reminding students that every child deserves education, care, play and safety. Below is a complete, ready-to-read script: an introduction, readings from the Quran and the Hadith, a morning talk, a "Did you know?" segment, a short story, a quiz and a closing, plus a table for sharing the parts among students.

## Running order for a World Children's Day school assembly

This order fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Teachers can drop or add a segment to fit the time.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 minute | Host |
| 2 | Quran reading | 1 minute | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 minute | A student |
| 4 | Morning talk | 2 minutes | A student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" on children's rights | 2 minutes | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | Short story | 2 minutes | A confident reader |
| 7 | Quiz | 2 minutes | Host |
| 8 | Closing | 1 minute | Host |

## Introduction to the World Children's Day assembly

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
> Good morning, and welcome to a morning full of childhood and laughter.
> Dear principal, dear teachers, dear fellow students, good morning to you all.
> On the twentieth of November the world celebrates World Children's Day. It is a day that reminds adults that children are a trust in their hands, and reminds us, the children, that we have rights, and that we also have duties towards ourselves, our families and our school.
> The child who learns today is the doctor, the teacher, the engineer and the inventor of tomorrow.
> We begin our assembly with a verse from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## Quran reading

The student recites these two verses. The Arabic text is taken from quran.com; the English meaning below follows the Saheeh International translation.

> "Wealth and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life. But the enduring good deeds are better to your Lord for reward and better for [one's] hope." [Surat al-Kahf, verse 46]

> "And lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy and say, 'My Lord, have mercy upon them as they brought me up [when I was] small.'" [Surat al-Isra, verse 24]

The host can add one line: "Children are the adornment of life, and the parents who raised us deserve our kindness and our prayers."

## Hadith

> Abdullah ibn Umar reported that the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) said: "Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock. The leader of the people is a shepherd and is responsible for them, and a man is a shepherd over his household and is responsible for them…" [Agreed upon: Sahih al-Bukhari 7138 and Sahih Muslim]

The host follows with: "So everyone who looks after a child, whether a father, a mother or a teacher, is responsible for that child's care and upbringing."

## Morning talk for World Children's Day

> Dear friends,
> World Children's Day is not only a day for sweets and presents. It is a day to remember that childhood comes with rights. Every child has the right to a name and a nationality, to go to school, to get health care, to play and rest, and to live safe from harm.
> And just as we have rights, we have duties: to respect our parents and teachers, to work hard at our lessons, to keep our school clean and cared for, to treat our classmates kindly, and to help anyone who needs it.
> Remember that many children around the world cannot go to school. So let us be thankful for the gift of learning, and let our motto today be: "I learn today to build tomorrow."

## Did you know? Facts about World Children's Day and children's rights

Two students read these facts in turn. They come from the United Nations and UNICEF:

- **Did you know** that in **1954** the UN General Assembly recommended that every country set aside a day for children?
- **Did you know** that **20 November** was chosen because it is the day the UN adopted the **Declaration of the Rights of the Child** in **1959**, and the **Convention on the Rights of the Child** in **1989**?
- **Did you know** that the Convention defines a child as every person under the age of **18**?
- **Did you know** that the Convention has **54 articles**, and that it is the most widely ratified human rights treaty in the world?
- **Did you know** that the rights it lists include the right to education, to health care, to play and rest, and to express one's views?
- **Did you know** that **UNICEF**, the UN agency for children, was founded in **1946**?

> **Did you know?** Article 31 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child gives every child the right to rest, leisure and play. Play isn't a waste of time: it is a right that helps children grow up healthy.

For more short facts that work well in an assembly, browse the [Did You Know section for students](/en/did-you-know/general/).

## Short story for the assembly: Salma's school bag

> Every morning Salma woke up slowly and grumbled to her mother: "Why do I have to go to school every single day?"
> One day her teacher showed the class photos of children in faraway places who walked for hours to reach their school, then sat on the ground to learn to read and write, their eyes shining with joy at every new letter.
> Salma was quiet for a long time. Then she asked: "Do those children really love school that much?"
> The teacher smiled. "Learning is a treasure, Salma. People who know its value hold on to it."
> The next morning Salma was up before her mother called her. She packed her bag by herself and said: "Today I'm going to learn something new."
> **The lesson:** the right to an education is a great gift, and whoever looks after it builds their own future.

## Closing the World Children's Day assembly

> To close our assembly, we say to every child in the world: you matter, and you have the right to learn, to play and to live in safety.
> To our fathers, mothers and teachers we say: thank you for your care and for everything you do for us.
> And we promise to live up to that trust: to work hard, to be respectful and to help one another.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is World Children's Day?

World Children's Day falls on **20 November** every year. It marks the anniversary of the Declaration of the Rights of the Child (1959) and the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989). Some countries also hold their own national children's day on other dates.

### What is the difference between World Children's Day and Arab Children's Day?

World Children's Day is an international observance recognised by the United Nations on 20 November. Arab Children's Day is a regional occasion marked by Arab countries on a different date. The same script works for both if you change the date in the introduction.

### Is this assembly suitable for primary school pupils?

Yes. The language is simple. For the youngest classes, shorten the morning talk and keep just three facts from the "Did you know?" segment.

### How can I make the World Children's Day assembly special?

Ask students to draw small posters about children's rights and hold them up during the assembly, or have one pupil from each class read out a single right. Finish with a song about childhood.

## Key takeaway

A **World Children's Day school assembly** is a chance to teach students that 20 November marks the Declaration of the Rights of the Child (1959) and the Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989), and that every child has rights to education, health, play and safety, along with duties towards family and school. Share out the segments using the table, rehearse before the day, and your assembly will be clear and enjoyable.
