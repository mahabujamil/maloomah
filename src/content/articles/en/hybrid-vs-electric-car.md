With fuel prices rising, the most common question when buying a new car has become: **hybrid or electric?** Each type has pros and cons, and the right choice depends on how you drive. This article explains the difference simply and helps you decide.

## Modern car types in brief

### 1. Regular hybrid (HEV)

Combines a **petrol engine** with a small **electric motor** and battery. The battery **charges itself** from the engine and from braking, so there's no plug. One of the best-known pioneers is the **Toyota Prius**, launched in Japan in 1997.

### 2. Plug-in hybrid (PHEV)

Has a bigger battery that you charge from **the mains**, so it can drive on electricity alone, usually anywhere from a few dozen to around a hundred kilometres depending on the model. When the battery runs out, the petrol engine takes over like a regular hybrid.

### 3. Fully electric (BEV)

Runs **only on electricity**, with no petrol engine at all, and is charged at home or at charging stations. Most modern models cover roughly 300 to 600 km on a single charge.

## Quick comparison

| | Regular hybrid | Plug-in hybrid | Fully electric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Energy | Petrol + self-charging electric | Electric + petrol | Electric only |
| Needs charging? | No | Yes (optional) | Yes |
| Running cost | Low | Very low if charged regularly | Usually the lowest |
| Maintenance | Similar to petrol | Similar to petrol | Less: no engine oil, far fewer moving parts |
| Long trips | Excellent | Excellent | Need to plan charging stops |
| Purchase price | Medium | Higher | Varies widely by model and taxes |

## When to choose an electric car

- If you can **charge at home** or at work; this is the most important point.
- If your **daily trips** are predictable and within the car's range.
- If you want the **lowest running and maintenance costs**.
- If charging stations are available where you travel.

## When to choose a hybrid

- If you **have nowhere to charge** at home, for example in an apartment building without private parking.
- If you **often travel long distances** or to areas without chargers.
- If you want to **save fuel** without changing your habits.

> **Did you know?** Electric cars recover some energy when braking, recharging the battery and relying less on the friction brakes. That's why their brake pads often last longer.

## Charging: how long does it take?

- **Regular home charging:** from several hours to overnight, depending on battery size and charger power.
- **Fast charging:** roughly 20 to 40 minutes to reach about 80% in many modern cars.

## Important tips before buying

1. **Check battery health** on used cars; the battery is the most expensive part of an electric car.
2. **Ask about the battery warranty**; many manufacturers cover it for many years or a high mileage.
3. **Check the charging port type**, such as CCS2 or GB/T, and make sure it suits the chargers in your country.
4. **Remember the heat**: air conditioning and hot weather reduce range somewhat.
5. **Compare total cost over several years**: purchase, fuel or electricity, maintenance and insurance, not just the price tag.
6. **Make sure servicing and spare parts** for the brand are available where you live.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an electric car battery need replacing soon?

Not usually. Modern car batteries are designed to last many years, though their capacity slowly decreases with use and age.

### Is a hybrid cheaper to run than a petrol car?

Usually yes, especially in city traffic, where the electric motor does more of the work.

### Which is better for city driving and frequent long trips?

If you can charge at home and mostly drive in the city, an electric car is usually cheaper to run. If you can't charge or often drive long distances between cities, a hybrid is more convenient.

## Key takeaway

A **hybrid** is a convenient, economical choice that never needs plugging in; a **plug-in hybrid** combines both worlds; and a **fully electric** car is the cheapest to run if you have access to charging. Ask yourself: can I charge at home, and how far do I drive each day? Your answers will point to the right car for you.
