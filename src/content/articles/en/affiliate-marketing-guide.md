You tell a friend about a pair of headphones you love, and a week later they buy the same pair. The shop gained a customer because of you, and you got nothing. Now imagine you'd sent your friend a special link, and the shop paid you a small cut of the sale. That's affiliate marketing for beginners in one sentence, and it's why **affiliate marketing** has become one of the most popular ways to [make money online](/en/articles/ways-to-earn-money-online/): you need no product of your own, no warehouse and no shipping.

## What is affiliate marketing?

Affiliate marketing means **promoting another company's products with your own unique link and earning a percentage of every purchase made through it**. The company handles selling and delivery; your job is to send it buyers who trust your recommendation.

## How affiliate marketing works

There are five stages:

1. You join a store's or company's affiliate programme.
2. The programme gives you unique links to its products.
3. You share those links in your content: an article, a video or a post.
4. When someone clicks your link and buys within a set time window, the sale is credited to you.
5. You get paid once your balance reaches the programme's payout threshold.

Step four is where the magic happens. Your link carries a code that identifies you, so the store knows which buyer came from your recommendation.

## Popular affiliate programmes

| Programme | What stands out |
| --- | --- |
| Amazon Associates | Millions of products, with programmes for many regional Amazon stores |
| AliExpress | Very cheap, varied products and a huge audience |
| Temu and Shein | Low-priced products and fashion, with partner programmes |
| Regional stores | Many regions have their own big retailers with affiliate programmes |
| Digital products | Web hosting, software and courses, often with higher commissions |

Fame isn't the only thing to look at. Commission rates vary a lot between programmes, and even between product categories inside the same programme. Read each programme's terms before you sign up, and ask a simple question: does my audience actually shop here?

## Where should you share your links?

For the long run, a blog or website is hard to beat. A good article keeps pulling in visitors from Google for months or years after you publish it. Posts like "The 10 best headphones under $50", honest product reviews and side-by-side comparisons reach people who are already planning to buy, which is exactly the reader you want.

If you enjoy being on camera, YouTube works well for reviews and unboxing videos, with your links in the description. TikTok and Instagram suit short clips that show the product in use, with a link in your bio. Telegram and WhatsApp groups are handy for sharing deals and discounts, as long as you don't flood members until they leave. Coupon and deals sites are another option.

> **Did you know?** With Amazon's programme, any purchase a visitor makes within 24 hours of clicking your link usually counts for you, even if they buy something other than the product you recommended.

## How to get started

Start with a specific niche you know and enjoy, such as tech, kitchen gadgets or baby products. People rarely trust someone who reviews everything, but they listen to someone who clearly understands one area and answers their questions honestly.

Next, choose your platform (a blog, a YouTube channel or a TikTok account) and join one or more programmes that fit your audience. Then comes the part that matters most: content that helps people decide, not a stream of ads. Someone who reads a clear comparison of three washing machines and finally understands which one suits their home is the person who clicks and buys.

Be upfront that your links are affiliate links. Many programmes and laws require it, and audiences respect being told. Finally, look at your numbers. Which products sell? Which posts or videos get the clicks? Do more of what works.

## Affiliate marketing mistakes that make beginners fail

The most common mistake is spamming links everywhere with nothing useful around them. A worse one is promoting a poor product because the commission is high. You might earn once, but you lose your audience's trust.

Hiding the fact that your links earn you money is another trap. So is expecting quick money: affiliate marketing needs an audience, and audiences take time to grow. And relying on a single programme leaves you exposed if it changes its rates or rules overnight.

## Questions beginners ask about affiliate marketing

### Can I do affiliate marketing without an audience?

You can start from zero, but real income comes once you have an audience that trusts you, or steady visitors from search engines.

### Do I need a website?

Not necessarily. Many affiliates use YouTube, TikTok or Instagram. A website is a strong long-term asset, though, because it can attract search traffic for years.

### How much can I earn?

It depends on your audience size, the products, commission rates and how much people trust your recommendations. Many start small and grow gradually. Either way, a click only turns into a sale when the person behind it believes you, so helpful content and honesty remain the heart of the whole thing.
