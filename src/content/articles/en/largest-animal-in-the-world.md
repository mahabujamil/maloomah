Ask a class to name the largest animal in the world and most hands will go up for the elephant, with a few votes for dinosaurs. Both answers are wrong. The record belongs to the **blue whale**, the biggest animal alive today and, as far as many scientists can tell, the largest animal that has ever lived. Below are its numbers, its surprisingly small diet, and the record holders on land, in the air and in the sea.

## The largest animal in the world is the blue whale

First, a common mix-up: the blue whale is not a fish. It's a mammal, which means it surfaces to breathe air and feeds its calves on milk. The name is a little generous, too. Underwater it looks more blue-grey than blue.

Its measurements are worth laying out one by one:

| Fact | Approximate figure |
| --- | --- |
| Length | up to about 30 m (98 ft) |
| Weight | can exceed 150 tonnes |
| Heart | about 180 kg in a specimen measured in 2014 |
| Length at birth | about 7 m |
| Food per day | up to roughly 4 tonnes of krill |
| Lifespan | about 80 to 90 years, by estimates |

Numbers that large are hard to feel, so try this. Park three buses end to end in the school car park and you have roughly the length of one blue whale. To balance it on a giant scale, you'd need more than 25 African elephants on the other side. Even a newborn calf, at around 7 m, is longer than most family cars.

## What does the blue whale eat?

Here's the part students usually enjoy. The biggest animal on Earth doesn't hunt big prey. It lives on **krill**, small shrimp-like crustaceans only a few centimetres long.

That only works because of how it eats. A blue whale has no teeth. Hanging from its upper jaw are plates called **baleen**, which act like a kitchen sieve. The whale takes in a vast gulp of water full of krill, then pushes the water back out through the baleen. The water escapes and the krill stay trapped inside. Repeat that enough times and the daily total can reach about 4 tonnes.

> **Did you know?** Blue whale calls are among the loudest sounds made by any animal and can travel hundreds of kilometres underwater, but most are so low-pitched that human ears can't hear them.

## Is the blue whale endangered?

Yes, and its story shows how much damage people can do in a short time. Commercial whaling in the 20th century hunted blue whales so heavily that they came close to disappearing. Only in 1966 was hunting them banned internationally.

Since then their numbers have been recovering, but slowly, which is why the IUCN still lists the species as Endangered. Hunting is no longer the main problem. Whales today are hit by ships, caught up in fishing gear and disturbed by ocean noise from engines, and that noise matters a lot to an animal that relies on sound to keep in touch with others.

## The second largest animal and other record holders

Step one place down the size ranking and you're still in the ocean. Second place goes to other great whales, above all the fin whale, which can reach about 25 m. The sperm whale deserves a mention as well, since it has the largest brain of any animal.

Ask instead which animal is biggest within each group, and the answers change:

| Category | Record holder | Approximate figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Largest land animal | African elephant | adult males about 6 tonnes |
| Tallest animal | giraffe | can exceed 5.5 m |
| Largest fish | whale shark | can exceed 12 m |
| Largest reptile | saltwater crocodile | can exceed 6 m |
| Largest bird | ostrich | about 2.7 m tall |

Notice that the whale shark, despite its name, really is a fish, unlike the blue whale. That's why it holds the title of largest fish rather than largest animal. On land the African elephant is the heaviest, while the giraffe wins on height.

## Largest animal ever: were dinosaurs bigger?

The students who guessed "dinosaur" weren't completely off. Some long-necked plant-eating dinosaurs, such as Argentinosaurus, may have matched or even beaten the blue whale for length. Estimates suggest they were most likely lighter, though.

A more serious challenger turned up in 2023, when scientists described an extinct ancient whale called Perucetus and estimated it might have rivalled the blue whale's weight. The catch is that those estimates come from a partial skeleton, and researchers are still arguing about them. Until better evidence appears, the blue whale remains the largest confirmed animal in Earth's history.

Why could a whale grow so much bigger than anything on land? Because water carries its weight. A land animal needs massive legs and heavy bones to hold its body up every second of the day. A whale floats, so it needs neither.

## Questions students ask about the biggest animals

### What is the second largest animal in the world?
The fin whale, which can grow to about 25 m long.

### Is the blue whale a fish?
No. It's a mammal that breathes air with lungs, gives birth to live young and nurses them.

### Which animal lives the longest?
Among vertebrates, the Greenland shark is a leading candidate. Some studies estimate it can live more than 250 years, far beyond the blue whale's estimated 80 to 90.
