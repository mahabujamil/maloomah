**Ancient Egyptian civilization** was one of the oldest and longest-lasting civilizations in the world. It flourished along the River Nile for about three thousand years and left behind pyramids, temples, writing and knowledge that we still study today. In this simple lesson we look at the ancient Egyptian civilization and its achievements: how it began, its main periods and its biggest contributions to humanity, with a revision table and frequently asked questions to help you study.

## What was ancient Egyptian civilization?

Ancient Egyptian civilization, often called **Pharaonic civilization** in Arabic, grew up in the Nile Valley of Egypt from about **3100 BC**, when King **Narmer** (identified by many historians with the legendary Menes) united Upper and Lower Egypt into a single state. It lasted for many centuries, until Egypt became a Roman province in 30 BC.

The word "pharaoh", used for the kings of ancient Egypt, comes from an ancient Egyptian phrase meaning "great house", in other words the royal palace. Over time it became a title for the king himself.

## The Nile: the secret behind the civilization

The Greek historian Herodotus is often quoted as saying that "Egypt is the gift of the Nile". Most of Egypt is desert, but every summer the Nile flooded the land along its banks and then fell back, leaving behind a layer of rich black **silt**.

The ancient Egyptians used the Nile for:

1. **Farming:** they grew wheat, barley and flax in the fertile silt.
2. **Irrigation:** they dug canals and used the **shaduf** to lift water from the river to the fields.
3. **Transport:** the Nile was the main highway, carrying people, goods and huge blocks of stone by boat.
4. **Measuring the flood:** they built **Nilometers** to measure the water level and prepare for the farming season.

They divided the year into three seasons tied to the river: the flood, the growing season and the harvest.

## The periods of ancient Egypt

Historians divide ancient Egyptian history into major periods, the best known being three "kingdoms". The dates are approximate and vary slightly between sources:

| Period | Approximate dates | What it's known for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early Dynastic Period | c. 3100–2686 BC | Unification and the first dynasties |
| Old Kingdom | c. 2686–2181 BC | The age of the pyramid builders, including Khufu |
| Middle Kingdom | c. 2055–1650 BC | Farming, irrigation projects and literature |
| New Kingdom | c. 1550–1070 BC | Empire and great temples; kings include Ramesses II and Tutankhamun |
| Late and Ptolemaic periods | until 30 BC | Ptolemaic rule after Alexander, ending with Cleopatra VII |

Between the kingdoms came times of weakness and division that historians call "intermediate periods".

## Achievements of ancient Egyptian civilization

### 1. Hieroglyphs and papyrus

The ancient Egyptians developed **hieroglyphic writing**, a script of picture symbols that they carved on the walls of temples and tombs. They made writing sheets from the **papyrus** plant that grew along the Nile, and the English word "paper" comes from its name.

### 2. The solar calendar

The Egyptians created a **civil calendar of 365 days**: twelve months of 30 days each, plus five extra days at the end of the year. It influenced the calendar introduced by Julius Caesar, the ancestor of the calendar we use today.

### 3. Engineering and architecture

The Egyptians built the **pyramids**. The largest, the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza, originally stood about 146 metres tall and was the tallest human-made structure for thousands of years. They also built vast temples such as **Karnak** in Luxor and **Abu Simbel**, which Ramesses II had carved into a cliff. To learn more about how they did it, read our lesson [How were the pyramids built?](/en/articles/how-were-the-pyramids-built/).

### 4. Medicine

Egyptian doctors were among the most skilled in the ancient world. Important medical papyri have survived, such as the **Edwin Smith Papyrus**, which describes treating wounds and fractures, and the **Ebers Papyrus**, which contains hundreds of remedies made from herbs and natural materials.

### 5. Mathematics and astronomy

The Egyptians used arithmetic and geometry to re-measure fields after the flood and to plan buildings. The **Rhind Mathematical Papyrus** is one of the most famous ancient maths documents, with problems on fractions, areas and volumes. They also watched the stars and linked the dawn rising of Sirius with the coming of the flood.

### 6. Mummification

The Egyptians became experts at **mummification**, preserving bodies from decay because of their belief in life after death. That is why mummies of kings who lived thousands of years ago have survived.

## How did we learn the secrets of ancient Egypt?

For centuries nobody could read hieroglyphs. Then in **1799** the **Rosetta Stone** was found near the town of Rosetta (Rashid) in Egypt. It carries one text written in three scripts: hieroglyphic, demotic and Greek. By comparing them, the French scholar **Jean-François Champollion** deciphered hieroglyphs in **1822**, opening the door to reading Egypt's history in its own words.

In **1922** the British archaeologist **Howard Carter** discovered the tomb of King **Tutankhamun** in the Valley of the Kings at Luxor, almost intact with its treasures. Today almost all of Tutankhamun's treasures are displayed together for the first time at the **Grand Egyptian Museum** near the Giza pyramids, which officially opened on 1 November 2025.

> **Did you know?** In the 1960s the whole Abu Simbel temple was cut into huge blocks, moved and rebuilt on higher ground to save it from being flooded by the lake behind the Aswan High Dam. It was part of an international campaign led by UNESCO.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is it called Pharaonic civilization?

After the title "pharaoh" given to the kings of ancient Egypt. It comes from an ancient Egyptian phrase meaning "great house", the palace, which later became a title for the king.

### How long did ancient Egyptian civilization last?

About three thousand years, from the unification of Egypt around 3100 BC until it became a Roman province in 30 BC. Exact dates vary slightly between sources.

### What were the main achievements of ancient Egypt?

Hieroglyphic writing, papyrus, a 365-day solar calendar, the pyramids and great temples, medicine, mathematics, mummification and well-organised farming and irrigation along the Nile.

### What is the difference between hieroglyphic and demotic?

Hieroglyphs are picture symbols used mainly in temples and tombs. Demotic is a faster, shorthand script used for everyday business in later periods.

### Where can I see ancient Egyptian monuments today?

At many sites in Egypt, such as the Giza pyramids, the temples of Luxor, Aswan and Abu Simbel, and in museums, most famously the Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square and the Grand Egyptian Museum.

## Key takeaway

**Ancient Egyptian civilization** grew up along the Nile from about 3100 BC and lasted around three thousand years. Its achievements include hieroglyphs, papyrus, the solar calendar, the pyramids, medicine and mathematics. Thanks to the Rosetta Stone and Champollion's work, we can now read its history in its own words.
