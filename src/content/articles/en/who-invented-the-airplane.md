The first airplane flight lasted twelve seconds and covered less ground than a football pitch. So who invented the airplane? **Orville and Wilbur Wright**, two American brothers who, on **17 December 1903**, made the first successful flight in a powered, heavier-than-air machine that the pilot could actually control. They weren't the first people to dream of flying, though, and they certainly weren't the last to improve on it. Their story makes much more sense once you know who came before them and what came after.

## Who invented the first airplane?

Wilbur Wright (1867–1912) and his younger brother Orville (1871–1948) ran a bicycle shop in Dayton, Ohio. That turned out to be useful training. Bicycles taught them how a rider stays balanced, and how to build mechanical parts that are light but strong. The shop's profits also paid for every experiment they ran.

They didn't jump straight to an engine. They worked up to it in stages:

1. They studied what earlier experimenters had done and watched how birds fly.
2. They built gliders with no engine and tested them between 1900 and 1902.
3. They built a small wind tunnel to compare dozens of wing shapes.
4. They worked out a way to steer the aircraft left and right, which was their most important breakthrough.
5. They built a light engine and propellers, and put everything together in a machine they called the Flyer.

## The Wright brothers' first flight on 17 December 1903

The brothers picked their test site carefully: a sandy stretch near the village of Kitty Hawk, North Carolina, at a spot called Kill Devil Hills. The wind there was strong and steady, which helped with take-off, and the soft sand made for gentler landings.

On the morning of 17 December 1903, Orville lay on the lower wing and piloted the Flyer while Wilbur watched. It stayed up for 12 seconds and travelled about 37 metres (120 feet). The brothers then took turns, flying four times that day:

| Flight | Pilot | Time | Approx. distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | Orville | 12 seconds | 37 m (120 ft) |
| 2nd | Wilbur | 12 seconds | 53 m (175 ft) |
| 3rd | Orville | 15 seconds | 61 m (200 ft) |
| 4th | Wilbur | 59 seconds | 260 m (852 ft) |

Look at the jump in the last row. By the fourth attempt they were staying up for almost a minute, because each flight taught them something. The original Flyer now hangs in the National Air and Space Museum in Washington, D.C.

## Who tried to fly before the Wright brothers?

People had been trying to fly for a very long time. One of the earliest attempts on record was made by **Abbas ibn Firnas**, a 9th-century scholar from Córdoba in Andalusia. Historical sources say he built wings, covered himself in feathers and glided a short distance before landing hard and hurting his back. He's remembered as one of the first people to approach flight scientifically.

Six centuries later, Leonardo da Vinci filled notebooks with designs for machines with flapping, bird-like wings. None of them was ever built.

In 1783, the Montgolfier brothers in France launched the first hot-air balloon to carry people. A balloon floats because it's lighter than air, though, so it doesn't count as an airplane.

The science of the fixed-wing aircraft really began with George Cayley, a British engineer whose glider carried his coachman across a small valley in 1853. Then came Otto Lilienthal in Germany, who made about 2,000 glider flights between 1891 and 1896 in machines he built himself. He died in 1896 after a crash. The Wright brothers read about his work closely and learned a great deal from it.

## Why did the Wright brothers succeed where others failed?

To fly, an aircraft needs three things: something to lift it, something to push it forward, and a way for the pilot to control it. The Wrights were the first to get all three working together:

| Element | What the Wright brothers did |
| --- | --- |
| Lift | Designed wings they had tested in their own wind tunnel |
| Thrust | Built a light engine with the help of mechanic Charlie Taylor, plus efficient propellers |
| Control | Invented "wing warping", twisting the wing tips to steer and balance the aircraft |

Control was the part everyone else had underestimated. Earlier inventors concentrated on getting off the ground. The Wrights realised that a pilot has to keep an aircraft balanced and pointed in the right direction, much as a cyclist leans into a corner. Once again, the bicycle shop paid off.

## Who invented the helicopter and the jet airplane?

Flight moved fast after 1903, and new kinds of aircraft followed.

Plenty of inventors tried to build a **helicopter**, but the Russian-born American engineer Igor Sikorsky designed the first practical one of its kind, the VS-300, which first flew in 1939.

The **jet engine** has two inventors who never worked together. In Britain, Frank Whittle patented a jet engine in 1930. In Germany, Hans von Ohain designed a separate jet engine, and in 1939 it powered the first jet aircraft flight in history. Since each man got there independently, both are credited.

## Questions students ask about the first airplane

### Is Abbas ibn Firnas the inventor of the airplane?

He was one of the earliest pioneers of flight, about a thousand years before the Wright brothers. But his attempt was a short glide on wings with no engine. The first powered airplane a pilot could control was the Wrights' Flyer, so both have their place in the story.

### Who flew the first airplane?

Orville Wright, the younger brother. Wilbur had tried three days earlier, on 14 December 1903, but that attempt failed, so Orville took the controls on the morning of the 17th.

### What's the difference between "airplane" and "aeroplane"?

Nothing but spelling. Airplane is American English and aeroplane is British English.

If there's one lesson in the Wrights' story, it's that big inventions rarely arrive all at once. Those twelve seconds at Kitty Hawk sat on top of years of gliders, wind-tunnel tests and failed attempts.
