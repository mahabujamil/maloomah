**Who invented the airplane?** The answer in science and history books is **the American brothers Orville and Wilbur Wright**. On **17 December 1903** they made the first successful flight in a powered, heavier-than-air machine that the pilot could control. But the story of flight is much older than that: many people before them tried to make the human dream of flying like a bird come true. This lesson tells the whole story in simple language for students, with a timeline of the key moments, and answers two related questions: who invented the helicopter, and who invented the jet engine?

## Who invented the first airplane?

The **Wright brothers** built the first successful airplane. Wilbur Wright (1867–1912) and his younger brother Orville (1871–1948) ran a **bicycle shop in Dayton, Ohio**, where they made and repaired bicycles. Their bicycle experience taught them about balance and light mechanical parts, and the shop's profits paid for their experiments.

They didn't start with a powered aircraft. Instead, they:

1. **Studied** the work of earlier experimenters and watched how birds fly.
2. **Built gliders** (aircraft with no engine) and tested them between 1900 and 1902.
3. **Built a small wind tunnel** to test many different wing shapes.
4. **Invented a way to control** the aircraft and steer it left and right, their most important breakthrough.
5. **Built a light engine** and suitable propellers, then put it all together in their machine, the Flyer.

## The first flight on 17 December 1903

The brothers chose a sandy area near the village of **Kitty Hawk, North Carolina**, at a spot called Kill Devil Hills, because it had strong, steady winds and soft sand for landing.

On the morning of 17 December 1903, **Orville** lay on the lower wing and piloted the Flyer while **Wilbur** watched. The airplane rose and stayed in the air for just **12 seconds**, covering about **37 metres (120 feet)**. It was a short hop, but it changed human history.

The brothers made **four flights** that day, taking turns, and the fourth was the longest:

| Flight | Pilot | Time | Approx. distance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | Orville | 12 seconds | 37 m (120 ft) |
| 2nd | Wilbur | 12 seconds | 53 m (175 ft) |
| 3rd | Orville | 15 seconds | 61 m (200 ft) |
| 4th | Wilbur | 59 seconds | 260 m (852 ft) |

The original Flyer is now on display at the **National Air and Space Museum** in Washington, D.C.

## Who tried to fly before the Wright brothers?

The Wright brothers built on the ideas and experiments of many people before them:

- **Abbas ibn Firnas (9th century):** a scholar from Córdoba in Andalusia. Historical sources say he made wings, covered himself in feathers and glided a short distance before landing hard and hurting his back. He is remembered as one of the first people to attempt flight in a scientific way.
- **Leonardo da Vinci (15th century):** the Italian artist and scientist drew designs for flying machines with flapping, bird-like wings, but they stayed on paper.
- **The Montgolfier brothers (1783):** in France, they launched the first hot-air balloon to carry people. A balloon is lighter than air, so it isn't an airplane.
- **George Cayley (1853):** a British engineer who laid the scientific foundations of the fixed-wing airplane. He built a glider that carried his coachman across a small valley.
- **Otto Lilienthal (1891–1896):** a German engineer who made about 2,000 flights in gliders he built himself. He died in 1896 after a glider crash. His work strongly influenced the Wright brothers.

## Why did the Wright brothers succeed where others failed?

The Wright brothers were the first to bring three things together:

| Element | What the Wright brothers did |
| --- | --- |
| Lift | Designed wings they had tested in their own wind tunnel |
| Thrust | Built a light engine with the help of mechanic Charlie Taylor, plus efficient propellers |
| Control | Invented "wing warping", twisting the wing tips to steer and balance the aircraft |

**Control** was the real secret of their success. Earlier inventors focused on getting a machine into the air, but the Wrights understood that a pilot must control an aircraft's balance and direction, just as a cyclist controls a bicycle.

## Who invented the helicopter and the jet airplane?

After the Wright brothers' success, flight developed very quickly and new kinds of aircraft appeared:

- **The helicopter:** many inventors tried to build one, but the Russian-born American engineer **Igor Sikorsky** designed the first practical helicopter of its kind, the VS-300, which first flew in **1939**.
- **The jet airplane:** the British engineer **Frank Whittle** patented a jet engine in **1930**, while the German engineer **Hans von Ohain** designed another jet engine that powered the first jet aircraft flight in history in **1939**. That's why both men are credited with inventing the jet engine: each worked independently of the other.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who invented the airplane, and when?

The Wright brothers, Orville and Wilbur, made the first successful powered, controlled airplane flight on **17 December 1903** in the United States. That date is seen as the start of the age of flight.

### Is Abbas ibn Firnas the inventor of the airplane?

Abbas ibn Firnas was one of history's earliest **pioneers of flight**, about a thousand years before the Wright brothers. But his attempt was a short glide on wings with no engine. The first powered airplane that a pilot could control was the Wright brothers' Flyer.

### Who flew the first airplane?

**Orville Wright**, the younger brother. Wilbur had tried three days earlier, on 14 December 1903, but that attempt failed, so it was Orville's turn on the morning of 17 December.

### What's the difference between "airplane" and "aeroplane"?

They mean the same thing. **Airplane** is the American English spelling and **aeroplane** is the British English one.

## Key takeaway

The **inventors of the airplane** were **Orville and Wilbur Wright**, who made the first powered flight, lasting 12 seconds, on 17 December 1903. Many pioneers came before them, including the Andalusian scholar Abbas ibn Firnas, and flight kept developing until the helicopter and the jet airplane appeared in 1939. The biggest lesson from their story: experiments, patience and learning from mistakes are the path to every great invention.
