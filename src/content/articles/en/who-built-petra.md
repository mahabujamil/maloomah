You walk for more than a kilometre through a narrow gorge, its walls towering over you, and then the rock opens up and there it is: a huge pink facade that looks as though it grew out of the mountain. That's the Treasury, the best-known monument in **Petra**, Jordan's ancient Rose City and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Almost everyone who sees it asks the same thing: who built Petra, and when? And how do you carve a city out of solid rock?

## Who built Petra? The Nabataeans

**Petra was built by the Nabataeans, an ancient Arab people of the southern Levant and north-west Arabia, who made it the capital of their kingdom.** They called it Raqmu. "Petra" is the later Greek name, and it simply means "rock".

The Nabataeans were traders before anything else. They controlled the caravan routes that carried incense, spices and other valuable goods between southern Arabia, Egypt, the Levant and the Mediterranean ports, and those caravans had to pass through their territory. The wealth that came from this trade is what paid for their capital.

## When was Petra built?

Petra wasn't built in a single year or by a single king. The Nabataeans settled in the area from around the 4th century BC, and the city grew over generations. It reached its peak between the 1st century BC and the 1st century AD, which is when its most famous facades were carved.

## Who built the Treasury in Petra?

Many researchers believe the **Treasury (Al-Khazneh)** was carved in the 1st century AD, possibly during the reign of the Nabataean king Aretas IV. Its facade is about 40 metres tall, and the remarkable part is that nobody stacked stones to make it. It was carved straight into the cliff, from the top down. The carvers started at the summit and worked their way towards the ground, and there was no room for mistakes, because you can't put rock back once it's cut away.

The name is misleading. Researchers think the building was most likely a royal tomb, not a place to keep money. "Treasury" comes from a folk tale that treasure was hidden in the stone urn at the top of the facade, and you can still see bullet marks on that urn, left by people who once tried to shoot it open and make the gold pour out.

> **Did you know?** The Nabataeans built an **advanced water system** in the middle of the desert. Dams, channels cut into the rock, basins and cisterns collected and stored rainwater and controlled flash floods. That's what allowed a large city to flourish in such a dry region.

## Petra's main sights

Every visit starts with the Siq, the narrow, twisting canyon about 1.2 km long that served as the city's main entrance. The Treasury appears at its end, and that first glimpse is the moment everyone waits for.

Petra is much bigger than one facade, though. Further on is the Theatre, carved into the rock with seats for thousands of spectators, and the Royal Tombs, a row of large carved facades along the mountainside. If you have the legs for it, the Monastery (Ad Deir) waits high above: an enormous facade you can only reach by climbing hundreds of steps.

## How the world rediscovered Petra

When trade routes shifted, and after the great earthquake of AD 363, Petra slowly lost its importance. It's sometimes called a "lost city", but that isn't quite right. Local Bedouin knew it all along.

What changed in 1812 was that the Swiss explorer Johann Ludwig Burckhardt, travelling in disguise as "Sheikh Ibrahim", reached Petra and wrote about it, bringing it back to the attention of the Western world. Today it's Jordan's most important tourist site, with hundreds of thousands of visitors a year.

A trip to Jordan can easily take in [the Dead Sea](/en/articles/dead-sea/) as well. And if ancient engineering is your thing, compare Petra with another long-running puzzle: [How were the pyramids built?](/en/articles/how-were-the-pyramids-built/)

## Questions students ask about Petra

### Which people built Petra?

The Nabataeans, an ancient Arab people known for trade, who made Petra the capital of their kingdom.

### Why is Petra called the Rose City?

Because of its sandstone cliffs, whose colours range from pink to red and orange and shift as the sunlight changes through the day.

### Where is Petra?

In Ma'an Governorate in southern Jordan, next to the town of Wadi Musa. It's roughly a three-hour drive from the capital, Amman.

### Is Petra one of the Seven Wonders of the World?

Yes. It was chosen as one of the New Seven Wonders of the World in a global vote in 2007.
