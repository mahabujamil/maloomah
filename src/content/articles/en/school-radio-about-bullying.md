Most bullied students never say a word about it. They just start avoiding the corridor at break, or eating lunch alone, or finding reasons to stay home. That silence is exactly why the topic belongs in front of the whole school. Below is a complete **school radio about bullying** you can read as it is: an introduction, a verse from the Quran, an authentic hadith, a morning speech, a "Did you know?" segment with reliable facts, practical tips, a short quiz and a closing. It starts with a table so you can hand out the parts quickly.

## Running order for a school radio about bullying

The whole thing fits a morning assembly of about 10 to 12 minutes. Short on time? Drop the quotes or the quiz; the core message survives without them.

| Order | Segment | Approx. time | Presented by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Introduction | 1 minute | Host |
| 2 | Quran | 1 minute | A student who recites well |
| 3 | Hadith | 1 minute | A student |
| 4 | Morning speech | 2 minutes | A student |
| 5 | "Did you know?" | 1.5 minutes | Two students taking turns |
| 6 | What to do about bullying | 1.5 minutes | Three students |
| 7 | Quotes about bullying | 1 minute | A student |
| 8 | Quiz | 2 minutes | Host |
| 9 | Closing | 1 minute | Host |

## Introduction for a school radio about bullying

The host should read this slowly and pause after the third line, so the examples have a moment to land:

> In the name of God, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. Praise be to God, who honoured human beings and commanded us to good character.
> Our respected head teacher, dear teachers and fellow students, good morning, with respect and friendship.
> A hurtful word, a joke about a classmate's looks, a shove in the corridor, a nasty message on a phone… some of us call it a joke, but to someone else it is a wound they don't forget.
> Today our radio is about bullying: what it is, why we must reject it, and how we protect ourselves and our classmates from it.
> We begin with verses from the Holy Quran, recited by ……

## The Quran segment

Verse 11 of Surah Al-Hujurat is a natural choice, because it names the forms of verbal bullying almost one by one. The Arabic text is taken exactly from quran.com, and the student should read it from the Mushaf with proper recitation. The English meaning below is the Saheeh International translation:

> "O you who have believed, let not a people ridicule [another] people; perhaps they may be better than them; nor let women ridicule [other] women; perhaps they may be better than them. And do not insult one another and do not call each other by [offensive] nicknames. Wretched is the name [i.e., mention] of disobedience after [one's] faith. And whoever does not repent - then it is those who are the wrongdoers." [Surah Al-Hujurat 49:11]

After the recitation, the host links it to the theme: the verse forbids mocking others, insulting them and calling them names they dislike, and every one of these is a form of bullying. Now for the hadith, presented by ……

## The hadith segment

> Abdullah ibn Amr reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "A Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe, and the emigrant is the one who abandons what God has forbidden." [Sahih al-Bukhari 10; Muslim reports something similar]

The student can explain it in two sentences. A true Muslim doesn't harm people with words or hands. And these days the "tongue" includes everything we type and post, so a mocking comment in the class group chat is harm done with the tongue, even if nobody said it out loud.

## Morning speech about bullying

> Dear classmates,
> Bullying is when a student deliberately hurts a classmate, again and again, using the fact that they are physically stronger, more popular or louder. It takes many forms:
> physical bullying such as hitting, pushing and taking belongings; verbal bullying such as mocking, insults and cruel nicknames; social bullying such as leaving someone out and spreading rumours about them; and cyberbullying through messages, photos and comments online.
> A bully may think they are joking, but in a real joke everyone laughs. In bullying, one person laughs and another person hurts.
> And to anyone who just stands and watches: your silence encourages the bully. Standing next to your classmate, or telling a teacher, is enough to make you part of the solution.
> Let our motto be: **"Our school is safe for every student, and respect has no exceptions."**

The speech names four types: **physical, verbal, social and cyberbullying**. They come back in the quiz, so students who were listening here will have the answer ready.

## "Did you know?" facts about bullying

Two students read these facts, one each in turn:

- Did you know that UNESCO reports suggest about one in three students worldwide has been bullied at least once in a month?
- Did you know that UNESCO's member states made the first Thursday of November the **International Day against Violence and Bullying at School, including Cyberbullying**, first marked in 2020?
- Did you know that UNESCO estimates about one in ten students experiences cyberbullying?
- Did you know that mocking someone's looks, skin colour, accent or name is among the most common forms of bullying between students?
- Did you know that many bullied students tell no one, out of fear or embarrassment, so they need a friend who notices?
- Did you know that bullying can affect a student's concentration, sleep and wish to go to school, so it is not "just a joke"?

> **Did you know?** One classmate standing beside a bullied student can be enough to stop the bully, because bullies usually want an audience that laughs along. Without one, they lose their weapon.

If you need more short facts for other assemblies, there are plenty on our [science "Did you know?" page](/en/did-you-know/science/).

## What to do if you are bullied or see bullying

This is the practical heart of the assembly: students should walk away knowing exactly what to do. Three students each take one situation.

The first student speaks to anyone who is being bullied:
1. Stay as calm as you can and walk away. Don't hit back or insult back.
2. Tell an adult you trust: a parent, a teacher or the school counsellor.
3. Never blame yourself. The fault is the bully's, not yours.

The second student speaks to anyone who sees it happen:
1. Don't laugh, don't join in, and don't film or share it.
2. Stand next to your classmate and invite them to join you and your friends.
3. Tell a teacher or the school office what you saw. That isn't "telling tales"; it is protecting your classmate.

The third student covers life online:
1. Don't reply to abusive messages, and keep a screenshot as evidence.
2. Block the person and report them in the app.
3. Think before you type: would you accept someone saying this to you?

## Short quotes about bullying for school radio

One student can read these, or you can put them on the assembly noticeboard for the rest of the week:

- Kind words build; hurtful words destroy.
- The truly strong protect the weak; they don't hurt them.
- Our differences in looks, colour and accent make us richer, not lesser.
- Don't be a bully, and don't be a silent bystander.
- Your smile at a lonely classmate could change their whole day.

Every answer came up earlier in the assembly, so the quiz also shows who was paying attention:

## Closing for a school radio about bullying

> To close our radio, let us remember that our school is our second home, and every student in it deserves to feel safe and respected.
> Let us promise today not to mock anyone, not to stay silent about harm we see, and to be a friend to anyone who needs one.
> Thank you for listening, and peace be upon you.

## Questions teachers ask about this assembly

### How do I define school bullying in one sentence?

School bullying is **repeated aggressive behaviour** in which a student or group deliberately hurts a classmate who is weaker than them, physically, with words, by leaving them out or online. "Repeated" and "weaker" are the words that separate it from an ordinary argument between friends.

### How long does it take, and how can I shorten it?

The full script runs about 10 to 12 minutes. You can bring it down to about 6 minutes by keeping only the introduction, the Quran, the hadith, the morning speech and the closing.

### Does it work in primary school?

Yes, with a small change. Pick just three of the "Did you know?" facts, and explain the types of bullying with examples from a young child's day: grabbing someone's bag, refusing to let a classmate play at break, or a silly nickname that gets repeated every morning.

### Which hadith suits an assembly about bullying?

"A Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe," reported by al-Bukhari (no. 10) and similarly by Muslim. It is authentic, and its meaning is clear to students of any age.

### Is there a world day against bullying?

Yes. The International Day against Violence and Bullying at School, including Cyberbullying, falls on the first Thursday of November every year, and running this assembly that week gives it an occasion students will notice.
